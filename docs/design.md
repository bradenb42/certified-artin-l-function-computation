# How the computation works

The input is a monic separable polynomial f in Z[x] together with generators
of its Galois group G, given as permutations of the roots in a fixed
numbering. The output is, for every irreducible character chi of G, the
conductor, the Euler factors, the Gamma factor and the root number of
L(s, chi), plus a certificate.

The stages run in the order below. Each writes its own file into the run
directory, and the driver records how long it took.

## Group and character data

The character table is computed by the Dixon-Schneider method modulo a prime
p = 1 mod exp(G), p > 2 sqrt|G|, and lifted to exact values in Q(zeta_e)
through eigenvalue multiplicities. Alongside the table the run records a certificate for it: the central
characters as exact elements of Z[zeta_e], and the classes whose class matrices
were used. A checker confirms the table by verifying eigenvector identities,
which is cheaper than recomputing it.

Schur indices are bounded from the Frobenius-Schur indicator, the divisibility
bounds and every available pair of a subgroup and a linear character; when the
bounds meet, the index is exact. Matrix models of a multiple of each
character are built by projecting an induced representation and clearing
denominators. Nothing downstream depends on either; the models give a second route to the
Euler factors.

## Ramified primes

A prime ramifies exactly when it divides the discriminant of the ring of
integers of one of the fields generated by an irreducible factor of f. Each
candidate prime is decided by an odd discriminant valuation, by the Dedekind
criterion, or by constructing the maximal order at that prime; the last also
yields the ramification indices and residue degrees. Every decision is
recorded with a witness that can be rechecked independently.

## Precision

One policy object decides all working precision. Stages register the pairs
(invariant, index) they will use, which can only raise precisions, and ask the
policy for the precision at each prime; working below it is an error. Every
consultation is logged, so a checker can confirm that each stage worked at or
above the precision its own data require. A conservative mode uses a bound that
covers every test in advance; a sharp mode applies the bound test by test and
needs roughly a factor of the index fewer digits.

## Frobenius classes at the unramified primes

Three routes, in increasing order of cost:

- the cycle type of f mod ell locates the block of classes, and the factorisation types of a family of subgroup resolvents pin down the rational class. The family is chosen by weighted greedy set cover over the pairs of rational classes that share a block;
- inside a rational class, twisted resolvents with coefficients in Z[zeta_o] separate the individual classes: counting roots of the reduction that are given powers of a root of unity yields an induced character value;
- the direct route computes the roots in a residue field, reads the Frobenius permutation, aligns the numbering and conjugates. This always determines the class, and is used whenever the estimated cost of the twisted resolvents exceeds a budget.

## Local fields at the ramified primes

At a prime whose ramification indices are prime to it, the splitting field of
f over the p-adic numbers embeds in an explicit Galois extension
K = Q_{ell^F}(varpi) with varpi^E = ell. The roots of f in K are found by
Panayi's method, and the decomposition and inertia groups and the Frobenius
coset are read off the action of the two generating automorphisms.

The permutations identifying the local roots with the global ones form a coset
of G; one is found by a structural filter followed by an invariant
congruence. A second, independently chosen matching is computed and the two
transported triples are checked to be conjugate in G, which is what makes the recorded groups well defined.

## Filtrations and conductors

From a uniformiser of the local field the module computes the lower
ramification filtration, the Herbrand function, the upper breaks and the
ramification polygons. It checks normality of the chain, that the first
ramification group is the wild inertia subgroup, that the higher quotients are
elementary abelian, and that the upper breaks on abelian subquotients are
integers; it enumerates all filtrations consistent with the factor polygons
and builds a resolvent that discriminates between them. The conductor
exponents follow, and two identities are enforced: the weighted sum of the
exponents equals the discriminant valuation of the Galois closure, and the
exponent of the permutation character of each factor's root set equals the
discriminant valuation of that factor's ring of integers.

## Euler factors and their identities

The Euler factor at a ramified prime is computed twice, once from isotypic
multiplicities over the Frobenius coset and once from power traces with
Newton's identities, and a third time from a matrix model when one is
available. The degree must equal the dimension of the inertia invariants and
must agree with the conductor and Swan exponents; the product over the
characters weighted by their degrees must equal the local factor of the
Dedekind zeta function of the Galois closure; and for a family of subgroups
the product over the characters appearing in the permutation character must
equal the local factor of the subfield's zeta function, computed directly from
the factorisation of that subgroup's resolvent.

## The archimedean place and the root numbers

The class of complex conjugation comes from a certified count of the real
roots and, when needed, from the real root counts of the separating
resolvents. That gives the Gamma factor exponents, the parity and the
archimedean root number. Local root numbers at tamely ramified primes are
determinants times Gauss sums, normalised through Lubin-Tate theory. For
self-dual characters the local factors lie in a finite set, which makes the
global root number exact; the predictions for orthogonal and symplectic
characters are checked and a mismatch stops the run.

## The functional equation

Dirichlet coefficients are assembled from the Euler factors and paired with a test function obtained by numerically inverting the Mellin transform of the Gamma factor, with rigorous decay bounds. The truncated sums are compared at a fixed pair of arguments against an explicit tail bound. The same rule is then applied to deliberately perturbed data: a wrong root number, a wrong conductor, a wrong Euler factor, a wrong Frobenius class. The verdicts show which errors the test detects at a given truncation point. A separate anchor computes the residue of the Dedekind zeta function of the field generated by a root, from f alone, and compares it with the product of L-values from the character data.
