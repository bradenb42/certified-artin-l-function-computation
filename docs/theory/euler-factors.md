# The Euler factor at a ramified prime

**Setting.** $\ell$ ramified; $D=D\_\ell\le G$, $I=I\_\ell=(D\_\ell)*0$, $f=[D\:I]$, in the global numbering (*[*matching*](https://claude.ai/chat/matching.md) *to* [*the filtration*](https://claude.ai/chat/ramification-filtration.md)*); $\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)$ with $K=\mathbb{Q}(\chi)$; a model $\rho\:G\to\mathrm{GL}*{d}(\mathcal{O}*F)$ of trace $m\chi$ from* [*the character table*](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md) *§4 ($F\supseteq K$ cyclotomic, $m=m*\chi$ or any multiple; $m=1$ when $\chi$ is realizable over $F$), acting on $V=F^d$; $V\_\chi$ denotes an abstract representation with character $\chi$. The Euler factor is

$$L\_\ell(s,\chi)=P\_\ell(\chi;\ell^{-s})^{-1},\qquad P\_\ell(\chi;T)=\det\big(1-\rho\_\chi(\mathrm{Fr}*\ell),T\ \big|\ V*\chi^{I\_\ell}\big),$$

with $\mathrm{Fr}\_\ell$ an arithmetic Frobenius lift (inducing $x\mapsto x^\ell$ on the residue field); the geometric convention replaces $\chi$ by $\bar\chi$.

## 1. The Frobenius coset from the residual action

**Proposition 1.1.** Let $k\_L=\mathcal{O}*L/\mathfrak{m}L=\mathbb{F}{\ell^f}$, and let $D\to\mathrm{Gal}(k\_L/\mathbb{F}*\ell)$ be the residual action. Its kernel is $I$ and the preimage of $x\mapsto x^\ell$ is a single coset $\Phi=\mathrm{Fr},I$, which generates $D/I\cong\mathbb{Z}/f$. In the $\ell$-adic numbering ([local Galois groups](https://claude.ai/chat/local-galois-groups.md)), with $\omega$ the Teichmüller generator of [the filtration](https://claude.ai/chat/ramification-filtration.md) Prop. 1.1, $\Phi={\sigma\in D:\sigma\omega=\omega^\ell}$. In terms of the roots: every $\sigma\in\Phi$ satisfies $\sigma\bar\beta\_i=\bar\beta\_i^{,\ell}$ for all residues $\bar\beta\_i\in k\_L$ of the roots; conversely, if the residues $\bar\beta\_1,\dots,\bar\beta\_n$ generate $k\_L$ over $\mathbb{F}*\ell$, then $\Phi={\sigma\in D:\sigma\bar\beta\_i=\bar\beta\_i^{,\ell}\ \forall i}$; if they generate only a subfield $k'\subsetneq k\_L$, this condition cuts out the union of the cosets $\mathrm{Fr}^{a}I$ with $a\equiv1\bmod[k':\mathbb{F}*\ell]$, and $\omega$ is needed to select $\Phi$. Transported by a matching $\tau$ ([matching](https://claude.ai/chat/matching.md) to [well-definedness](https://claude.ai/chat/well-definedness.md)), $\tau\Phi\tau^{-1}=\mathrm{Fr}*\ell I*\ell\subseteq D\_\ell\le G$, well defined up to $G$-conjugacy together with $D\_\ell$ and $I\_\ell$.

*Proof.* $D/I\cong\mathrm{Gal}(k\_L/\mathbb{F}*\ell)$ is cyclic generated by $x\mapsto x^\ell$; the Teichmüller argument of* [*the filtration*](https://claude.ai/chat/ramification-filtration.md) *Prop. 1.1 gives the description by $\omega$. An element acting as $x\mapsto x^{\ell^a}$ on $k\_L$ acts so on the subfield $k'$, and on $k'$ the maps $x\mapsto x^{\ell^a}$ and $x\mapsto x^\ell$ coincide iff $a\equiv1\bmod[k':\mathbb{F}*\ell]$. Transport: [well-definedness](https://claude.ai/chat/well-definedness.md) Theorem 1.2. $\square$

In practice $\Phi$ is read once from $\omega$ ([the filtration](https://claude.ai/chat/ramification-filtration.md) §1), and the class in $G$ of each element of the cosets $\mathrm{Fr}^kI\_\ell$ that enters below is obtained by the class identification of [the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md).

## 2. Three computations of $P\_\ell(\chi;T)$, and their agreement

**(A) From the model restricted along $D\_\ell\le G$.** Let $\sigma\_1,\dots,\sigma\_b$ generate $I\_\ell$ and $\mathrm{Fr}$ be any element of $\mathrm{Fr}*\ell I*\ell$ (all as permutations, hence as words in the generators of $G$, hence as matrices $\rho(\cdot)$). Compute

$$V^{I}=\bigcap\_{k=1}^{b}\ker\big(\rho(\sigma\_k)-1\big)\ \subseteq F^d\qquad\text{or}\qquad V^I=\mathrm{im}\Big(\frac1{|I|}\sum\_{\sigma\in I}\rho(\sigma)\Big),$$

a basis $B$ of $V^I$, the matrix $A$ of $\rho(\mathrm{Fr})$ in $B$ (well defined: $\rho(\mathrm{Fr})V^I\subseteq V^I$ because $I\trianglelefteq D$), and $Q(T)=\det(1-AT)\in F[T]$. Then $Q=P\_\ell(\chi;T)^m$, and $P\_\ell(\chi;T)$ is the unique $m$-th root of $Q$ in $1+TF[[T]]$, computed to degree $\deg Q/m$ (its coefficients lie in $\mathcal{O}\_K$, [the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md) Thm. 5.1(5)).

**(B) From the character, by traces.** $t\_k:=\mathrm{tr}\big(\rho\_\chi(\mathrm{Fr})^k\big|V\_\chi^I\big)=\frac{1}{|I|}\sum\_{\sigma\in I}\chi(\mathrm{Fr}^k\sigma)$ for $k=1,\dots,\dim V^I\_\chi$, and $P\_\ell(\chi;T)=\exp\big(-\sum\_{k\ge1}t\_kT^k/k\big)$ truncated at degree $\dim V\_\chi^I$, equivalently via Newton's identities from $t\_1,\dots,t\_{\dim V^I\_\chi}$ ([the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md) Theorem 5.1(1)).

**(C) From the character, by eigenvalue multiplicities.** Let $\psi\_j$ ($j\in\mathbb{Z}/f$) be the linear characters of $D\_\ell$ trivial on $I\_\ell$ with $\psi\_j(\mathrm{Fr})=\zeta\_f^{,j}$. Then
$$P\_\ell(\chi;T)=\prod\_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/f}\big(1-\zeta\_f^{,j},T\big)^{\langle\chi|*{D*\ell},,\psi\_j\rangle},\qquad \langle\chi|*{D*\ell},\psi\_j\rangle=\frac{1}{|D\_\ell|}\sum\_{k=0}^{f-1}\zeta\_f^{-jk}\sum\_{\sigma\in I\_\ell}\chi(\mathrm{Fr}^k\sigma)\in\mathbb{Z}\_{\ge0}.$$

**Theorem 2.1.** (A), (B), (C) give the same polynomial, of degree $\dim V\_\chi^I$, with coefficients in $\mathcal{O}*K$ and roots of unity of order dividing $f$ as reciprocal roots; and $P*\ell(\chi;T)$ depends on none of: the lift $\mathrm{Fr}\in\mathrm{Fr}*\ell I*\ell$; the prime $\mathfrak{L}\mid\ell$ or the matching; the model $\rho$ and the multiple $m$; the basis $B$.

*Proof.* Lift. $V^I$ is a $D$-stable subspace on which $I$ acts trivially, so $D/I$ acts on it; for $\mathrm{Fr}'=\mathrm{Fr}\tau$ or $\tau\mathrm{Fr}$ with $\tau\in I$, $\rho(\mathrm{Fr}')|*{V^I}=\rho(\mathrm{Fr})|*{V^I}$ as operators, not merely conjugate, since $\rho(\tau)$ is the identity on $V^I$ and $\rho(\mathrm{Fr})$ preserves $V^I$. Hence (A) is lift-independent; (B) is lift-independent because the multiset ${\mathrm{Fr}^k\sigma:\sigma\in I}$ equals ${\mathrm{Fr}'^k\sigma:\sigma\in I}$ ($I\trianglelefteq D$, [the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md) Theorem 5.1); (C) because $\psi\_j$ is a function on $D/I$. Prime and matching: conjugation by $g\in G$ replaces $(D,I,\mathrm{Fr})$ by conjugates and $\chi$ is a class function; for (A), $\rho(g)$ carries $V^I$ to $V^{gIg^{-1}}$ and intertwines the two operators. Model: (A) with trace $m\chi$ gives $\det(1-\rho(\mathrm{Fr})T|V^I)=\prod$ over the $m$ copies $=P\_\ell(\chi;T)^m$ ([the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md) Corollary 5.2), and $m$-th roots in $1+TF[[T]]$ are unique. Basis: determinants are basis-free. Agreement of (A) and (B): $\mathrm{tr}(\rho(\mathrm{Fr})^k|V^I)=\mathrm{tr}(\rho(\mathrm{Fr})^ke\_I)$ with $e\_I=|I|^{-1}\sum\_{\sigma\in I}\rho(\sigma)$ the projector onto $V^I$, commuting with $\rho(\mathrm{Fr})$; this is [the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md) Theorem 5.1(1). Agreement of (B) and (C): $V^I$ is a representation of the cyclic group $D/I=\langle\mathrm{Fr}I\rangle$ of order $f$, so it decomposes into the eigenlines of $\rho(\mathrm{Fr})$ with eigenvalues $\zeta\_f^j$, the eigenvalue $\zeta\_f^j$ having multiplicity equal to the multiplicity of $\psi\_j$ in $V^I$ as a $D$-module, which is $\langle\chi|\_D,\psi\_j\rangle$ because $\psi\_j$ is trivial on $I$ and $\mathrm{Hom}\_D(\psi\_j,V)=\mathrm{Hom}*D(\psi\_j,V^I)$; the displayed inner product is the definition, with $\psi\_j$ constant on the cosets $\mathrm{Fr}^kI$. Degree: $\dim V^I$. Coefficients: elementary symmetric functions of roots of unity, invariant under $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/K)$ since expressed through values of $\chi$. Reciprocal roots: $\mathrm{Fr}^f\in I$ acts trivially on $V^I$, so $\rho(\mathrm{Fr})|*{V^I}$ has order dividing $f$. $\square$

**Corollary 2.2 (what is required of the computations).** (C) is the primary computation: it needs the class in $G$ of every element of $D\_\ell$ (one class identification per element, $|D\_\ell|$ in all, or per class of $D\_\ell$), the table row $\chi$, and $\zeta\_f$; it is exact in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_f]$ and yields $P\_\ell(\chi;T)$ for all $\chi$ at once from the vector $\big(\sum\_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Fr}^kI\_\ell\cap C\_r}1\big)*{k,r}$ of class counts of the cosets. (A) is the certificate: it uses the model and linear algebra over $F$ of size $d=m\chi(1)$, and must return $P*\ell(\chi;T)^m$.

## 3. The dimension check and further checks

**Proposition 3.1.** $\dim V\_\chi^{I\_\ell}=\langle\mathrm{Res}*{I*\ell}\chi,1\rangle=\frac{1}{|I\_\ell|}\sum\_{\sigma\in I\_\ell}\chi(\sigma)$, a nonnegative integer computable from the table and the class counts of $I\_\ell$; it equals $\chi(1)$ minus the tame part $\mathrm{codim}V^{I\_\ell}$ of $f\_\ell(\chi)$ ([conductor exponents](https://claude.ai/chat/conductor-exponents.md) §1), so
$$\deg P\_\ell(\chi;T)=\chi(1)-\big(f\_\ell(\chi)-\mathrm{sw}\_\ell(\chi)\big).$$

*Proof.* $e\_I=|I|^{-1}\sum\_{\sigma\in I}\rho\_\chi(\sigma)$ is the projector onto $V^I$, so $\dim V^I=\mathrm{tr},e\_I=|I|^{-1}\sum\_{\sigma\in I}\chi(\sigma)$, which is the multiplicity of the trivial character in $\chi|\_I$. The second statement is [conductor exponents](https://claude.ai/chat/conductor-exponents.md) Proposition 1.1's decomposition into the $i=0$ term and the Swan part. $\square$

**Mandatory checks on each $P\_\ell(\chi;T)$.**

1. $\deg P=\dim V^I$ computed by Proposition 3.1 from the character, and, when the model is used, $\mathrm{rank}$ of the projector $e\_I$ on $F^d$ equals $m\cdot\dim V^I$.
2. $P(T)$ divides $(1-T^f)^{\dim V^I}$ in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_f][T]$, and $P(T)\in\mathcal{O}\_K[T]$ after expansion (the coefficients, computed in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_f]$, must lie in $\mathcal{O}\_K\subseteq\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_e]$).
3. $P\_\ell(\chi^\tau;T)=\tau(P\_\ell(\chi;T))$ for $\tau\in\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$, and $P\_\ell(\bar\chi;T)=\overline{P\_\ell(\chi;T)}$.
4. $\prod\_\chi P\_\ell(\chi;T)^{\chi(1)}=(1-T^f)^{[G\:D\_\ell]}$: the identity $\zeta\_N=\prod\_\chi L(s,\chi)^{\chi(1)}$ at $\ell$, the $[G\:D\_\ell]$ primes of $N$ above $\ell$ each contributing $(1-\ell^{-fs})^{-1}$.
5. For the permutation character $\pi\_{H\_j}$ of a global factor $f\_j$: $P\_\ell(\pi\_{H\_j};T)=\prod\_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\ell}(1-T^{f(\mathfrak{p}/\ell)})$ over the primes $\mathfrak{p}$ of $K\_j$ above $\ell$, whose residue degrees are the $f$'s of the $\ell$-adic factors of $f\_j$ ([the ramified primes](https://claude.ai/chat/ramified-primes.md)); so the Euler factors of permutation characters are checked against the local factorization data alone.

*Proofs.* 1 to 3 are Theorem 2.1. 4: for the regular representation $\mathbb{C}[G]$ (left multiplication), $\mathbb{C}[G]^{I}$ has basis the indicator functions of the right cosets $Ig$, indexed by $I\backslash G$; $\mathrm{Fr}$ maps $Ig$ to $\mathrm{Fr},Ig=I,\mathrm{Fr},g$ (it normalizes $I$), and $\mathrm{Fr}^kIg=Ig$ iff $\mathrm{Fr}^kg\in Ig$ iff $\mathrm{Fr}^k\in I$ iff $f\mid k$; so every $\mathrm{Fr}$-orbit on $I\backslash G$ has length $f$, there are $[G\:I]/f=[G\:D]$ of them, and the characteristic polynomial of such a permutation is $(1-T^f)^{[G\:D]}$; the left side equals $\det(1-\mathrm{Fr},T\mid\mathbb{C}[G]^I)$ because $\sum\_\chi\chi(1)\chi$ is the regular character. 5: $\mathrm{Res}*D\pi*{H\_j}=\sum\_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrm{Ind}*{D*{\mathfrak p}}^D1$ (Mackey, [conductor exponents](https://claude.ai/chat/conductor-exponents.md) §4), and $(\mathrm{Ind}*{D*\mathfrak{p}}^D1)^I$ with $\mathrm{Fr}$ acting is the permutation module on $I\backslash D/D\_{\mathfrak{p}}$, a single $\mathrm{Fr}$-orbit of length $f(\mathfrak{p}/\ell)=[D\:ID\_{\mathfrak{p}}]$. $\square$

## 4. Placement and cost

At each ramified $\ell$: the class counts of the cosets $\mathrm{Fr}^kI\_\ell$ ($k=0,\dots,f-1$) cost $|D\_\ell|$ class identifications once; then every $P\_\ell(\chi;T)$ is a product of at most $f$ binomial powers with exponents given by $r$-term sums, $O(rf)$ per character. The model computation (A) costs $O(b,d^{\omega})$ field operations over $F$ plus one characteristic polynomial and an $m$-th root, and is run as a certificate for the characters of interest (the odd two-dimensional ones of family 3, and any $\chi$ whose $L$-function is tabulated). The outputs $P\_\ell(\chi;T)$ for all ramified $\ell$, together with the unramified factors $P\_\ell(\chi;T)=\prod\_j(1-\zeta\_o^jT)^{m\_j(\mathrm{Fr}\_\ell)}$ from the eigenvalue multiplicities of [the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md) Prop. 2.1(4) at the classes of [rational classes](https://claude.ai/chat/rational-classes.md) to [the direct route](https://claude.ai/chat/direct-route.md), and the conductors of [conductor exponents](https://claude.ai/chat/conductor-exponents.md) to [the global conductor](https://claude.ai/chat/global-conductor.md), are the complete local data of $L(s,\chi)$ entering the functional equation.
