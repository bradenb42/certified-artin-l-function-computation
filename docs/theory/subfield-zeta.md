# The Dedekind zeta identities for the subfields $N^H$

**Setting.** $H\le G$ of small index $m\_H=[G\:H]$; $F\_H=N^H=\mathbb{Q}(\beta\_H)$ with $\beta\_H=F\_H(\alpha)$ the value of an $H$-invariant and $R\_H\in\mathbb{Z}[x]$ its minimal polynomial ([separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md) §3, [the Euler identities](https://claude.ai/chat/euler-identities.md) §2; $R\_H$ squarefree of degree $m\_H$, computed exactly at $p$). $\pi\_H=\mathrm{Ind}*H^G\mathbf{1}=\sum*{\chi}n\_\chi(H)\chi$ with $n\_\chi(H)=\langle\chi|*H,1\rangle=\langle\chi,\pi\_H\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}*{\ge0}$ from the table; $r\_1(H),r\_2(H)$ the signature of $F\_H$; $d\_{F\_H}$ its discriminant. The characters $\chi$ with $n\_\chi(H)>0$ are the *constituents* of $\pi\_H$; $\mathbf{1}$ is always one, with $n\_\mathbf{1}=1$.

## 1. The factorization

**Theorem 1.1.** $\zeta\_{F\_H}(s)=L(s,\pi\_H)=\prod\_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}L(s,\chi)^{n\_\chi(H)}$, and the completed versions agree:

$$\Lambda\_{F\_H}(s):=|d\_{F\_H}|^{s/2},\Gamma\_\mathbb{R}(s)^{r\_1+r\_2},\Gamma\_\mathbb{R}(s+1)^{r\_2},\zeta\_{F\_H}(s)=\prod\_\chi\Lambda(s,\chi)^{n\_\chi(H)},$$

with $|d\_{F\_H}|=\prod\_\chi\mathfrak{f}(\chi)^{n\_\chi(H)}$, $r\_1+r\_2=\sum\_\chi n\_\chi(H)a\_\chi$, $r\_2=\sum\_\chi n\_\chi(H)b\_\chi$, and $\prod\_\chi W(\chi)^{n\_\chi(H)}=1$.

*Proof.* Euler factors: at every prime $\ell$, $P\_\ell(\pi\_H;T)=\prod\_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\ell}(1-T^{f(\mathfrak{p}/\ell)})$ by [the Euler identities](https://claude.ai/chat/euler-identities.md) Proposition 2.1 (inductivity with $\theta=\mathbf{1}$, valid at ramified and unramified $\ell$), which is the reciprocal Euler factor of $\zeta\_{F\_H}$; additivity gives $L(s,\pi\_H)=\prod\_\chi L(s,\chi)^{n\_\chi}$. Discriminant: [the global conductor](https://claude.ai/chat/global-conductor.md) Theorem 6.2 with $K=H$, $\theta=\mathbf{1}$ ($\mathfrak{f}*{F\_H}(\mathbf{1})=1$) gives $\mathfrak{f}(\pi\_H)=|d*{F\_H}|$, and $\mathfrak{f}$ is multiplicative. Gamma factor: $\pi\_H(c)=\mathrm{fix}*{G/H}(c)=r\_1$ (*[*the archimedean place*](https://claude.ai/chat/archimedean.md) *Prop. 1.1(d): the real roots of $R\_H$) and $\pi\_H(1)=m\_H=r\_1+2r\_2$, so $a*{\pi\_H}=\frac{m\_H+r\_1}{2}=r\_1+r\_2$, $b\_{\pi\_H}=r\_2$ ([the archimedean place](https://claude.ai/chat/archimedean.md) Prop. 2.1), and $a,b$ are additive. Root number: $\Lambda\_{F\_H}(s)=\Lambda\_{F\_H}(1-s)$ (Hecke), so $W(\pi\_H)=1$, and $W$ is multiplicative ([the global root number](https://claude.ai/chat/global-root-numbers.md) Prop. 1.1 with the product formula). $\square$

## 2. The coefficientwise verification

**Direct side.** $\zeta\_{F\_H}(s)=\sum\_{m\ge1}c\_m(H)m^{-s}$ with $c\_m(H)=#{\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathcal{O}*{F\_H}:\ N\mathfrak{a}=m}$, multiplicative, $c*{\ell^k}(H)=#{(k\_\mathfrak{p})*{\mathfrak{p}\mid\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}*{\ge0}^{g\_\ell}:\ \sum\_\mathfrak{p}f(\mathfrak{p}/\ell)k\_\mathfrak{p}=k}$, i.e. the coefficient of $T^k$ in $\prod\_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\ell}(1-T^{f(\mathfrak{p}/\ell)})^{-1}$. The residue degrees at $\ell$ come from the prime splitting in $F\_H$: for $\ell\nmid\mathrm{disc}R\_H$ from the factorization of $R\_H$ modulo $\ell$ (Kummer to Dedekind), and for $\ell\mid\mathrm{disc}R\_H$ from the $\ell$-maximal order of $\mathbb{Z}[\beta\_H]$ ([the ramified primes](https://claude.ai/chat/ramified-primes.md) §2). Nothing about $G$, the table, or the Galois computations enters; the only inputs are $R\_H$ and $\ell$-adic factorization.

**Character side.** $b\_m(H)$ = the $m$-th coefficient of $\prod\_\chi L(s,\chi)^{n\_\chi(H)}$, computed by Dirichlet convolution of the coefficient sequences $a\_\cdot(\chi)$ of [Euler factors](https://claude.ai/chat/euler-factors.md) to [the Euler identities](https://claude.ai/chat/euler-identities.md) and [rational classes](https://claude.ai/chat/rational-classes.md) to [the direct route](https://claude.ai/chat/direct-route.md) (unramified primes: $P\_\ell(\chi;T)=\prod\_j(1-\zeta\_o^jT)^{m\_j(\mathrm{Fr}\_\ell)}$ from [the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md) Prop. 2.1(4)), truncated at $X$; exact in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_e]$.

**Proposition 2.1.** $c\_m(H)=b\_m(H)$ for all $m\le X$ iff for every prime $\ell\le X$,

$$\prod\_\chi P\_\ell(\chi;T)^{n\_\chi(H)}\equiv\prod\_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\ell}\big(1-T^{f(\mathfrak{p}/\ell)}\big)\pmod{T^{\lfloor\log\_\ell X\rfloor+1}}.$$

Since the right side has degree $\le m\_H$, the congruence is a full equality for $\ell\le X^{1/m\_H}$ and a partial one, through the first $\lfloor\log\_\ell X\rfloor$ coefficients, for larger $\ell$; for $\ell>\sqrt X$ it is the single equation $\sum\_\chi n\_\chi(H),a\_\ell(\chi)=#{\mathfrak{p}\mid\ell:\ f(\mathfrak{p}/\ell)=1}=\mathrm{fix}*{G/H}(\mathrm{Frob}*\ell)$ at unramified $\ell$, the count of [rational classes](https://claude.ai/chat/rational-classes.md) to [separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md).

*Proof.* Both sides are multiplicative, so coefficientwise equality up to $X$ is equality of the local factors modulo $T^{k+1}$ for $\ell^k\le X$; $b\_m$ and $c\_m$ are integers, the left side being a rational-valued combination of Galois-stable data. $\square$

**Verification.** For each $H$ in the family $\mathcal{H}$ (the cyclic subgroups of $G$ and the point stabilizers of the factors, plus whatever [separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md) to [cyclotomic refinement](https://claude.ai/chat/cyclotomic-refinement.md) used): (i) $c\_m(H)=b\_m(H)$ for $m\le X$; (ii) $|d\_{F\_H}|=\prod\_\chi\mathfrak{f}(\chi)^{n\_\chi(H)}$ with $d\_{F\_H}$ from [the ramified primes](https://claude.ai/chat/ramified-primes.md) applied to $R\_H$, one exact integer equation per $H$, in addition to [conductor exponents](https://claude.ai/chat/conductor-exponents.md)'s per-prime version; (iii) $r\_1(H)=\sum\_\chi n\_\chi(H)\chi(c)$, with $r\_1(H)$ the number of real roots of $R\_H$ (Sturm); (iv) $\prod\_\chi W(\chi)^{n\_\chi(H)}=1$, an exact identity among the algebraic numbers of [the global root number](https://claude.ai/chat/global-root-numbers.md). All four are exact; none involves a tail bound or a choice of $t$.

## 3. What the identities determine, and what they detect that [the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) does not

Let $\mathcal{D}(\chi)=({a\_m(\chi)}*{m\le X},\mathfrak{f}(\chi),(a*\chi,b\_\chi),W(\chi))$ be the data of $\chi$ as assembled, and suppose the actual data used are $\mathcal{D}'(\chi)$, possibly wrong. [the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) tests, for each $\chi$ separately, that $\mathcal{D}'(\chi)$ is consistent with a functional equation (a rejection test with analytic resolution $\mathcal{E}$). The identities of §2 test, for each $H\in\mathcal{H}$, the multiplicity-weighted product of the $\mathcal{D}'(\chi)$ against data of $F\_H$ computed independently and exactly.

**Theorem 3.1 (what is tested).** Let $\mathcal{H}$ contain the cyclic subgroups. Then the identities (i) to (iv) over $\mathcal{H}$ hold iff, for every rational virtual character $\theta\in R\_\mathbb{Q}(G)$, the data of $\theta$ assembled from the $\mathcal{D}'(\chi)$ by multiplicativity, coefficients up to $X$, conductor, Gamma exponents, root number, equal the true data of $\theta$. In particular they hold iff, for every Galois orbit $O$ of irreducible characters, the *orbit products* $\prod\_{\chi\in O}\mathcal{D}'(\chi)$ (coefficientwise, and multiplicatively for $\mathfrak{f}$ and $W$, additively for $(a,b)$) are correct.

*Proof.* Each identity is the statement for $\theta=\pi\_H$; the $\pi\_H$ span $R\_\mathbb{Q}(G)\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ ([rational classes](https://claude.ai/chat/rational-classes.md) Theorem 3.1) and, after multiplication by the Artin exponent, $R\_\mathbb{Q}(G)$ itself; the assembled data are multiplicative/additive in $\theta$, and root extraction in $1+T\mathbb{Q}[[T]]$, in $\mathbb{Q}*{>0}$ and in $\mathbb{Z}$ is unique, so equality for all $\pi\_H$ propagates to all of $R*\mathbb{Q}(G)$ and conversely; the orbit sums $\sum\_{\chi\in O}\chi$ form a basis of $R\_\mathbb{Q}(G)\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ ([the Euler identities](https://claude.ai/chat/euler-identities.md) Theorem 3.1). $\square$

**Theorem 3.2 (detected by §2 but not by** [**the functional-equation test**](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md)**).** The following per-character error patterns pass every test of [the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) (at every $t$, for every $X$, and with any resolution) but are rejected by the identities of §2 as soon as they affect a coefficient $m\le X$, the conductor, the signature or the root number:

1. *Substitution between non-conjugate characters.* $\mathcal{D}'(\chi)=\mathcal{D}(\chi')$ and $\mathcal{D}'(\chi')=\mathcal{D}(\chi)$ for two irreducible $\chi,\chi'$ in different Galois orbits (with equal degree so that the substitution is well typed): each substituted $L$-function is a genuine Artin $L$-function with its own correct functional equation, so [the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) passes; but there is $H$ with $n\_\chi(H)\ne n\_{\chi'}(H)$ (rational class functions separate Galois orbits, [the Euler identities](https://claude.ai/chat/euler-identities.md) Theorem 3.1(c) and the argument of [rational classes](https://claude.ai/chat/rational-classes.md)), and for that $H$ the identity (i) fails at the first $m\le X$ where $a\_m(\chi)\ne a\_m(\chi')$, which exists for $X$ beyond the first prime where the Frobenius classes differ in $\chi$ and $\chi'$, and (ii) fails if $\mathfrak{f}(\chi)\ne\mathfrak{f}(\chi')$, (iii) if $\chi(c)\ne\chi'(c)$, (iv) if $W(\chi)\ne W(\chi')$. More generally any permutation of the rows of the table that does not preserve Galois orbits, applied to the whole data, passes [the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) and fails §2.
2. *Errors at primes near $X$.* A wrong Frobenius class at an unramified $\ell\le X$ changes $a\_\ell(\chi)$ by a nonzero algebraic integer; [the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) sees it only through $g\_\chi(\ell t/\sqrt{\mathfrak f})\cdot|\Delta a\_\ell|$ against $\mathcal{E}$, which for $\ell$ near $X$ is below resolution at the $t$ used ([the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) §5(1)); the identity (i) at $H=G\_1$ or any $H$ with $\sum\_\chi n\_\chi(H)\Delta a\_\ell(\chi)\ne0$ sees the exact integer discrepancy. Errors at a single $\chi$ with $n\_\chi(H)\ne0$ are always of this form for some $H$ unless they cancel against errors in the other characters of the same orbit product.
3. *Errors below the analytic resolution at any prime.* Same mechanism: (i) is an exact comparison; [the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) is not.
4. *Conductor and root-number errors before* [*the functional-equation test*](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md)*'s threshold.* A wrong $\mathfrak{f}'(\chi)$ or $W'(\chi)$ is rejected by [the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) only once $X\ge X\_0$ ([the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) Theorems 4.1 to 4.2); (ii) and (iv) reject it immediately provided the orbit product of the error is nontrivial, e.g. a wrong $W'(\chi)=W(\chi)u$ with $u\ne1$ for a rational-valued $\chi$, or a wrong conductor at one $\chi$ of an orbit.
5. *Multiplicity errors in the assembled characters.* If a character used in the pipeline is a wrong combination of irreducibles (e.g. a row of the table with a wrong multiplicity of $\mathbf{1}$ or of a constituent), [the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) for that combination may still pass if the combination is a genuine character; (i) to (iv) with the correct $n\_\chi(H)$ fail.

**Theorem 3.3 (detected by** [**the functional-equation test**](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) **but not by §2).** Errors that preserve every orbit product pass §2 for every $H$: in particular an *intra-orbit inconsistency at a single prime*: any modification of the factors ${P\_\ell(\chi^\tau)}*{\tau}$ of one Galois orbit at one prime $\ell$ that preserves their product (for instance exchanging $P*\ell(\chi)$ and $P\_\ell(\bar\chi)$ at $\ell$ only) is invisible to §2, while it breaks the functional equation of $\chi$ ([the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) Theorem 4.3, the modified $L'(\chi)$ not being an Artin $L$-function), so [the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) rejects it once $X\ge X\_0(\ell,\chi)$.

**Theorem 3.4 (detected by neither).** (1) Consistent Galois relabelings $\chi\mapsto\chi^{\sigma\_O}$, one $\sigma\_O$ per orbit, applied to the entire data; these are the only errors that preserve both every functional equation and every orbit product and are supported on $m\le X$, by Theorem 3.1 the orbit products fix the data of $R\_\mathbb{Q}(G)$, and what remains is the distribution within orbits, which [the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) constrains only up to a Galois automorphism ([the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) §5(2)). They are excluded by the matching certificate of [matching](https://claude.ai/chat/matching.md) and, for family 3, by the newform comparison with a fixed embedding. (2) Anything at $m>X$.

*Proofs of 3.2 to 3.4.* 3.2(1): $n\_\chi(H)=\langle\chi,\pi\_H\rangle$ and $\pi\_H\in\mathrm{Cl}*\mathbb{Q}(G)$; two irreducibles have the same inner products with all rational class functions iff they have the same Galois orbit average, iff they are conjugate. 3.2(2) to (4): exactness of (i) to (iv) versus the bounds of* [*the functional-equation test*](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) *§3 to §4. 3.2(5): Theorem 1.1 is an identity between irreducible constituents. 3.3: §2 depends on the data only through the products $\prod*{\chi\in O}$, by Theorem 3.1; [the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) Theorem 4.3 applies to each $\chi$. 3.4: Theorem 3.1 together with [the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) Remark 2.3 and §5. $\square$

## 4. Cost and placement

Per $H\in\mathcal{H}$: the splitting of all primes $\ell\le X$ in $F\_H$ (one factorization of $R\_H$ modulo $\ell$ per prime, $\tilde O(m\_H\log\ell)$ each; the same factorizations as [separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md)'s resolvent tests, reused), the $\ell$-maximal orders at the primes dividing $\mathrm{disc}R\_H$ ([the ramified primes](https://claude.ai/chat/ramified-primes.md)), one Sturm sequence, one discriminant; on the character side one Dirichlet convolution per constituent power, $O(X\log X)$ operations in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_e]$ per factor, and the final comparison of $X$ integers. The family should include, besides the cyclic subgroups (needed for Theorem 3.1), the point stabilizers $H\_j$ of the global factors ($F\_{H\_j}=\mathbb{Q}(\beta\_j)$, whose splitting data are those of [the ramified primes](https://claude.ai/chat/ramified-primes.md) and [local Galois groups](https://claude.ai/chat/local-galois-groups.md) directly) and any subgroup with a constituent of family 3 of multiplicity one, for which identity (i) becomes a direct check of the coefficients of the weight-one candidate against the ideal counts of a field of degree $[G\:H]$.
