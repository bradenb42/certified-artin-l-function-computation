# Identities among Euler factors across characters, and what they determine

*One of the derivations behind* [*artin*](https://claude.ai/README.md)*; the* [*index*](https://claude.ai/chat/README.md) *lists them all and names the code that implements each.*

**Setting.** $\ell$ fixed (ramified or not); $D=D\_\ell$, $I=I\_\ell$, $f=[D\:I]$, $\mathrm{Fr}\in\mathrm{Fr}*\ell I$; for a virtual character $\theta$ of $G$, $P*\ell(\theta;T)$ is defined by additivity from [Euler factors](https://claude.ai/chat/euler-factors.md) ($P\_\ell(\theta;T)=\det(1-\rho\_\theta(\mathrm{Fr})T\mid V\_\theta^{I})$ for characters, and quotients for differences). $\mathrm{Cl}*\mathbb{Q}(G)$ is the space of rational class functions of* [*rational classes*](https://claude.ai/chat/rational-classes.md)*; $R(G)$ the character ring; $R*\mathbb{Q}(G)=R(G)\cap\mathrm{Cl}\_\mathbb{Q}(G)$ the rational-valued virtual characters. For $H\le G$, $F\_H=N^H$, $\pi\_H=1\_H^G$.

## 1. Additivity and inductivity

**Proposition 1.1 (additivity).** $P\_\ell(\theta+\theta';T)=P\_\ell(\theta;T)P\_\ell(\theta';T)$; $P\_\ell$ extends to a homomorphism $R(G)\to(1+T,\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_e)[[T]])^\times$, and to $R(G)\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ by unique root extraction in $1+T,\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_e)[[T]]$.

*Proof.* $(V\oplus V')^I=V^I\oplus V'^I$ and the determinant of a block-diagonal operator is the product; $1+T,k[[T]]$ is uniquely divisible for $k$ of characteristic $0$. $\square$

**Proposition 1.2 (inductivity).** Let $H\le G$, $\theta$ a character of $H$, and for each prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of $F\_H$ above $\ell$ let $D\_\mathfrak{p}=D\cap gHg^{-1}$ be the decomposition group of the corresponding double coset $DgH$, $I\_\mathfrak{p}=I\cap D\_\mathfrak{p}$ its inertia, $f\_\mathfrak{p}=f(\mathfrak{p}/\ell)=[D\:ID\_\mathfrak{p}]$, and $\mathrm{Fr}*\mathfrak{p}\in D*\mathfrak{p}\cap\mathrm{Fr}^{f\_\mathfrak{p}}I$ a Frobenius of $\mathfrak{p}$. Then

$$P\_\ell(\mathrm{Ind}*H^G\theta;T)=\prod*{\mathfrak{p}\mid\ell}P\_\mathfrak{p}(\theta^g;T^{f\_\mathfrak{p}}),\qquad P\_\mathfrak{p}(\theta^g;T)=\det\big(1-\theta^g(\mathrm{Fr}*\mathfrak{p})T\ \big|\ W^{I*\mathfrak{p}}\big),$$

the local factor of $\theta$ over $F\_{H,\mathfrak{p}}$. In particular $L(s,\mathrm{Ind}\_H^G\theta)=L(s,\theta)$ (over $F\_H$) prime by prime.

*Proof.* Mackey: $\mathrm{Res}*D\mathrm{Ind}H^G\theta=\bigoplus{DgH}\mathrm{Ind}*{D\_\mathfrak{p}}^D\theta^g$, and by additivity it suffices to treat $U=\mathrm{Ind}*{D'}^DW$ for $D'=D*\mathfrak{p}$, $W=\theta^g$. Mackey again for $I\trianglelefteq D$: $\mathrm{Res}*IU=\bigoplus*{x\in I\backslash D/D'}\mathrm{Ind}*{I\cap xD'x^{-1}}^I(W^x)$, hence $U^I=\bigoplus\_xU\_x$ with $U\_x\cong(W^x)^{I\cap xD'x^{-1}}$. The double cosets $I\backslash D/D'$ are the cosets of $ID'$ in $D$, of number $f'=[D\:ID']$, permuted cyclically by $\mathrm{Fr}$ (as $D/I$ is cyclic generated by $\mathrm{Fr}I$): $D=\bigsqcup*{k=0}^{f'-1}\mathrm{Fr}^kID'$. So $\mathrm{Fr}$ maps $U\_{x\_k}$ to $U\_{x\_{k+1}}$ and $\mathrm{Fr}^{f'}$ maps $U\_{x\_0}$ to itself; for such a cyclic block permutation $\det(1-\mathrm{Fr},T\mid U^I)=\det(1-\mathrm{Fr}^{f'}T^{f'}\mid U\_{x\_0})$. Write $\mathrm{Fr}^{f'}=\iota,\mathrm{Fr}'$ with $\iota\in I$, $\mathrm{Fr}'\in D'$; on $U^I$, $\iota$ acts trivially, so $\mathrm{Fr}^{f'}|*{U*{x\_0}}=\mathrm{Fr}'|*{U*{x\_0}}$, and $U\_{x\_0}=W^{I\cap D'}$ with $\mathrm{Fr}'$ acting through $W$; $\mathrm{Fr}'$ induces $x\mapsto x^{\ell^{f'}}$ on the residue field of $L$, so it is a Frobenius of $L/F\_{H,\mathfrak{p}}$. $\square$

## 2. The identities with the local zeta factors of $N^H$

**Proposition 2.1.** For $H\le G$: $\displaystyle\prod\_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}P\_\ell(\chi;T)^{\langle\chi|*H,1\rangle}=P*\ell(\pi\_H;T)=Z\_{H,\ell}(T):=\prod\_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\ell\text{ in }F\_H}\big(1-T^{f(\mathfrak{p}/\ell)}\big)$, the reciprocal of the Euler factor of $\zeta\_{F\_H}$ at $\ell$.

*Proof.* $\pi\_H=\sum\_\chi\langle\chi,\pi\_H\rangle\chi$ with $\langle\chi,\pi\_H\rangle=\langle\chi|*H,1\rangle$ (Frobenius reciprocity); Proposition 1.2 with $\theta=1$ gives $P*\mathfrak{p}(1;T)=1-T$. $\square$

**Computing $Z\_{H,\ell}$ without Galois data.** Let $\beta\_H=F\_H(\alpha)$ be the value of an $H$-invariant with $\mathrm{Stab}*G(F\_H)=H$ (*[*separating subgroups*](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md) *§3), $R\_H(x)=\prod*{\tau\in G/H}(x-F\_H(\tau\alpha))\in\mathbb{Z}[x]$ its resolvent (computed exactly at $p$, [the precision policy](https://claude.ai/chat/precision-policy.md)/[separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md)), so that $F\_H=\mathbb{Q}(\beta\_H)$ and $R\_H$ is its minimal polynomial. If $\ell\nmid\mathrm{disc}R\_H$, factor $R\_H\bmod\ell=\prod\_k\bar h\_k$ into distinct irreducibles: by Kummer to Dedekind the primes above $\ell$ are in bijection with the $\bar h\_k$ and $f(\mathfrak{p}*k/\ell)=\deg\bar h\_k$. If $\ell\mid\mathrm{disc}R\_H$, compute the $\ell$-maximal order $\mathcal{O}'$ of $\mathbb{Z}[\beta\_H]$ (*[*the ramified primes*](https://claude.ai/chat/ramified-primes.md) *§2) and decompose $\mathcal{O}'/\ell\mathcal{O}'\cong\prod\_k\mathcal{O}'/\mathfrak{p}k^{e\_k}$: the residue degrees are the degrees over $\mathbb{F}\ell$ of the simple factors of the semisimple quotient. In either case $Z*{H,\ell}(T)=\prod\_k(1-T^{f\_k})$, obtained from a degree-$[G\:H]$ polynomial and $\ell$-adic arithmetic at precision $\le v\_\ell(\mathrm{disc}R\_H)+1$, with no reference to $D\_\ell$, the filtration, or any Frobenius coset.

The identities of Proposition 2.1 for a family $\mathcal{H}$ of subgroups of small index (the cyclic subgroups of $G$, the point stabilizers of the factors, the subgroups of [separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md) to [cyclotomic refinement](https://claude.ai/chat/cyclotomic-refinement.md)) are checks on the factors of [Euler factors](https://claude.ai/chat/euler-factors.md): each is an equality in $\mathbb{Z}[T]$ between a product of [Euler factors](https://claude.ai/chat/euler-factors.md) outputs (with exponents from the table) and a polynomial from [the ramified primes](https://claude.ai/chat/ramified-primes.md). For the point stabilizer $H\_j$ of a factor they are [Euler factors](https://claude.ai/chat/euler-factors.md) check 5.

## 3. What the system determines

Let $\Pi\_\mathcal{H}\subseteq R\_\mathbb{Q}(G)$ be the $\mathbb{Z}$-span of ${\pi\_H\:H\in\mathcal{H}}$. Regard the unknown as the homomorphism $P\_\ell\:R(G)\to1+T,\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_e)[[T]]$; the identities say $P\_\ell(\pi\_H)=Z\_{H,\ell}$ for $H\in\mathcal{H}$.

**Theorem 3.1.** (a) The identities determine $P\_\ell(\theta;T)$ for every $\theta\in\Pi\_\mathcal{H}$, as $\prod\_HZ\_{H,\ell}^{c\_H}$ when $\theta=\sum\_Hc\_H\pi\_H$.
(b) If $\mathcal{H}$ contains the cyclic subgroups of $G$, then $\Pi\_\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathbb{Q}=\mathrm{Cl}*\mathbb{Q}(G)$ (*[*rational classes*](https://claude.ai/chat/rational-classes.md) *Theorem 3.1) and there is an integer $A\ge1$ (the Artin exponent, a divisor of $|G|$) with $A\cdot R*\mathbb{Q}(G)\subseteq\Pi\_\mathcal{H}$; hence the identities determine $P\_\ell(\theta;T)^A$ for every $\theta\in R\_\mathbb{Q}(G)$, and by unique root extraction $P\_\ell(\theta;T)$ itself. In particular:

- for rational-valued $\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)$, $P\_\ell(\chi;T)$ is determined exactly;
- for every $\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)$, the orbit product $O\_\ell(\chi;T):=\prod\_{\tau\in\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\chi)/\mathbb{Q})}\tau\big(P\_\ell(\chi;T)\big)=P\_\ell\big(\sum\_\tau\chi^\tau;T\big)\in\mathbb{Z}[T]$ is determined exactly. (c) Nothing finer is determined by identities of this type: if $\chi\mapsto\sigma\_\chi\in\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\chi)/\mathbb{Q})$ is any choice constant on Galois orbits, the assignment $\chi\mapsto P\_\ell(\chi^{\sigma\_\chi};T)$ satisfies every identity $\prod\_\chi P(\chi)^{\langle\chi|*H,1\rangle}=Z*{H,\ell}$; more generally any assignment with the same orbit products does.

*Proof.* (a) Multiplicativity. (b) The $\mathbb{Q}$-span statement is [rational classes](https://claude.ai/chat/rational-classes.md); the existence of $A$ is the finiteness of the index of the permutation lattice in $R\_\mathbb{Q}(G)$ (Artin's induction theorem in its permutation form; Lam's computation of the Artin exponent gives $A\mid|G|$). Rational-valued irreducibles and orbit sums lie in $R\_\mathbb{Q}(G)$. (c) The multiplicities $\langle\chi|\_H,1\rangle$ are rational, hence constant on Galois orbits of $\chi$, so each identity is a product over orbits of (orbit product)$^{m\_O}$, unchanged by permuting factors within an orbit or replacing them by any factors with the same product. $\square$

This is the Euler-factor face of [rational classes](https://claude.ai/chat/rational-classes.md): permutation characters see rational classes, so the identities see Galois orbits of characters.

## 4. Characters of degree at most two

The additional structural information available without identifying the Frobenius coset is: $\deg P\_\ell(\chi;T)=\dim V\_\chi^{I}=\langle\chi|*I,1\rangle$ (*[*Euler factors*](https://claude.ai/chat/euler-factors.md) *Prop. 3.1, needing only $I*\ell\le G$ and the table); $P\_\ell(\chi;T)\in\mathcal{O}*K[T]$ with $K=\mathbb{Q}(\chi)$ (*[*the character table*](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md) *Theorem 5.1(5)); and all reciprocal roots in $\mu\_f$ (*[*Euler factors*](https://claude.ai/chat/euler-factors.md) *Theorem 2.1). Write $P*\ell(\chi;T)=\prod\_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/f}(1-\zeta\_f^{,j}T)^{a\_j}$, $a\_j\ge0$, $\sum\_ja\_j=\dim V^I$, so that $O\_\ell(\chi;T)=\prod\_j(1-\zeta\_f^jT)^{b\_j}$ with $b\_j=\sum\_{\tau}a\_{t\_\tau^{-1}j}$, where $\tau\mapsto t\_\tau\in(\mathbb{Z}/f)^\times$ records the action of $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\chi)/\mathbb{Q})$ on $\mu\_f\cap K$-conjugates.

**Theorem 4.1 (what degree and integrality add).** Let $\mathcal{P}$ be the set of polynomials $P'\in\mathcal{O}*K[T]$ with $\deg P'=\dim V^I$, reciprocal roots in $\mu\_f$, and $\prod*\tau\tau(P')=O\_\ell(\chi;T)$. Then $P\_\ell(\chi;T)\in\mathcal{P}$, and:

1. If $\dim V^I=0$: $\mathcal{P}={1}$, exact.
2. If $\dim V^I=1$: $\mathcal{P}={1-\tau(\lambda)T:\tau\in\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})}$, the Galois orbit of the true factor $1-\lambda T$. It is a singleton iff $\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}$, i.e. $\lambda=\pm1$; this holds for every $\ell$ whenever $\mu\_f\cap K\subseteq{\pm1}$, in particular whenever $K$ is real or $\gcd(f,w\_K)\le2$ ($w\_K$ the number of roots of unity in $K$).
3. If $\dim V^I=2$: $\mathcal{P}$ contains the Galois orbit of $P\_\ell(\chi;T)$ but can be strictly larger, even after imposing the exact value of the determinant. Example: $K\supseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_5)$, $f$ divisible by $5$, true factor $P=(1-\zeta\_5T)(1-\zeta\_5^2T)$; then $O=\prod\_{t\in(\mathbb{Z}/5)^\times}(1-\zeta\_5^tT)(1-\zeta\_5^{2t}T)=\prod\_{j\ne0}(1-\zeta\_5^jT)^2$, and $P'=(1-\zeta\_5^4T)^2$ has the same degree, integrality, reciprocal roots, orbit product, and the same product of reciprocal roots $\zeta\_5^3$ (the determinant), yet is not a Galois conjugate of $P$.

Consequently, for $\chi(1)\le2$ the system together with $\dim V^I$ and integrality determines $P\_\ell(\chi;T)$ exactly when $\chi$ is rational-valued, or when $\dim V^I\le1$ and the tame Frobenius eigenvalue is $\pm1$; in the remaining cases it determines it only up to the alternatives of items 2 to 3, and the claim that degree and integrality always remove the ambiguity is false.

*Proof.* 1 is trivial. 2: $P'=1-\lambda'T$ with $\lambda'$ a reciprocal root of $O$, i.e. $\lambda'=\tau(\lambda)$; conversely each $\tau(\lambda)$ lies in $K$ and in $\mu\_f$ and has the same orbit product. Rationality of $\lambda$ forces $\lambda=\pm1$; if $\mu\_f\cap K\subseteq{\pm1}$ every candidate is $\pm1$ and the orbit is a point. 3: direct verification in the example: the multiset of reciprocal roots of $\prod\_t\tau\_t(P')$ is ${4t}\_t$ with multiplicity $2$, i.e. every $j\ne0$ twice; $\zeta\_5^{4+4}=\zeta\_5^3=\zeta\_5^{1+2}$; and ${4,4}\ne{t,2t}$ for every $t$. $\square$

## 5. Removing the residual ambiguity without identifying the Frobenius coset

Two exact inputs are available that are not Galois-symmetric in $\chi$: the values $\eta(\mathrm{Fr}*\ell)$ of the linear characters $\eta$ of $G$ unramified at $\ell$, which* [*cyclotomic refinement*](https://claude.ai/chat/cyclotomic-refinement.md) *gives exactly as Dirichlet character values $\chi*\eta(\ell)$ with $\ell\nmid c\_\eta$, through the pinned map $\iota$; and $\det\rho\_\chi$ ([the global conductor](https://claude.ai/chat/global-conductor.md) Prop. 2.1).

**Theorem 5.1 (twisting).** Let $U\_\ell={\eta(\mathrm{Fr}*\ell):\eta\in\widehat{G^{ab}},\ \eta|*{I\_\ell}=1}\le\mu\_f$, the image of $\mathrm{Fr}*\ell$ in $G/[G,G]I*\ell$ read through the characters. For each such $\eta$, $P\_\ell(\chi\eta;T)=P\_\ell(\chi;\eta(\mathrm{Fr}*\ell)T)$, and the orbit product $O*\ell(\chi\eta;T)$ is determined by the identities (Theorem 3.1). The multiplicity of the factor $(1-T)$ in $O\_\ell(\chi\eta;T)$ equals $[\mathbb{Q}(\chi\eta):\mathbb{Q}]\cdot a\_{j(\eta)}$ where $\zeta\_f^{,j(\eta)}=\eta(\mathrm{Fr}*\ell)^{-1}$. Hence the twisted identities determine $a\_j$ for every $j$ with $\zeta\_f^{-j}\in U*\ell$, and together with the untwisted orbit product they determine $P\_\ell(\chi;T)$ exactly whenever every reciprocal root $\lambda$ of $P\_\ell(\chi;T)$ with $\lambda\notin\mathbb{Q}$ satisfies $\lambda^{-1}\in U\_\ell\cdot$(a set on which the $a\_j$ are already forced). In particular, if $U\_\ell=\mu\_f$, i.e. the image of $\mathrm{Fr}*\ell$ in $G/[G,G]I*\ell$ has order $f$, then $P\_\ell(\chi;T)$ is determined exactly for every $\chi$ of every degree; and if $U\_\ell\supseteq\mu\_f\cap K$, for every $\chi$ with $\mathbb{Q}(\chi)=K$.

*Proof.* $\eta$ trivial on $I$ acts on $V\_\chi^I$ as the scalar $\eta(\mathrm{Fr})$ on the Frobenius, so $\rho\_{\chi\eta}(\mathrm{Fr})|*{V^I}=\eta(\mathrm{Fr})\rho*\chi(\mathrm{Fr})|*{V^I}$, giving the twist formula; $\tau(P*\ell(\chi;uT))=\prod\_j(1-\tau(\zeta\_f^ju)T)^{a\_j}$ and $\tau(\zeta\_f^ju)=1$ iff $\zeta\_f^ju=1$, so the $(1-T)$-multiplicity in the orbit product is $[\mathbb{Q}(\chi\eta):\mathbb{Q}],a\_{j(\eta)}$. If $U\_\ell=\mu\_f$ every $a\_j$ is read off. $\square$

In Theorem 4.1's example, any $\eta$ unramified at $\ell$ with $\eta(\mathrm{Fr}*\ell)=\zeta\_5^{-4}$ distinguishes $P$ from $P'$: the twisted orbit product of $P'$ is $(1-T)^{2[\mathbb{Q}(\chi\eta):\mathbb{Q}]}$ while that of $P$ has no factor $1-T$. When $U*\ell$ is too small (small abelianization, or $\mathrm{Fr}*\ell$ falling into $[G,G]I*\ell$), the residual ambiguity is exactly the price of avoiding the Frobenius coset, and [Euler factors](https://claude.ai/chat/euler-factors.md) is the computation that pays it.

## 6. Conclusion: check versus second path

For each ramified $\ell$ the system of Proposition 2.1 over a family $\mathcal{H}$ containing the cyclic subgroups, augmented by the degrees $\dim V\_\chi^I$, integrality, and the twists of Theorem 5.1, is:

- a **second computation path**, independent of the Frobenius coset and of the class identification of $D\_\ell$'s elements, for: all rational-valued $\chi$ (any degree); all $\chi$ with $\dim V\_\chi^I\le1$ whose tame eigenvalue is $\pm1$, in particular all $\chi$ with $\mu\_f\cap\mathbb{Q}(\chi)\subseteq{\pm1}$; and all $\chi$ when $U\_\ell\supseteq\mu\_f\cap\mathbb{Q}(\chi)$;
- a **check** with residual ambiguity described by Theorem 4.1 (Galois orbit for $\dim V^I=1$; possibly non-conjugate alternatives for $\dim V^I=2$) for the others, the ambiguity being resolved only by [Euler factors](https://claude.ai/chat/euler-factors.md).

For the odd two-dimensional characters of family 3 with real-quadratic or rational coefficient field, the second path is complete at every ramified prime; with imaginary coefficient field it is complete at every $\ell$ where $f$ is prime to $w\_K/2$ or where $U\_\ell$ contains the relevant roots of unity, and [Euler factors](https://claude.ai/chat/euler-factors.md) is required at the rest.
