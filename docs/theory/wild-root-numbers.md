# The local root number at a wildly ramified prime by Brauer induction in degree zero

*One of the derivations behind* [*artin*](https://claude.ai/README.md)*; the* [*index*](https://claude.ai/chat/README.md) *lists them all and names the code that implements each.*

**Setting.** $\ell$ ramified, $D=D\_\ell$ with its filtration ([the filtration](https://claude.ai/chat/ramification-filtration.md) to [filtration uniqueness](https://claude.ai/chat/filtration-uniqueness.md)), $L=N\_{\mathfrak{L}}$, $\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)$. Conventions ($\psi\_\ell$, $\psi\_F=\psi\_\ell\circ\mathrm{Tr}*{F/\mathbb{Q}*\ell}$, self-dual measures, $\mathrm{rec}$ with uniformizers $\mapsto$ arithmetic Frobenius) as in [tame root numbers](https://claude.ai/chat/tame-root-numbers.md). For a subgroup $A\le D$ put $F\_A=L^A$, $q\_A=|k\_{F\_A}|$, $d\_A=v\_{F\_A}(\mathfrak{D}*{F\_A/\mathbb{Q}*\ell})$ (so $\psi\_{F\_A}$ has conductor $\mathfrak{p}\_{F\_A}^{-d\_A}$). $R(H)$ is the character ring of a finite group $H$ and $R\_0(H)=\ker(\deg)$. A group is *elementary* if it is a direct product of a cyclic group and a $p$-group for some prime $p$; subgroups of elementary groups are elementary; elementary groups are nilpotent, hence M-groups (every irreducible character is induced from a linear character of a subgroup).

**Target.** $\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)=\varepsilon(\rho\_\chi|*D,\psi*\ell)$. Write

$$\theta:=\mathrm{Res}*D\chi-\chi(1),\mathbf{1}D\in R\_0(D),\qquad\varepsilon\ell(\chi)=\varepsilon(\theta,\psi*\ell)\cdot\varepsilon(\mathbf{1}*{\mathbb{Q}*\ell},\psi\_\ell)^{\chi(1)}=\varepsilon(\theta,\psi\_\ell),$$

the second factor being $1$ (Lemma 2.1 below). Deligne's theorem ([the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md) Theorem 5.1(4)) gives $\varepsilon$ on $R(H)$ for every $H\le D$ (over $F\_H$) as the unique function that is additive, agrees with Tate's local constants on linear characters via $\mathrm{rec}*{F\_H}$, and is inductive in degree zero: $\varepsilon(\mathrm{Ind}A^H\vartheta,\psi{F\_H})=\varepsilon(\vartheta,\psi*{F\_A})$ for $\vartheta\in R\_0(A)$.

## 1. A degree-zero Brauer decomposition

**Theorem 1.1 (integral generation of $R\_0$).** For every finite group $H$,

$$R\_0(H)=\sum\_{A}\mathrm{Ind}*A^H\big(\mathrm{span}*\mathbb{Z}{\mu-\mathbf{1}\_A:\ \mu\in\widehat{A},\ \mu\ne1}\big),$$

the sum over elementary subgroups $A\le H$. In particular $\theta=\sum\_{(A,\mu)}c\_{A,\mu},\mathrm{Ind}\_A^D(\mu-\mathbf{1}*A)$ with $c*{A,\mu}\in\mathbb{Z}$, $A$ elementary, $\mu$ linear and nontrivial.

*Proof.* Step 1: $R\_0(H)=\sum\_E\mathrm{Ind}\_E^HR\_0(E)$ over elementary $E$. By Brauer, $R(H)=\sum\_E\mathrm{Ind}\_E^HR(E)$; given $\vartheta\in R\_0(H)$ write $\vartheta=\sum\_E\mathrm{Ind}\_E^H\vartheta\_E$ and set $d\_E=\deg\vartheta\_E$, so $\sum\_E[H\:E]d\_E=0$. Then $\vartheta=\sum\_E\mathrm{Ind}\_E^H(\vartheta\_E-d\_E\mathbf{1}\_E)+\sum\_Ed\_E\big(\mathrm{Ind}\_E^H\mathbf{1}\_E-[H\:E]\mathbf{1}*H\big)$, the first sum being of the required form. For the second, apply Brauer to the trivial character, $\mathbf{1}H=\sum\_jb\_j\mathrm{Ind}{E\_j}^H\lambda\_j$ ($E\_j$ elementary, $\lambda\_j$ linear), and the projection formula $\mathrm{Ind}E^H\mathbf{1}E\cdot\mathrm{Ind}{E\_j}^H\lambda\_j=\mathrm{Ind}{E\_j}^H\big(\lambda\_j\cdot\mathrm{Res}*{E\_j}\mathrm{Ind}\_E^H\mathbf{1}\_E\big)$:

$$\mathrm{Ind}\_E^H\mathbf{1}*E-[H\:E]\mathbf{1}H=\sum\_jb\_j,\mathrm{Ind}{E\_j}^H\Big(\lambda\_j\cdot\big(\mathrm{Res}*{E\_j}\mathrm{Ind}\_E^H\mathbf{1}*E-[H\:E]\mathbf{1}*{E\_j}\big)\Big),$$

and each inner virtual character has degree $\lambda\_j(1)\cdot([H\:E]-[H\:E])=0$. Step 2: for elementary (nilpotent) $E$, $R\_0(E)$ is generated by the $\mathrm{Ind}\_B^E(\nu-\mathbf{1}\_B)$, $B\le E$, $\nu$ linear. Let $S$ be their span; it suffices to show $\eta-\eta(1)\mathbf{1}\_E\in S$ for every $\eta\in\mathrm{Irr}(E)$, by induction on $\eta(1)$. For $\eta$ linear, $\eta-\mathbf{1}\_E\in S$ ($B=E$). For $\eta(1)=d>1$, $\eta=\mathrm{Ind}\_B^E\nu$ with $[E\:B]=d$ and $\nu$ linear (M-group), so $\eta-d\mathbf{1}\_E=\mathrm{Ind}\_B^E(\nu-\mathbf{1}\_B)+\big(\mathrm{Ind}*B^E\mathbf{1}B-d,\mathbf{1}E\big)$. Write $\mathrm{Ind}B^E\mathbf{1}B=\sum{\eta'}m{\eta'}\eta'$; then $m{\mathbf{1}}=1$ and $\sum{\eta'\ne\mathbf{1}}m*{\eta'}\eta'(1)=d-1$, so $\mathrm{Ind}\_B^E\mathbf{1}*B-d,\mathbf{1}E=\sum{\eta'\ne\mathbf{1}}m*{\eta'}\big(\eta'-\eta'(1)\mathbf{1}\_E\big)$ with every $\eta'(1)\le d-1$, which lies in $S$ by induction. Transitivity of induction ($\mathrm{Ind}\_E^H\mathrm{Ind}\_B^E=\mathrm{Ind}\_B^H$) and the fact that subgroups of elementary groups are elementary finish the proof. $\square$

**Construction 1.2 (the explicit decomposition).** Enumerate the elementary subgroups $A$ of $D$ up to conjugacy and their linear characters $\mu\ne1$ (through $A^{ab}=A/[A,A]$, computed from generators). The characters $\mathrm{Ind}*A^D(\mu-\mathbf{1}A)$ are class functions on $D$ with values in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta{\exp D}]$, computed from the class identification of $D$ (*[*Euler factors*](https://claude.ai/chat/euler-factors.md) *§4) by the induction formula; by Theorem 1.1 they span the lattice $R\_0(D)$ of rank $r\_D-1$ ($r\_D$ = number of classes of $D$). Solve $\theta=\sum c*{A,\mu}\mathrm{Ind}*A^D(\mu-\mathbf{1}A)$ for integers $c{A,\mu}$ by a Hermite normal form over $\mathbb{Z}$ on the coordinate vectors with respect to the basis of class indicators (after separating real and imaginary parts, or working in the $\mathbb{Z}$-module $R\_0(D)$ with the basis ${\eta-\eta(1)\mathbf{1}}*{\eta\in\mathrm{Irr}(D)\setminus{1}}$). Any solution serves: $\varepsilon$ does not depend on the decomposition (Deligne's uniqueness). A short decomposition is obtained by preferring, for each irreducible constituent $\eta$ of $\mathrm{Res}*D\chi$, subgroups $A$ with $\langle\eta|A,\mu\rangle=1$ and small $[D\:A]$; when $D$ itself is an M-group (e.g. supersolvable, in particular when $D$ is a $p$-group by a cyclic), every $\eta=\mathrm{Ind}A\mu$ and the decomposition is $\theta=\sum\eta\langle\chi|D,\eta\rangle\big(\mathrm{Ind}{A\eta}(\mu*\eta-\mathbf{1})+(\mathrm{Ind}*{A*\eta}\mathbf{1}-[D\:A\_\eta]\mathbf{1})\big)$ followed by the recursion of Step 2 on the permutation characters.

## 2. The $\varepsilon$-factor of each degree-one constituent

**Lemma 2.1.** For a character $\xi$ of $F^\times$ ($F/\mathbb{Q}\_\ell$ finite) with conductor exponent $a=a(\xi)$ and any $c\in F^\times$ with $v\_F(c)=a+d\_F$:

$$\varepsilon(\xi,\psi\_F)=\xi(c),q\_F^{-a/2}\sum\_{u\in(\mathcal{O}\_F/\mathfrak{p}\_F^{,a})^\times}\xi^{-1}(u),\psi\_F(u/c)\quad(a\ge1),\qquad\varepsilon(\xi,\psi\_F)=\xi(\varpi\_F)^{d\_F}\quad(a=0);$$

the right side is independent of the choice of $c$ (and, for $a=0$, of $\varpi\_F$), $|\varepsilon(\xi,\psi\_F)|=1$, and $\varepsilon(\mathbf{1}\_F,\psi\_F)=1$.

*Proof.* Tate's formula with the self-dual measure and the normalization at $s=\tfrac12$; the unramified case is the local functional equation for $f=\mathbf{1}*{\mathcal{O}F}$, $\hat f=q^{-d/2}\mathbf{1}{\mathfrak{p}^{-d}}$, giving $\xi(\varpi)^{d}$ (*[*tame root numbers*](https://claude.ai/chat/tame-root-numbers.md) *§2 for $d=0$; the general computation is identical). Independence of $c$: replacing $c$ by $cu\_0$ and substituting $u=u'u\_0$ multiplies and divides by $\xi(u\_0)$. Modulus for $a\ge1$: put $G=\sum\_u\xi^{-1}(u)\psi\_F(u/c)$; then $|G|^2=\sum*{t}\xi^{-1}(t)S\_t$ with $S\_t=\sum\_{v\in(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^a)^\times}\psi\_F(v(t-1)/c)$ (substitute $u=vt$). Since $x\mapsto\psi\_F(x/c)$ is a character of $\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^a$ nontrivial on $\mathfrak{p}^{a-1}/\mathfrak{p}^a$, summing over all of $\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^a$ and subtracting the sum over $\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}^a$ gives $S\_t=q^a[t\equiv1\bmod\mathfrak{p}^a]-q^{a-1}[t\equiv1\bmod\mathfrak{p}^{a-1}]$. Hence $|G|^2=q^a-q^{a-1}\sum\_{t\in U^{(a-1)}/U^{(a)}}\xi^{-1}(t)=q^a$, because $\xi$ is nontrivial on $U^{(a-1)}$ (conductor exactly $\mathfrak{p}^a$) and a nontrivial character sums to $0$. So $|\varepsilon|=|\xi(c)|,q^{-a/2}q^{a/2}=1$. $\square$

**Definition 2.2.** For $(A,\mu)$ in the decomposition, $\xi\_{A,\mu}:=\mu\circ\mathrm{rec}*{F\_A}$, a character of $F\_A^\times$ of finite order, with $a(\xi*{A,\mu})=f\_{F\_A}(\mu)$, the conductor exponent of $\mu$ over $F\_A$ computed from the filtration $A\_i=A\cap D\_i$ by [conductor exponents](https://claude.ai/chat/conductor-exponents.md) Theorem 2.1 ($=1+\varphi\_{L/F\_A}(c\_\mu)$, or $0$ if $\mu|*{A\cap D\_0}=1$), and $d\_A=v*{F\_A}(\mathfrak{D}*{F\_A/\mathbb{Q}*\ell})=\big(\sum\_{\sigma\in D\setminus1}i\_L(\sigma)-\sum\_{\sigma\in A\setminus1}i\_L(\sigma)\big)/e(L/F\_A)$, by transitivity of the different and Hilbert's formula ([conductor exponents](https://claude.ai/chat/conductor-exponents.md) Prop. 2.2).

**Proposition 2.3 (making $\xi\_{A,\mu}$ explicit).** Let $M=L^{\ker\mu}$, cyclic over $F\_A$ of order $m=\mathrm{ord},\mu$, $M^{\mathrm{ur}}$ its maximal unramified subextension of degree $f\_M$, and $n=a(\xi\_{A,\mu})$. Choose a uniformizer $\pi$ of $F\_A$ and let $F\_{A,\pi,n}$ be the level-$n$ Lubin to Tate extension of $F\_A$ for $\pi$ (generated by the roots of $[\pi^n](https://claude.ai/chat/X)$ for the Lubin to Tate polynomial $[\pi](https://claude.ai/chat/X)=\pi X+X^{q\_A}$), of degree $(q\_A-1)q\_A^{,n-1}$, totally ramified. Let $F\_{A,m}$ be the unramified extension of $F\_A$ of degree $m$. Then $M\subseteq F\_{A,m}F\_{A,\pi,n}$, and:

1. On $F\_{A,m}F\_{A,\pi,n}$, $\mathrm{rec}*{F\_A}(\pi)$ is the Frobenius on $F*{A,m}$ and the identity on $F\_{A,\pi,n}$, while $\mathrm{rec}*{F\_A}(u)$, $u\in\mathcal{O}*{F\_A}^\times$, is the identity on $F\_{A,m}$ and $[u^{-1}]$ (the Lubin to Tate endomorphism on torsion points) on $F\_{A,\pi,n}$; restriction to $M$ gives $\mathrm{rec}*{F\_A}$ on $M$, and $\mathrm{rec}*{F\_A}(u)|\_M$ depends only on $u\bmod U^{(n)}$.
2. Computationally: factor a defining polynomial of $M/F\_A$ over $F\_{A,m}F\_{A,\pi,n}$ (a field of degree $m(q\_A-1)q\_A^{n-1}$ over $F\_A$; the unramified degree $m$ can be replaced by the order of $\mathrm{rec}(\pi)|*M$ once known, which is a multiple of $f\_M$ dividing $m$), express a generator $y$ of $M$ as a polynomial in a torsion point $\lambda\_n$ and a root of unity, and read $\mathrm{rec}(u)(y)$ by substituting $*[*u^{-1}*](https://claude.ai/chat/%5Clambda_n)*$; identify the result with $\sigma(y)$ for $\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(M/F\_A)=A/\ker\mu$ (root identification at the precision of* [*the precision policy*](https://claude.ai/chat/precision-policy.md)*), which gives $\xi*{A,\mu}(u)=\mu(\sigma)$ for representatives $u$ of $(\mathcal{O}*{F\_A}/\mathfrak{p}^{n})^\times$, and $\xi*{A,\mu}(\pi)=\mu(\sigma\_\pi)$ with $\sigma\_\pi$ read from item 1.
3. When $\mu$ is unramified ($\mu|*{A\cap D\_0}=1$), only $\xi(\varpi)^{d\_A}=\mu(\Phi\_A)^{d\_A}$ is needed, $\Phi\_A$ the Frobenius of $L/F\_A$ modulo inertia, i.e. the coset $\phi^{f(F\_A)}D\_0\cap A$ of* [*Euler factors*](https://claude.ai/chat/euler-factors.md)*, and no Lubin to Tate computation is required. When $M/F\_A$ is tame,* [*tame root numbers*](https://claude.ai/chat/tame-root-numbers.md) *Prop. 3.1 gives $\xi*{A,\mu}$ in closed form.

*Proof.* Lubin to Tate theory (Milne, *CFT* I.3; Serre in Cassels to Fröhlich VI.3): $F\_A^{ab}=F\_A^{\mathrm{ur}}F\_{A,\pi}$, $\mathrm{rec}(\pi)|*{F*{A,\pi}}=\mathrm{id}$, $\mathrm{rec}(\pi)|*{F^{\mathrm{ur}}}=\mathrm{Frob}$, $\mathrm{rec}(u)|*{F\_{A,\pi}}=[u^{-1}]$, $\mathrm{rec}(u)|*{F^{\mathrm{ur}}}=\mathrm{id}$; the norm group of $M$ contains $U^{(n)}$ (conductor $\mathfrak{p}^n$) and $\pi^{m}$ (as $[M\:F\_A]=m$ and $F\_A^\times/N(M^\times)$ is cyclic of order $m$), so $M$ is contained in the fixed field of $\mathrm{rec}(\langle\pi^m\rangle U^{(n)})$, which is $F*{A,m}F\_{A,\pi,n}$. $\square$

## 3. Assembly by inductivity in degree zero

**Theorem 3.1.** With $\theta=\sum\_{(A,\mu)}c\_{A,\mu}\mathrm{Ind}\_A^D(\mu-\mathbf{1}\_A)$ from Construction 1.2,

$$\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)=\varepsilon(\theta,\psi\_\ell)=\prod\_{(A,\mu)}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon(\xi\_{A,\mu},\psi\_{F\_A})}{\varepsilon(\mathbf{1}*{F\_A},\psi*{F\_A})}\Big)^{c\_{A,\mu}}=\prod\_{(A,\mu)}\varepsilon(\xi\_{A,\mu},\psi\_{F\_A})^{,c\_{A,\mu}},$$

an explicit monomial in the quantities of Lemma 2.1; $|\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)|=1$; the value is independent of the decomposition chosen, of the Frobenius lifts, of the matching, and of the prime above $\ell$; and the contribution $\varepsilon(\chi(1)\mathbf{1}*D,\psi*\ell)=1$ of the subtracted trivial part is added back as the empty factor.

*Proof.* Additivity turns the sum into a product; inductivity in degree zero applies to each $\mathrm{Ind}*A^D(\mu-\mathbf{1}A)$ since $\mu-\mathbf{1}A$ has degree $0$; on the linear characters of $A$, $\varepsilon$ is Tate's constant of $\mu\circ\mathrm{rec}{F\_A}$ by local class field theory; $\varepsilon(\mathbf{1}{F\_A},\psi*{F\_A})=1$ by Lemma 2.1. Modulus one termwise. Independence: Deligne's uniqueness for the first three, [well-definedness](https://claude.ai/chat/well-definedness.md) Theorem 3.3 for the matching and the prime. $\square$

Where the tame case of [tame root numbers](https://claude.ai/chat/tame-root-numbers.md) is a special case: there $\theta$ was decomposed through the unramified fields $F\_\theta$ with induction constants equal to $1$; in general induction from a ramified $F\_A$ carries a Langlands constant $\lambda(F\_A/\mathbb{Q}*\ell,\psi*\ell)$, and the degree-zero form is precisely what makes those constants cancel, they never have to be computed.

**Checks.** (i) $|\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)|=1$; (ii) $\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)\varepsilon\_\ell(\bar\chi)=\lambda\_\chi(\mathrm{rec}*{\mathbb{Q}*\ell}(-1))$; (iii) for one-dimensional $\chi$, agreement with the $\ell$-component of the Dirichlet root number ([the archimedean place](https://claude.ai/chat/archimedean.md) to [tame root numbers](https://claude.ai/chat/tame-root-numbers.md) check 3); (iv) two different decompositions of $\theta$ give the same value; (v) $\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi^\tau)=\tau'\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)$ with $\tau'$ fixing $\zeta\_{\ell^\infty}$; (vi) the functional equation ([the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md) Lemma 5.3) with $W(\chi)=\varepsilon\_\infty(\chi)\prod\_\ell\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)$ now exactly known.

## 4. Size of the induction data

**Proposition 4.1.** Let $\Delta=|D|$, $r\_D$ the number of conjugacy classes of $D$, $s\_e(D)$ the number of conjugacy classes of elementary subgroups of $D$, and $e(\nu)=e\cdot v\_\ell(\mathrm{disc}f)$ the bound of [the filtration](https://claude.ai/chat/ramification-filtration.md) Lemma 2.3.

1. The number of pairs $(A,\mu)$ available is at most $\sum\_{A}|A^{ab}|\le s\_e(D),\Delta$, and $s\_e(D)\le$ the number of subgroups of $D$, which is at most $\Delta^{\lfloor\log\_2\Delta\rfloor}$; the number of pairs used in a decomposition can be taken $\le r\_D-1\le\Delta-1$ (a basis of $R\_0(D)$ among the generators), and, when $D$ is an M-group, $\le\sum\_\eta\langle\chi|\_D,\eta\rangle\cdot\log\_2\Delta$ by the recursion depth.
2. The coefficients $c\_{A,\mu}$ solving the system by Hermite normal form satisfy $|c\_{A,\mu}|\le(r\_D\Delta)^{r\_D}$ (Cramer/Hadamard on an integer matrix with entries bounded by $\Delta$).
3. For each pair, $[F\_A:\mathbb{Q}*\ell]=[D\:A]\le\Delta$, $q\_A\le\ell^{f}$, $a(\xi*{A,\mu})\le1+\max$ upper jump of $A\le1+e(\nu)$, $d\_A\le\delta(L)\le e(\nu)$; the Tate sum has $q\_A^{a-1}(q\_A-1)\le\ell^{f(1+e(\nu))}$ terms, reducible to $O(q\_A^{\lceil a/2\rceil})$ terms by the standard evaluation of Gauss sums of conductor $\ge2$ ($\xi(1+x)=\psi(\alpha x/c)$ on $U^{(\lceil a/2\rceil)}$ for a unit $\alpha$ determined modulo $\mathfrak{p}^{\lfloor a/2\rfloor}$, whence $\varepsilon$ is $\xi(c)\xi^{-1}(\alpha)\psi(\alpha/c)$ times a quadratic Gauss sum when $a$ is odd and $1$ when $a$ is even).
4. The Lubin to Tate computation for $(A,\mu)$ takes place in a field of degree $m(q\_A-1)q\_A^{,a-1}\le\Delta\cdot\ell^{f(1+e(\nu))}$ over $F\_A$; it is needed only for wildly ramified $\mu$, and for a given $A$ the same torsion field serves all $\mu$ of the same conductor.

*Proof.* 1: a subgroup is generated by at most $\log\_2\Delta$ elements; $R\_0(D)$ has rank $r\_D-1$; the recursion of Theorem 1.1 halves degrees at each step. 2: HNF/Cramer on the coordinate matrix. 3: [conductor exponents](https://claude.ai/chat/conductor-exponents.md) Theorem 2.1 and [the filtration](https://claude.ai/chat/ramification-filtration.md) Lemma 2.3 (upper jumps are $\le$ lower jumps); the Gauss-sum reduction is Bushnell to Henniart 23.6. 4: Proposition 2.3. $\square$

The dominant cost is the Lubin to Tate embedding for wild $\mu$ of large conductor; the polynomial dependence on $\Delta$, $\ell^{f}$ and $e(\nu)$ is what the pipeline commits to, and when it is unaffordable the numerical route of [the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md) Lemma 5.3 (a finite candidate set for $W(\chi)$, the exact tame and unramified factors reducing the wild unknown to a root of unity of bounded order, namely the order of the values of the $\xi\_{A,\mu}$ times $4$) remains available as the verification of last resort.
