# The direct route for expensive blocks

*One of the derivations behind* [*artin*](https://claude.ai/README.md)*; the* [*index*](https://claude.ai/chat/README.md) *lists them all and names the code that implements each.*

**Setting.** $\ell\nmid\mathrm{disc}f$; $\lambda\vdash n$ is the block of $\mathrm{Frob}*\ell$ (cycle type of $f\bmod\ell$), $r=r*\lambda=\mathrm{lcm}(\lambda)$ the order of that cycle type; $m=\exp G$; $t$ a base of $G$ and $m\_t$ its monomial ([separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md) §3); $(G\_i,F\_i,c\_i,m\_i)*{i=1..t}$ the certificate chain (*[*the precision policy*](https://claude.ai/chat/precision-policy.md) *§1, §4); $\mathfrak{L}\mid\ell$ a prime of $N(\zeta\_m)$ and $\lambda\_0=\mathfrak{L}\cap\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_m]$. Conventions: $(\tau P)(x)=P(x*{\tau(1)},\dots,x\_{\tau(n)})$, a left action of $S\_n$, and $g(P(\alpha))=(gP)(\alpha)$ for $g\in G$. For a class function $\theta\:G\to\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_m]$ the *orbit sum* is
$$F\_\theta=\sum\_{\sigma\in G}\theta(\sigma),\sigma!\cdot m\_t\ \in\ \mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_m][x\_1,\dots,x\_n].$$

A block is *expensive* when the subgroups that [separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md) assigns to it and the twisted resolvents that [cyclotomic refinement](https://claude.ai/chat/cyclotomic-refinement.md) assigns to it have $\sum[G\:H]$ or $\sum[G\:C]$ above a threshold $W$ (set in §5), and it is *ambiguous* when it contains more than one conjugacy class. Only ambiguous blocks need any test beyond $f\bmod\ell$; the direct route below is for those that are also expensive, and it always returns the conjugacy class, not only the rational class.

## 1. Roots in $\mathbb{F}\_{\ell^r}$ and the Frobenius permutation

**Lemma 1.1.** All roots of $f$ in $\bar{\mathbb{F}}*\ell$ lie in $\mathbb{F}*{\ell^r}$, and $r$ is the least such exponent. Listing them as $\beta\_1,\dots,\beta\_n$ (any order), the permutation $\phi\_\beta$ defined by $\beta\_i^{,\ell}=\beta\_{\phi\_\beta(i)}$ has cycle type $\lambda$, and for the labeling $\beta\_i=\bar\iota(\alpha\_{\rho(i)})$ induced by an embedding $\iota\:N\to\bar{\mathbb{Q}}*\ell$ with Frobenius element $\sigma*\ell\in G$ and a permutation $\rho\in S\_n$, one has $\phi\_\beta=\rho^{-1}\sigma\_\ell\rho$.

*Proof.* An irreducible factor of degree $d$ of $f\bmod\ell$ has its roots in $\mathbb{F}*{\ell^d}$, permuted cyclically by $x\mapsto x^\ell$; $d$ ranges over the parts of $\lambda$. For the last claim, $\beta\_i^\ell=\bar\iota(\sigma*\ell\alpha\_{\rho(i)})=\bar\iota(\alpha\_{\sigma\_\ell\rho(i)})=\beta\_{\rho^{-1}\sigma\_\ell\rho(i)}$. $\square$

*Cost.* Factor $f\bmod\ell$ ($\tilde O(n\log\ell+n^2)$ operations in $\mathbb{F}*\ell$); construct $\mathbb{F}*{\ell^r}$ by an irreducible polynomial of degree $r$ ($\tilde O(r^2\log\ell)$ expected); for each factor $g\_j$ of degree $d\_j$ find one root in $\mathbb{F}*{\ell^r}$ by equal-degree splitting ($\tilde O(d\_j,r\log\ell)$ operations in $\mathbb{F}*{\ell^r}$) and the rest as its Frobenius orbit ($d\_j-1$ exponentiations). Total $\tilde O(n r\log\ell)$ operations in $\mathbb{F}*{\ell^r}$, i.e. $\tilde O(n r^2\log\ell)$ in $\mathbb{F}*\ell$. The permutation $\phi\_\beta$ comes for free from the orbit structure.

## 2. Orbit sums at unlabeled roots are not tests

The labeling of the roots in $\mathbb{F}\_{\ell^r}$ is arbitrary: it is an $S\_n$-torsor, and the unknown $\rho$ of Lemma 1.1 ranges over a coset $\rho\_0G$ ([the precision policy](https://claude.ai/chat/precision-policy.md) §4) whose position in $S\_n/G$ is exactly the alignment problem.

**Lemma 2.1.** (a) $F\_\theta(\rho\beta)=(\rho F\_\theta)(\beta)$, and for $\rho\in S\_n$, $\rho F\_\theta=F\_\theta$ as polynomials iff $\rho\in G$ and $\theta(\rho^{-1}\sigma)=\theta(\sigma)$ for all $\sigma$; in particular for $\rho\notin G\cdot(S\_n)*t$ the polynomials $\rho F*\theta$ and $F\_\theta$ have different monomial supports and take different values at $\alpha$ after a Tschirnhaus transformation ([separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md) Lemma 3.2). (b) Any function $Q$ of the labeled roots and of $\ell$ whose value is independent of the labeling is a function of $(f\bmod\ell)$ alone, hence of the block; in particular it cannot separate two classes in the same block.

*Proof.* (a) $\rho F\_\theta=\sum\_\sigma\theta(\sigma)\rho\sigma m\_t$ has support $\rho Gt$, and $\rho Gt=Gt$ iff $\rho t\in Gt$ iff $\rho\in G(S\_n)*t$; when $\rho\in G$ the coefficients match iff $\theta\circ L*{\rho^{-1}}=\theta$. (b) Independence of labeling means $Q(\rho\beta)=Q(\beta)$ for all $\rho\in S\_n$, so $Q$ is a symmetric function of the roots, i.e. a function of the coefficients of $f\bmod\ell$. $\square$

So $F\_\theta$ evaluated at an unaligned labeling has no meaning, and no labeling-independent quantity does better than the cycle type. Every refinement inside a block uses global information: either the exact resolvents computed at $p$ ([separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md), [cyclotomic refinement](https://claude.ai/chat/cyclotomic-refinement.md): the roots reduced modulo $\lambda$ are the true $F(\tau\alpha)$, not values at $\ell$-adic labels), or the certificate values $c\_i$ through alignment. The direct route is the second.

## 3. The direct construction

**Step 1 (alignment in $\mathbb{F}\_{\ell^r}$).** Run [the precision policy](https://claude.ai/chat/precision-policy.md) Proposition 4.1 with the residues in place of $\ell$-adic approximations: start with $\pi=\mathrm{id}$; at level $i$, for the $m\_i$ coset representatives $\tau\in G\_{i-1}/G\_i$ evaluate $F\_i(\beta\_{\pi\tau})\in\mathbb{F}*{\ell^r}$ and keep the unique $\tau$ with $F\_i(\beta*{\pi\tau})=c\_i\bmod\ell$; set $\pi\leftarrow\pi\tau$. Output $\pi$.

**Step 2 (Frobenius).** $\sigma\_\beta:=\pi^{-1}\phi\_\beta\pi$.

**Step 3 (values).** For any class function $\theta$, $\theta(\mathrm{Frob}*\ell)=\theta(\sigma*\beta)$, exactly in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_m]$; the class of $\mathrm{Frob}*\ell$ is the class of $\sigma*\beta$, found by one class identification. Equivalently, in orbit-sum form,

$$\theta(\mathrm{Frob}*\ell)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum*{\sigma\in G}\theta(\sigma)\cdot#{g\in G:\ g^{-1}\sigma\_\beta g=\sigma}=\frac{|C\_G(\sigma\_\beta)|}{|G|}\sum\_{\sigma\in\sigma\_\beta^G}\theta(\sigma),$$

and if the value is wanted in $\mathbb{F}\_{\lambda\_0}$ it is reduced through $\lambda\_0$ afterwards.

**Theorem 3.1 (correctness).** Let $E=\bigcup\_{i}E\_i$, where $E\_i$ is the set of primes dividing $\mathrm{disc}\prod\_{\tau\in G\_{i-1}/G\_i}(x-F\_i(\tau\alpha))$. For $\ell\nmid\mathrm{disc}f$, $\ell\notin E$: Step 1 finds at every level exactly one passing $\tau$, the output $\pi$ is admissible ($\beta\_{\pi(i)}=\bar\iota(\alpha\_i)$ for some embedding $\iota$), and $\sigma\_\beta$ is a Frobenius element of $\ell$ in $G$; hence Step 3 is exact for every $\theta$. For $\ell\in E$, $\ell\nmid\mathrm{disc}f$, the same holds after lifting the roots to $\mathbb{Z}*{\ell^r}/\ell^{k*\ell}$ by Hensel (simple roots) and evaluating the $F\_i$ there, with $k\_\ell$ from [the precision policy](https://claude.ai/chat/precision-policy.md) §5.

*Proof.* Let $\iota\_0$ be an embedding with labeling permutation $\pi\_0$, so $\beta\_{\pi(i)}=\bar\iota\_0(\alpha\_{\rho(i)})$ with $\rho=\pi\_0^{-1}\pi$ and $F(\beta\_\pi)=\bar\iota\_0(F(\rho\alpha))$ for every integral $F$. [the precision policy](https://claude.ai/chat/precision-policy.md) Proposition 4.1 shows that, over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}*\ell$, exactly one $\tau$ at each level satisfies $F\_i(\rho\tau\alpha)=c\_i$ given $\rho\in G*{i-1}$, and that the final $\rho$ lies in $G$. Reduction modulo $\mathfrak{L}$ preserves these equalities and, for $\ell\notin E\_i$, preserves the inequalities as well: the $m\_i$ values $F\_i(\tau\alpha)$, $\tau\in G\_{i-1}/G\_i$, are the roots of an integer polynomial whose discriminant is prime to $\ell$, so they stay distinct modulo $\mathfrak{L}$ and $c\_i$ is congruent to exactly one of them. Thus the residue test picks the same $\tau$ as the exact test. With $\rho\in G$, $\iota:=\iota\_0\circ\rho$ is an embedding with $\bar\iota(\alpha\_i)=\beta\_{\pi(i)}$, and by Lemma 1.1 applied to the labeling $\beta\_\pi$ (whose Frobenius permutation is $\pi^{-1}\phi\_\beta\pi$), $\sigma\_\beta$ is the Frobenius element of the prime attached to $\iota$. The lifted variant is [the precision policy](https://claude.ai/chat/precision-policy.md) Theorem 5.1 (test T2 at precision $k\_\ell$). $\square$

By [separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md) Proposition 3.4, $|E\_i|\le m\_i(m\_i-1)\log\_2(2B\_{F\_i})$, so $E$ is a finite set fixed in advance.

**Proposition 3.2 (reduction through $\lambda\_0$).** Let $\theta$ have values in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_m]$ and let $\sigma\_1,\dots,\sigma\_s$ represent the classes of a block with $\theta(\sigma\_i)$ pairwise distinct. Then the residues $\theta(\sigma\_i)\bmod\lambda\_0$ are pairwise distinct for every prime $\ell$ not dividing $\prod\_{i\<j}N\_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_m)/\mathbb{Q}}(\theta(\sigma\_i)-\theta(\sigma\_j))$; if $\theta$ is a $\mathbb{Z}$-combination of characters with $|\theta|\le T$ on $G$, this excludes at most $\binom s2\varphi(m)\log\_2(2T)$ primes, and the residue $\theta(\sigma\_\beta)\bmod\lambda\_0$ then identifies the class of $\mathrm{Frob}\_\ell$ inside the block. Which prime $\lambda\_0$ above $\ell$ is used is immaterial provided the same one is used for all $\theta(\sigma\_i)$.

*Proof.* $\theta(\sigma\_i)-\theta(\sigma\_j)$ is a nonzero algebraic integer; it lies in $\lambda\_0$ only if $\ell$ divides its norm, which is bounded by $(2T)^{\varphi(m)}$. Galois conjugation of $\lambda\_0$ applies the same automorphism to all residues. $\square$

This is the sense in which coefficients are "reduced through a prime above $\ell$": it is an optional final reduction of exact values, useful when the downstream consumer works modulo $\lambda\_0$ (for instance the modular comparison with a weight-one form), never a substitute for the alignment. After Step 2 the class is known exactly and Proposition 3.2 is not needed for the class itself.

## 4. Cyclotomic versus rational separation

Let $\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{Q}\otimes\_\mathbb{Z}R(G)$ be the space of $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combinations of characters. These are the class functions that arise as traces of Frobenius on objects defined over $\mathbb{Q}$ (virtual representations, permutation modules, Euler characteristics of $G$-sets), and they are the $\theta$ of interest: any class function whatever can be written down, but only elements of $\mathcal{R}$ are "values of Frobenius on something".

**Proposition 4.1.** (a) Every $\theta\in\mathcal{R}$ satisfies $\theta(\sigma^k)=\sigma\_k(\theta(\sigma))$ for $\gcd(k,m)=1$, $\sigma\_k\in\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_m)/\mathbb{Q})$, $\zeta\_m\mapsto\zeta\_m^k$; conversely a class function $G\to\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_m)$ with this equivariance lies in $\mathcal{R}$.
(b) A rational-valued $\theta\in\mathcal{R}$ is constant on rational classes; no such $\theta$ separates $\sigma^G$ from $(\sigma^k)^G$.
(c) For any two distinct classes $\sigma^G\ne\sigma'^G$ there is an irreducible character $\chi$ with $\chi(\sigma)\ne\chi(\sigma')$; if $\sigma'=\sigma^k$ then necessarily $\chi(\sigma)\notin\mathbb{Q}$, and $\theta=\chi$ (values in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_{o(\sigma)}]\subseteq\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_m]$) separates the two classes. More generally, for a block $\mathcal{B}\_\lambda$ with classes $\sigma\_1,\dots,\sigma\_s$, the $\mathbb{Z}$-linear combinations of irreducible characters restricted to the block form a $\mathbb{Z}$-module whose image in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_m]^s$ has full rank $s$, so a single $\theta\in R(G)$ with pairwise distinct values on the block exists and is found by a small search over integer combinations of the characters whose columns differ on the block.

*Proof.* (a) For a character $\chi$ with representation $\rho$, $\chi(\sigma^k)$ is the sum of the $k$-th powers of the eigenvalues of $\rho(\sigma)$, i.e. $\sigma\_k(\chi(\sigma))$; extend linearly. For the converse, the equivariant class functions form a $\mathbb{Q}$-space of dimension $\sum\_{\text{rational classes}}(\text{number of classes in it})=r$, since on a rational class an equivariant function is determined by one value in the field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_{o})^{,\mathrm{Stab}}$ of the right degree; $\mathcal{R}$ has $\mathbb{Q}$-dimension $r$ and is contained in it. (b) $\theta(\sigma^k)=\sigma\_k(\theta(\sigma))=\theta(\sigma)$ when $\theta(\sigma)\in\mathbb{Q}$. (c) Columns of the character table are distinct (column orthogonality: $\sum\_\chi\chi(\sigma)\overline{\chi(\sigma')}=0$ for non-conjugate $\sigma,\sigma'$ while $\sum\_\chi|\chi(\sigma)|^2=|C\_G(\sigma)|>0$). If $\chi(\sigma)\in\mathbb{Q}$ then $\chi(\sigma^k)=\chi(\sigma)$ by (a). For the block statement, the $r\times s$ matrix of character values on the block has rank $s$ (its columns are distinct and orthogonal under the column relations), so generic integer combinations of the rows have distinct entries. $\square$

So: separating a rational class into its conjugacy classes by a Frobenius-trace $\theta$ forces $\theta$ to take irrational cyclotomic values, and such $\theta$ always exist. This is the representation-theoretic face of [rational classes](https://claude.ai/chat/rational-classes.md) Proposition 2.2 (coset actions have rational traces) and of [cyclotomic refinement](https://claude.ai/chat/cyclotomic-refinement.md) (twisted resolvents compute induced characters with cyclotomic values, but only at $\sigma^{f}$). The direct route computes $\theta(\mathrm{Frob}*\ell)$ for every $\theta$, rational or not, because it determines $\sigma*\beta$ itself.

## 5. Cost per prime, by block

Write $c\_i$ for the number of arithmetic operations of a straight-line program evaluating $F\_i$ (for the classical Stauduhar invariants of maximal subgroups of $S\_n$ this is $O(n^2)$; for the orbit-sum invariants of [separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md) §3 it is $|G\_i|\binom{k+1}2$, so those are used only inside $G$ where $|G\_i|$ is small), $A=\sum\_{i=1}^tm\_ic\_i$ for the alignment work, and let $\mathcal{H}*\lambda$, $\mathcal{T}*\lambda$ be the families [separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md) and [cyclotomic refinement](https://claude.ai/chat/cyclotomic-refinement.md) assign to the block $\lambda$, with

$$w\_\lambda=\sum\_{H\in\mathcal{H}*\lambda}[G\:H],\qquad w'*\lambda=\sum\_{(C,\chi)\in\mathcal{T}*\lambda}[G\:C],f*{o(\chi)}^{,2},\qquad f\_o=o(\ell\bmod o).$$

Operations are counted in $\mathbb{F}*\ell$; a multiplication in $\mathbb{F}*{\ell^r}$ costs $\tilde O(r)$.

| route cost per prime in block $\lambda$ output                                    |                                                         |                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| $f\bmod\ell$ only                                                                 | $\tilde O(n\log\ell+n^2)$                               | block                                                                                                                                   |
| [separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md) resolvents | $\tilde O(w\_\lambda\log\ell)$                          | rational class                                                                                                                          |
| [cyclotomic refinement](https://claude.ai/chat/cyclotomic-refinement.md) twisted  | $\tilde O(w'\_\lambda\log\ell)$                         | class of $\sigma^{f}$; exact on the residue classes of [cyclotomic refinement](https://claude.ai/chat/cyclotomic-refinement.md) Thm 5.1 |
| direct                                                                            | $\tilde O\big(n r\_\lambda^2\log\ell+r\_\lambda A\big)$ | conjugacy class, always                                                                                                                 |

**Proposition 5.1.** For $\ell\notin E$ in block $\lambda$, the direct route costs $\tilde O(nr\_\lambda^2\log\ell+r\_\lambda A)$ and returns the conjugacy class of $\mathrm{Frob}\_\ell$. Hence, with the threshold

$$W\_\lambda:=\frac{n r\_\lambda^2\log\ell+r\_\lambda A}{\log\ell},$$

the direct route is cheaper than [separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md) whenever $w\_\lambda>W\_\lambda$ and than [cyclotomic refinement](https://claude.ai/chat/cyclotomic-refinement.md) whenever $w'*\lambda>W*\lambda$, and it dominates both in output. [separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md) and [cyclotomic refinement](https://claude.ai/chat/cyclotomic-refinement.md) remain preferable exactly for blocks with $w\_\lambda\le W\_\lambda$ (and then only if the rational class is the whole answer, i.e. every rational class in the block is a single conjugacy class) or $w'*\lambda\le W*\lambda$ with $\ell$ in an exact residue class of [cyclotomic refinement](https://claude.ai/chat/cyclotomic-refinement.md).

*Proof.* Lemma 1.1 for the roots, Step 1 for the $A$ evaluations in $\mathbb{F}\_{\ell^r}$, one class identification (polynomial in $n$, independent of $\ell$) for Step 2; correctness is Theorem 3.1. The comparisons are the displayed costs. $\square$

**Scale of the quantities.** $r\_\lambda\le g(n)$, Landau's function, $\log g(n)\sim\sqrt{n\log n}$; for $n\le12$, $r\_\lambda\le60$, for $n\le20$, $r\_\lambda\le420$. The chain indices $m\_i$ are the indices of consecutive groups in a descent from $S\_n$ to $G$; with a chain through maximal subgroups they are the indices of maximal subgroups, and $A$ is a few thousand operations for $n\le12$. By contrast $w\_\lambda$ is a sum of indices $[G\:H]$ that, for blocks whose rational classes are separated only by cyclic or near-cyclic subgroups ([separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md) Lemma 1.2), is of order $(r\_\lambda'-1)|G|/r\_\lambda$ with $r'*\lambda$ the number of rational classes in the block, i.e. of order $|G|$. So for $|G|\gg n r*\lambda^2+r\_\lambda A/\log\ell$, which is the regime of the whole project, the direct route wins on every ambiguous block, and [separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md)/[cyclotomic refinement](https://claude.ai/chat/cyclotomic-refinement.md) are worth running only as a filter on blocks that they resolve with $w\_\lambda$ or $w'*\lambda$ below $W*\lambda$.

**At excluded primes.** For $\ell\in E$ (a set of at most $\sum\_im\_i(m\_i-1)\log\_2(2B\_{F\_i})$ primes), the direct route lifts the roots to precision $k\_\ell$, multiplying the cost by $\tilde O(k\_\ell)$; [separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md)/[cyclotomic refinement](https://claude.ai/chat/cyclotomic-refinement.md) at their own excluded primes pay the same factor. Since $E$ is known in advance, the per-block decision can be made per prime: direct for $\ell\notin E$, whichever of the three is cheapest at precision $k\_\ell$ for $\ell\in E$.

**Placement.** For each ambiguous block, decide once whether it is expensive ($\min(w\_\lambda,w'*\lambda)>W*\lambda$, with $\log\ell$ replaced by $\log X$ for a uniform decision). In the loop over $\ell\le X$: factor $f\bmod\ell$; if the block is unambiguous, done; if it is cheap, run [separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md) (and [cyclotomic refinement](https://claude.ai/chat/cyclotomic-refinement.md) when $\ell$ is in an exact residue class) and stop when the class is determined; otherwise, or when [separating subgroups](https://claude.ai/chat/separating-subgroups.md)/[cyclotomic refinement](https://claude.ai/chat/cyclotomic-refinement.md) leave a rational class with several conjugacy classes, run the direct route. The direct route is also the only one that never needs the second-coordinate bookkeeping of [cyclotomic refinement](https://claude.ai/chat/cyclotomic-refinement.md), because it determines $\sigma\_\beta\in G$ rather than a class function of a power of it.
