# Artin conductor exponents and the conductor to discriminant acceptance test

**Setting.** $\ell$ ramified in $N$; $D=D\_\ell\le G$ with lower filtration $D\_{-1}=D\supseteq D\_0=I\supseteq D\_1\supseteq\cdots$ in the global numbering ([matching](https://claude.ai/chat/matching.md) to [filtration uniqueness](https://claude.ai/chat/filtration-uniqueness.md)), $f=[D\:D\_0]$, $e=|D\_0|$, $L=N\_{\mathfrak{L}}$ the completion, $i\_L$ the function of [the filtration](https://claude.ai/chat/ramification-filtration.md), $\delta(L)=\sum\_{\sigma\ne1}i\_L(\sigma)=\sum\_{i\ge0}(|D\_i|-1)=v\_L(\mathfrak{D}*{L/\mathbb{Q}*\ell})$. Characters $\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)$ from [the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md); for a class function $\chi$ of $G$ and a subgroup $D\_i$, $\langle\chi|*{D\_i},1\rangle=\frac{1}{|D\_i|}\sum*{\sigma\in D\_i}\chi(\sigma)$, which for a character is $\dim V^{D\_i}$.

## 1. The Artin character and the exponent formula

**Definition (Artin character of $D$).** $a\_D(\sigma)=-f,i\_L(\sigma)$ for $\sigma\ne1$, and $a\_D(1)=f,\delta(L)$, so that $\sum\_{\sigma\in D}a\_D(\sigma)=0$. The Artin conductor exponent of a class function $\chi$ of $G$ at $\ell$ is $f\_\ell(\chi):=\langle a\_D,\chi|*D\rangle\_D=\frac1{|D|}\sum*{\sigma\in D}a\_D(\sigma)\chi(\sigma)$, and $\mathfrak{f}(\chi)=\prod\_{\ell}\ell^{f\_\ell(\chi)}$ over the ramified primes of [the ramified primes](https://claude.ai/chat/ramified-primes.md) (for unramified $\ell$, $D\_0=1$, $a\_D=0$).

**Proposition 1.1.** Let $u\_i$ be the augmentation character of $D\_i$ ($u\_i=r\_{D\_i}-1\_{D\_i}$, $r$ the regular character) and $u\_i^*=\mathrm{Ind}{D\_i}^Du\_i$. Then
$$a\_D=\sum{i\ge0}\frac{|D\_i|}{|D\_0|},u\_i^*,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad f\_\ell(\chi)=\sum\_{i\ge0}\frac{|D\_i|}{|D\_0|}\Big(\chi(1)-\frac{1}{|D\_i|}\sum\_{\sigma\in D\_i}\chi(\sigma)\Big)=\frac{1}{|D\_0|}\sum\_{\sigma\in D\_0\setminus1}i\_L(\sigma)\big(\chi(1)-\chi(\sigma)\big).$$

*Proof.* Since $D\_i\trianglelefteq D$, $u\_i^*(\sigma)=\frac{1}{|D\_i|}\sum\_{g\in D,\ g\sigma g^{-1}\in D\_i}u\_i(g\sigma g^{-1})$ equals $-[D\:D\_i],[\sigma\in D\_i]$ for $\sigma\ne1$ and $*[*D\:D\_i*](https://claude.ai/chat/%7CD_i%7C-1)*$ for $\sigma=1$. Therefore, for $\sigma\ne1$, $\sum\_i\frac{|D\_i|}{|D\_0|}u\_i^*(\sigma)=-\frac{|D|}{|D\_0|}#{i\ge0:\sigma\in D\_i}=-f,i\_L(\sigma)=a\_D(\sigma)$, and at $\sigma=1$ the sum is $\frac{|D|}{|D\_0|}\sum\_i(|D\_i|-1)=f\delta(L)=a\_D(1)$. Frobenius reciprocity gives $\langle u\_i^\*,\chi\rangle\_D=\langle u\_i,\chi|*{D\_i}\rangle*{D\_i}=\chi(1)-\langle\chi|*{D\_i},1\rangle$, which is the first displayed formula. Expanding $\frac{|D\_i|}{|D\_0|}\big(\chi(1)-\frac1{|D\_i|}\sum*{\sigma\in D\_i}\chi(\sigma)\big)=\frac{1}{|D\_0|}\sum\_{\sigma\in D\_i}(\chi(1)-\chi(\sigma))$ and summing over $i$ counts each $\sigma\in D\_0$ exactly $i\_L(\sigma)$ times, the term $\sigma=1$ contributing $0$. $\square$

The first form is the one quoted; the second is what is computed. Writing $f\_\ell(\chi)=\mathrm{codim}V^{D\_0}+\sum\_{i\ge1}\frac{|D\_i|}{|D\_0|}\mathrm{codim}V^{D\_i}$ separates the tame part $\mathrm{codim}V^{I}$ from the Swan conductor $\mathrm{sw}*\ell(\chi)=\sum*{i\ge1}\frac{|D\_i|}{|D\_0|}\mathrm{codim}V^{D\_i}$, which vanishes iff $D\_1$ acts trivially on $V$.

**Computation from the table.** With the classes $C\_1,\dots,C\_r$ of $G$ and the class identification of [the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md), set $N\_k:=\sum\_{\sigma\in D\_0\cap C\_k,\ \sigma\ne1}i\_L(\sigma)$ for $k=1,\dots,r$ ($|D\_0|-1$ class identifications per ramified prime, once). Then for every $\chi$,

$$f\_\ell(\chi)=\frac{1}{|D\_0|}\Big(\chi(1),\delta(L)-\sum\_{k}N\_k,\chi(g\_k)\Big),$$

a single row-vector product with the character table. The values $i\_L(\sigma)$ are those of [the filtration](https://claude.ai/chat/ramification-filtration.md) §2 or [filtration uniqueness](https://claude.ai/chat/filtration-uniqueness.md) §2, transported to the global numbering by [matching](https://claude.ai/chat/matching.md); $f\_\ell(\chi)$ is independent of the matching and of the prime above $\ell$ ([well-definedness](https://claude.ai/chat/well-definedness.md) Theorem 3.3), Galois-equivariant in $\chi$ and equal for $\chi$ and $\bar\chi$ ([the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md) Theorem 5.1(5); the sums $\sum\_{\sigma\in D\_i}\chi(\sigma)$ are nonnegative integers).

## 2. Integrality

**Theorem 2.1 (Hasse to Arf, representation-theoretic form).** Let $H=\mathrm{Gal}(L/K')$ for a subfield $K'\subseteq L$, with lower filtration $H\_i=H\cap D\_i$ (Serre IV §1 Prop. 2) and Herbrand function $\varphi\_{L/K'}$. For a one-dimensional character $\psi$ of $H$ with $\psi|*{H\_0}\ne1$ let $c=\max{i:\psi|*{H\_i}\ne1}\ge0$ (if $\psi|*{H\_0}=1$ then $f*{K'}(\psi)=0$). Then

$$f\_{K'}(\psi)=1+\varphi\_{L/K'}(c),$$

and $\varphi\_{L/K'}(c)$ is an upper jump of the abelian extension $L^{\ker\psi}/K'$; by Hasse to Arf it is an integer, so $f\_{K'}(\psi)\in\mathbb{Z}$. Conversely, integrality of $f\_{K'}(\psi)$ for all one-dimensional $\psi$ of all $H$ is equivalent to Hasse to Arf for all abelian subextensions.

*Proof.* Proposition 1.1 for $H$: $f\_{K'}(\psi)=\sum\_{i\ge0}\frac{|H\_i|}{|H\_0|}(1-\langle\psi|*{H\_i},1\rangle)$, and $\langle\psi|*{H\_i},1\rangle$ is $1$ if $\psi|*{H\_i}=1$ and $0$ otherwise (a nontrivial character of a group sums to $0$). So $f*{K'}(\psi)=\sum\_{i=0}^{c}\frac{|H\_i|}{|H\_0|}=1+\frac{1}{|H\_0|}\sum\_{i=1}^{c}|H\_i|=1+\varphi\_{L/K'}(c)$ by the explicit formula of [the filtration](https://claude.ai/chat/ramification-filtration.md) §4. For the jump: $\ker\psi\supseteq H\_{c+1}$ and $\ker\psi\not\supseteq H\_c$; the upper filtration of $H/\ker\psi$ is $H^v\ker\psi/\ker\psi$ with $H^v=H\_{\psi\_{L/K'}(v)}$; at $v=\varphi(c)$ this is $H\_c\ker\psi/\ker\psi\ne1$, while for $v>\varphi(c)$, $\psi\_{L/K'}(v)>c$ and $H^v\subseteq H\_{c+1}\subseteq\ker\psi$. Hasse to Arf (Serre V §7 Thm 1) says upper jumps of abelian extensions are integers. The converse: every upper jump $v$ of an abelian $H/N$ is $\varphi(c)$ for some $\psi$ trivial on $N$ (choose $\psi$ with $H\_c\not\subseteq\ker\psi\supseteq H\_{c+1}N$, possible because $H/\ker\psi$ ranges over all cyclic quotients). $\square$

**Proposition 2.2 (induction).** For $H=\mathrm{Gal}(L/K')\le D$, $\psi$ a class function of $H$:
$$f\_\ell(\mathrm{Ind}*H^D\psi)=f*{K'/\mathbb{Q}*\ell}\cdot f*{K'}(\psi)+v\_\ell(\mathfrak{d}*{K'/\mathbb{Q}*\ell}),\psi(1).$$

*Proof.* $\mathrm{Res}*H,a\_D=f*{K'/\mathbb{Q}*\ell},a\_H+v*\ell(\mathfrak{d}*{K'/\mathbb{Q}*\ell}),r\_H$: for $\sigma\in H\setminus1$ both sides are $-f\_{L/\mathbb{Q}*\ell}i\_L(\sigma)$ since $f*{L/\mathbb{Q}*\ell}=f*{L/K'}f\_{K'/\mathbb{Q}*\ell}$ and the $i$-function of $L/K'$ is the restriction of $i\_L$; at $\sigma=1$, $a\_D(1)-f*{K'/\mathbb{Q}*\ell}a\_H(1)=f*{L/\mathbb{Q}*\ell}\big(\delta*{L/\mathbb{Q}*\ell}-\delta*{L/K'}\big)=f\_{L/\mathbb{Q}*\ell},v\_L(\mathfrak{D}*{K'/\mathbb{Q}*\ell})=f*{L/\mathbb{Q}*\ell}e*{L/K'}v\_{K'}(\mathfrak{D}*{K'/\mathbb{Q}*\ell})=|H|,f\_{K'/\mathbb{Q}*\ell}v*{K'}(\mathfrak{D}*{K'/\mathbb{Q}*\ell})=|H|,v\_\ell(\mathfrak{d}*{K'/\mathbb{Q}*\ell})$, using transitivity of the different and $\mathfrak{d}=N\_{K'/\mathbb{Q}\_\ell}\mathfrak{D}$. Then Frobenius reciprocity: $\langle a\_D,\mathrm{Ind}\psi\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Res},a\_D,\psi\rangle\_H$. $\square$

**Theorem 2.3 (Artin).** $f\_\ell(\chi)\in\mathbb{Z}\_{\ge0}$ for every character $\chi$ of $G$.

*Proof.* Nonnegativity: each term $\frac{|D\_i|}{|D\_0|}\mathrm{codim}V^{D\_i}$ is $\ge0$. Integrality: by Brauer's induction theorem, $\chi|*D=\sum\_jn\_j,\mathrm{Ind}*{H\_j}^D\psi\_j$ with $n\_j\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $\psi\_j$ one-dimensional characters of subgroups $H\_j\le D$; $f\_\ell$ is additive, so $f\_\ell(\chi)=\sum\_jn\_j\big(f\_{K'*j/\mathbb{Q}*\ell}f\_{K'*j}(\psi\_j)+v*\ell(\mathfrak{d}*{K'j/\mathbb{Q}\ell})\big)$ by Proposition 2.2, and each $f*{K'\_j}(\psi\_j)$ is an integer by Theorem 2.1 (or $0$ for $\psi\_j=1$). $\square$

The integrality of the computed $f\_\ell(\chi)$ is therefore a consequence of the computed filtration satisfying Hasse to Arf on all abelian subquotients ([filtration uniqueness](https://claude.ai/chat/filtration-uniqueness.md) Check 1); a non-integral $f\_\ell(\chi)$ for some $\chi$ is a certificate that Check 1 fails somewhere, and conversely integrality for all $\chi$ is a weaker, cheaper necessary condition tested on the output.

## 3. Independent computation of $v\_\ell(d\_N)$

**Proposition 3.1.** Let $g=[G\:D]$ be the number of primes of $N$ above $\ell$. Then
$$v\_\ell(d\_N)=g,f,\delta(L)=\frac{|G|}{|D\_0|}\sum\_{i\ge0}\big(|D\_i|-1\big).$$

*Proof.* $d\_N=N\_{N/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{D}*{N/\mathbb{Q}})$ and $v*\ell(d\_N)=\sum\_{\mathfrak{L}\mid\ell}f(\mathfrak{L}/\ell),v\_{\mathfrak{L}}(\mathfrak{D}*{N/\mathbb{Q}})$; the different localizes, $v*{\mathfrak{L}}(\mathfrak{D}*{N/\mathbb{Q}})=v\_L(\mathfrak{D}*{L/\mathbb{Q}\_\ell})=\delta(L)$, and the $g$ primes above $\ell$ are conjugate, each with residue degree $f$. Hilbert's formula gives the sum over the filtration; $gf=|G|/|D\_0|$. $\square$

**Two independent evaluations of $\delta(L)$.** (i) From the filtration: $\sum\_{i\ge0}(|D\_i|-1)$, i.e. from the values $i\_L(\sigma)$ of [the filtration](https://claude.ai/chat/ramification-filtration.md) §2 / [filtration uniqueness](https://claude.ai/chat/filtration-uniqueness.md) §2. (ii) From the different directly: $\delta(L)=v\_L\big(m'(\varpi\_L)\big)$ where $m$ is the minimal polynomial of the uniformizer $\varpi\_L$ over $L^{\mathrm{ur}}$ ($\mathcal{O}*L=\mathcal{O}*{L^{\mathrm{ur}}}[\varpi\_L]$, so $\mathfrak{D}*{L/L^{\mathrm{ur}}}=(m'(\varpi\_L))$, and $\mathfrak{D}*{L^{\mathrm{ur}}/\mathbb{Q}\_\ell}=1$). Computation (ii) uses only $\varpi\_L$, the tower arithmetic of [local Galois groups](https://claude.ai/chat/local-galois-groups.md) and the norm/minimal polynomial over $L^{\mathrm{ur}}$ (a product over $D\_0$ of the conjugates $\sigma\varpi\_L$, or a characteristic polynomial of multiplication by $\varpi\_L$ on $L$ as an $L^{\mathrm{ur}}$-space), not the values $i\_L(\sigma)$; it is exact at precision $\nu+1$ because $\delta(L)\le e\nu$ ([the filtration](https://claude.ai/chat/ramification-filtration.md) Lemma 2.3). Equality of (i) and (ii) is Hilbert's formula and is the first component of the acceptance test.

## 4. The conductor to discriminant identity as acceptance condition

**Theorem 4.1.** $\displaystyle\sum\_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}\chi(1),f\_\ell(\chi)=v\_\ell(d\_N)$ for every prime $\ell$.

*Proof.* $\sum\_\chi\chi(1)\chi=r\_G$, and $r\_G|*D=[G\:D],r\_D$ (the regular character of $G$ restricted to $D$ is $[G\:D]$ copies of the regular character of $D$). By linearity, $\sum*\chi\chi(1)f\_\ell(\chi)=[G\:D]\langle a\_D,r\_D\rangle\_D=[G\:D],a\_D(1)=g,f,\delta(L)=v\_\ell(d\_N)$ by Proposition 3.1. $\square$

**Acceptance condition (mandatory, one equation per ramified prime).** For each ramified $\ell$ of [the ramified primes](https://claude.ai/chat/ramified-primes.md), compute

$$\Sigma\_\ell:=\sum\_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}\chi(1),f\_\ell(\chi)\quad\text{from §1, and}\quad V\_\ell:=[G\:D]\cdot f\cdot v\_L\big(m'(\varpi\_L)\big)\quad\text{from §3(ii)},$$

and require $\Sigma\_\ell=V\_\ell$. What the equation tests: through §1 it involves the character table (via $\sum\_\chi\chi(1)\chi=r\_G$, i.e. the orthogonality relations), the class identification of the elements of $D\_0$, the transport of the filtration to $G$, and the values $i\_L$; through §3(ii) it involves the tower, the uniformizer and the different independently of $i\_L$. Since $\Sigma\_\ell=\frac{|G|}{|D\_0|}\sum\_{\sigma\in D\_0\setminus1}i\_L(\sigma)$ by Proposition 1.1 summed against $r\_G$, the equation is exactly Hilbert's formula $\sum\_{\sigma\ne1}i\_L(\sigma)=v\_L(\mathfrak{D}*{L/\mathbb{Q}*\ell})$ evaluated with the two sides computed by different means, multiplied by the correct global factor $[G\:D]f$, and passed through the table. Failure localizes the error: if the two evaluations of $\delta(L)$ in §3 agree but $\Sigma\_\ell\ne V\_\ell$, the table or the class identification is wrong; if they disagree, the filtration ([the filtration](https://claude.ai/chat/ramification-filtration.md)/[filtration uniqueness](https://claude.ai/chat/filtration-uniqueness.md)) is wrong.

**Complementary identities (also enforced).**

1. For every global irreducible factor $f\_j$ with root $\beta$, stabilizer $H\_j=\mathrm{Stab}*G(\beta)$ and field $K\_j=\mathbb{Q}(\beta)=N^{H\_j}$: $f*\ell(\mathrm{Ind}*{H\_j}^G1)=v*\ell(\mathrm{disc},\mathcal{O}*{K\_j})$, the right side being the discriminant exponent of* [*the ramified primes*](https://claude.ai/chat/ramified-primes.md) *(the sum, over the $\ell$-adic factors $g$ of $f\_j$, of $v*\ell(\mathrm{disc},\mathcal{O}*{\mathbb{Q}*\ell[x]/g})$ from the $\ell$-maximal orders, computed before any Galois computation). Proof: by Mackey, $\mathrm{Res}*D\mathrm{Ind}*{H\_j}^G1=\sum\_{O}\mathrm{Ind}*{D*{\beta\_O}}^D1$ over the $D$-orbits $O$ on the roots of $f\_j$, i.e. over the primes of $K\_j$ above $\ell$, and Proposition 2.2 with $\psi=1$ gives $f\_\ell(\mathrm{Ind}*{D*{\beta\_O}}^D1)=v\_\ell(\mathfrak{d}*{\mathbb{Q}*\ell(\beta\_O)/\mathbb{Q}*\ell})$; summing over the local factors gives $v*\ell(\mathrm{disc},\mathcal{O}\_{K\_j})$. One equation per global factor and per prime, independent of the character table.
2. $\sum\_{\ell}v\_\ell(d\_N)\log\ell$ is the logarithm of $|d\_N|$, and $|d\_N|=\prod\_\chi\mathfrak{f}(\chi)^{\chi(1)}$ is the global conductor to discriminant formula; per prime it is Theorem 4.1, so no further global check is available without $d\_N$, which is never computed globally.
3. $f\_\ell(\chi)\in\mathbb{Z}*{\ge0}$ for all $\chi$ (Theorem 2.3), and $f*\ell(\chi)=0$ iff $\chi|*{D\_0}=\chi(1)\cdot1$; $f*\ell(\chi)\ge\mathrm{codim}V^{D\_0}$ with equality iff $D\_1$ acts trivially on $V$.

## 5. Cost and placement

Per ramified prime: $|D\_0|-1$ class identifications and the vector $(N\_k)*k$; then $r$ multiplications per character, $O(r^2)$ in all, in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_e]$ (the result is a rational integer; the computation may be done modulo a large prime and lifted, or exactly). The different $v\_L(m'(\varpi\_L))$ costs one characteristic polynomial of degree $e$ over $L^{\mathrm{ur}}$ at precision $\nu+1$, $\tilde O(e^{\omega},\mathrm{M}(L^{\mathrm{ur}}))$, dominated by* [*local Galois groups*](https://claude.ai/chat/local-galois-groups.md)*. The outputs are the exponents $f*\ell(\chi)$ for all $\chi$ and all ramified $\ell$, hence the conductors $\mathfrak{f}(\chi)$, the Swan conductors, and the certified values $v\_\ell(d\_N)$; these feed the Euler factors and the functional equations ([the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md) §5) and, for one-dimensional $\chi$, must agree with the conductors of the Dirichlet characters $\chi\_\psi$ used in [cyclotomic refinement](https://claude.ai/chat/cyclotomic-refinement.md) §1, which is a further check on the abelian part.
