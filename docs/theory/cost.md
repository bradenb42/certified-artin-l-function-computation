# Total cost

**Notation.** $n=\deg f$, $\Delta=|\mathrm{disc}f|$, $\nu\_\ell=v\_\ell(\Delta)$, $r$ = number of classes of $G$, $r\_\mathbb{Q}$ = number of rational classes, $\Delta\_\ell=|D\_\ell|$, $e\_\ell,f\_\ell$ the ramification index and residue degree at $\ell$, $g(n)$ = Landau's function (largest order of an element of $S\_n$; $g(12)=60$, $g(20)=420$), $A\_{\rm eff}$ = the alignment work of [matching](matching.md) §3 with the structural filter ($\sum\_i\mathrm{fix}*{G*{i-1}/G\_i}(D),c\_i$; without the filter $\sum\_im\_ic\_i$), $m\_{\max}=\max\_{H\in\mathcal{H}}[G\:H]$ the largest resolvent degree used, $B\_{\max}=\max\_{F\in\mathcal{F}}B\_F$, $\mathfrak{f}*{\max}=\max*\chi\mathfrak{f}(\chi)$. Costs are in bit operations, $\tilde O$ hides polylogarithmic factors in all parameters.

Two remarks on the entries. First, the precision $k\_p$ carries the exponent $\mu(m)=m(m-1)$ of [the precision policy](precision-policy.md) only because of the Krasner-stability requirement (T5) at excluded primes and the pairwise comparisons (T3); if root counts at excluded primes are lifted only there and comparisons are made against the recorded integers $c\_i$ (T2), the exponent is $m$, and the resolvent term becomes $\tilde O(m\_{\max}^2\log B\_{\max})$ instead of $\tilde O(m\_{\max}^3\log B\_{\max})$; the estimates below use the refined policy. Second, P3's per-prime cost depends on the block of $\ell$; by Chebotarev the expected cost per prime is $\sum\_\lambda\frac{|\mathcal{B}*\lambda|}{|G|},\mathrm{cost}(\lambda)$, and for the direct route it is bounded by $\tilde O(n,g(n)^2\log X+g(n)A*{\rm eff})$ uniformly.

## 2. The dominant term

**Proposition 2.1.** Up to polylogarithmic factors,

$$T\_{\rm total}\ \asymp\ \underbrace{|G|^2n^2+m\_{\max}^2\log B\_{\max}}*{P1}\ +\ \underbrace{\sum*{\ell\mid\Delta}\Big(\Delta\_\ell^2\nu\_\ell+k\_\ell\Delta\_\ell A\_{\rm eff}+e\_\ell\ell^{f\_\ell}+\Lambda\_{\rm wild}(\ell)\Big)}*{P2}\ +\ \underbrace{\pi(X)\big(n,g(n)^2+g(n)A*{\rm eff}\big)}*{P3}\ +\ \underbrace{r,X}*{P4},$$

where $\Lambda\_{\rm wild}(\ell)=\mathrm{poly}(\Delta\_\ell,\ell^{f\_\ell(1+e\_\ell\nu\_\ell)})$ if $\ell$ is wildly ramified and $0$ otherwise, and $X\ge\sqrt{\mathfrak{f}*{\max}},(\log\tfrac1\epsilon)^{\chi*{\max}(1)/2}$ is forced by P4. With $m\_{\max}\le|G|$, $\Delta\_\ell\le|G|$, $A\_{\rm eff}\le|G|$, $\nu\_\ell\le\log\_\ell\Delta$ and $\pi(X)\le X$, the dominant term is

$$\max\Big{,|G|^2,n^2\log B\_{\max},\quad X\cdot n,g(n)^2,\quad\max\_{\ell\ \mathrm{wild}}\Lambda\_{\rm wild}(\ell),\Big},$$

the three regimes being: large group with irrational classes (P1: the table and the separating resolvents), large conductor (P3: the prime loop to $X\sim\sqrt{\mathfrak{f}*{\max}}$, since $\pi(X)\cdot n g(n)^2\gg rX$ whenever $ng(n)^2>r\log X$, i.e. always, as $r\le|G|$ enters P4 only linearly), and wild ramification with high conductor exponent (P2: the Lubin to Tate embeddings, exponential in $f*\ell e\_\ell\nu\_\ell$).

*Proof.* Sum of the table entries with the stated bounds; P2's non-wild part is $O(\Delta\_\ell^2\nu\_\ell)$ per prime and there are $\le\log\_2\Delta$ ramified primes, so it is dominated by $|G|^2\log\Delta$, absorbed in the first term; P4 is dominated by P3. $\square$

Which regime applies is decided by the input: for $G$ with a rational character table (e.g. $G=S\_n$) no separating resolvents are needed, $m\_{\max}$ is the largest index among the point stabilizers of the factors, and P1 collapses to the class computations; for $G$ with many irrational classes and $|G|$ large, P1 dominates unless $\mathfrak{f}*{\max}$ is sufficiently large; wild primes with $e*\ell\nu\_\ell$ large are the only source of exponential cost and are capped by using the light mode of [the verifier](verifier.md) §3 (numerical determination of the wild root numbers up to the finite candidate set), which removes $\Lambda\_{\rm wild}$ with the weaker guarantee stated there.

## 3. Feasible inputs at three budgets

Budgets in bit operations: $\mathsf{B}\_1=10^{13}$ (one core-hour at $\sim3\cdot10^9$/s), $\mathsf{B}\_2=10^{16}$ (about a hundred core-days), $\mathsf{B}\_3=10^{19}$ (a cluster-month, $10^5$ core-days). Constants hidden in $\tilde O$ are taken as $10^2$ to $10^3$; the figures are orders of magnitude, each obtained by setting one regime's term equal to the budget with the others subdominant.

For the project's target ($n=12$, $[N:\mathbb{Q}]=|G|$ far beyond splitting-field construction):

- Groups with rational tables of any order occurring in degree $12$ are within $\mathsf{B}*2$ in P1; their cost is then P3, i.e. the conductor range: $\mathfrak{f}*{\max}\lesssim10^{10}$ at $\mathsf{B}\_1$, $10^{16}$ at $\mathsf{B}\_2$, $10^{22}$ at $\mathsf{B}\_3$.
- Groups with irrational classes are limited by P1 to $|G|\lesssim10^5$ at $\mathsf{B}\_2$ when a full separating family is required; when the direct route of [the direct route](direct-route.md) is used for every ambiguous block (so that no separating resolvent beyond the descent chain is needed), P1 reduces to the table and $|G|\lesssim10^{7}$ to $10^8$ becomes feasible at $\mathsf{B}*2$, which covers every transitive group of degree $12$; the cost shifts to P3 through $A*{\rm eff}$, which then must be kept small by the structural filter of [matching](matching.md).
- Wild ramification is feasible exactly when $f\_\ell(1+e\_\ell\nu\_\ell)$ is at most a few tens; beyond that the light mode replaces the exact wild root number by the numerical decision of [the functional-equation test](functional-equation.md) among the finitely many candidates, and the certificate says so.
- The verification step never dominates: at $\tilde O(X)$ per character it is at most $r\cdot X\le10^{3}\cdot X$, below P3 for every budget.

The three budgets thus correspond to: $\mathsf{B}\_1$, a single polynomial of degree $\le12$ with $|G|\lesssim10^7$ (rational table) or $\lesssim3\cdot10^3$ (irrational, full resolvent families), conductors to $10^{10}$, tame or mildly wild ramification; $\mathsf{B}\_2$, tables of Artin representations over all transitive groups of degree $\le12$ with conductors to $10^{16}$; $\mathsf{B}\_3$, degree $12$ to $16$ with conductors to $10^{22}$ and the largest irrational-class groups admitting full resolvent families.
