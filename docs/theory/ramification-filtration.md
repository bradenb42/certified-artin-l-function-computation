# The ramification filtration of $L=\mathrm{Split}(f/\mathbb{Q}\_\ell)$

**Setting.** $L$ and $D=\mathrm{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}*\ell)\le S\_n$ (in the $\ell$-adic numbering) from* [*local Galois groups*](local-galois-groups.md)*, with the tower representation of $L$; $\nu=v*\ell(\mathrm{disc}f)$; $v\_L$ the normalized valuation of $L$ ($v\_L(\varpi\_L)=1$), $e=e(L)$, $f=f(L)$, $k\_L=\mathbb{F}*{\ell^f}$. For $\sigma\in D$ put $i\_L(\sigma)=\min*{x\in\mathcal{O}*L}v\_L(\sigma x-x)\in\mathbb{Z}*{\ge0}\cup{\infty}$ and, for $i\ge-1$, $D\_i={\sigma:\ i\_L(\sigma)\ge i+1}$ (lower numbering; $D\_{-1}=D$). For a subgroup $H\le D$ and $K'=L^H$, $\mathcal{O}*{K'}=\mathbb{Z}*\ell[y]$ for some $y$ (local rings of integers are monogenic). Everything is transported to the global numbering by [matching](matching.md) to [well-definedness](well-definedness.md) and is independent of choices up to $G$-conjugacy ([well-definedness](well-definedness.md) Theorem 3.3).

## 1. Inertia, Frobenius, wild inertia

**Proposition 1.1 (inertia from the maximal unramified subextension).** $L^{\mathrm{ur}}:=L\cap\mathbb{Q}*\ell^{\mathrm{ur}}=\mathbb{Q}*{\ell^{f}}$ with $f=\mathrm{lcm}\_jf(L\_j)$, and $I:=D\_0=\mathrm{Gal}(L/L^{\mathrm{ur}})$. Let $\omega\in\mathcal{O}*L$ be the Teichmüller representative of a generator of $k\_L$ over $\mathbb{F}*\ell$. Then

$$I={\sigma\in D:\ \sigma\omega=\omega},\qquad \Phi={\sigma\in D:\ \sigma\omega=\omega^{\ell}}\ \text{is the Frobenius coset},$$

$|I|=e$, $[D\:I]=f$, and $I\trianglelefteq D$ with $D/I$ cyclic generated by $\Phi$.

*Proof.* $D\_0$ is the kernel of $D\to\mathrm{Gal}(k\_L/\mathbb{F}*\ell)$; $\sigma$ acts trivially on $k\_L$ iff it fixes the residue of $\omega$; Teichmüller representatives are permuted by $\sigma$ and are the unique roots of unity of order prime to $\ell$ lifting their residues, so $\sigma\omega\equiv\omega\bmod\varpi\_L$ iff $\sigma\omega=\omega$, and likewise for $\omega^\ell$. The compositum of unramified extensions is unramified and $\mathbb{Q}*{\ell^{a}}\mathbb{Q}*{\ell^{b}}=\mathbb{Q}*{\ell^{\mathrm{lcm}(a,b)}}$, giving $f$. $\square$

Computationally $\omega$ is obtained in the tower as $\lim x^{\ell^{fk}}$ from any $x$ whose residue generates $k\_L$ (or from a generator of the residue field of the last tower step), and $\sigma\omega$ from the action of $\sigma$ on the tower generators ([local Galois groups](local-galois-groups.md) Proposition 2.2).

**Proposition 1.2 (the wild subgroup).** $D\_1$ is an $\ell$-group, $D\_0/D\_1$ is cyclic of order prime to $\ell$, hence $D\_1$ is the unique $\ell$-Sylow subgroup of $D\_0$ and is normal in $D$. Explicitly
$$D\_1={\sigma\in I:\ \sigma\varpi\_L/\varpi\_L\equiv1\bmod\varpi\_L},\qquad\text{and } D\_i={\sigma\in I:\ \sigma\varpi\_L/\varpi\_L\equiv1\bmod\varpi\_L^{,i}}\ (i\ge1).$$

*Proof.* Since $L/L^{\mathrm{ur}}$ is totally ramified, $\mathcal{O}*L=\mathcal{O}*{L^{\mathrm{ur}}}[\varpi\_L]$, so for $\sigma\in I$, $i\_L(\sigma)=v\_L(\sigma\varpi\_L-\varpi\_L)$ (Lemma 2.1 below). Hence $D\_i={\sigma\in I:\ v\_L(\sigma\varpi\_L/\varpi\_L-1)\ge i}$ for $i\ge1$. The map $\theta\_0\:D\_0\to k\_L^\times$, $\sigma\mapsto\sigma\varpi\_L/\varpi\_L\bmod\varpi\_L$, is a homomorphism independent of the choice of uniformizer (a change $\varpi'=u\varpi$ multiplies the value by $\sigma u/u\equiv1$ as $\sigma\in I$ fixes residues) with kernel $D\_1$; so $D\_0/D\_1\hookrightarrow k\_L^\times$ is cyclic of order prime to $\ell$. For $i\ge1$, $\theta\_i\:D\_i\to\varpi\_L^{,i}\mathcal{O}*L/\varpi\_L^{,i+1}\mathcal{O}L\cong k\_L^+$, $\sigma\mapsto\sigma\varpi\_L/\varpi\_L-1$, is a homomorphism with kernel $D{i+1}$; so $D\_i/D*{i+1}$ is an elementary abelian $\ell$-group and $D\_1$ is an $\ell$-group. A normal $\ell$-subgroup with quotient of order prime to $\ell$ is the unique $\ell$-Sylow; normality in $D$ follows from $i\_L$ being conjugation invariant. $\square$

## 2. The lower filtration from a uniformizer

**Lemma 2.1 (Serre, IV §1 Lemma 1).** If $\mathcal{O}\_L=\mathcal{O}*K[x]$ for a subfield $K=L^{H}$ and $\sigma\in H$, then $\min*{z\in\mathcal{O}\_L}v\_L(\sigma z-z)=v\_L(\sigma x-x)$.

*Proof.* $z=P(x)$ with $P\in\mathcal{O}\_K[X]$ and $\sigma z-z=P(\sigma x)-P(x)$ is divisible by $\sigma x-x$. $\square$

**Proposition 2.2 (computation).** Let $\varpi\_L$ be a uniformizer of $L$ (from the last tower step of [local Galois groups](local-galois-groups.md), or as an element of minimal positive valuation among products of the tower generators). Then for $\sigma\in D$:

$$i\_L(\sigma)=\begin{cases}0&\sigma\notin I,\ v\_L(\sigma\varpi\_L-\varpi\_L)&\sigma\in I,\end{cases}\qquad D\_i={\sigma\in I:\ v\_L(\sigma\varpi\_L-\varpi\_L)\ge i+1}.$$

$i\_L$ is constant on $D$-conjugacy classes, so it is evaluated once per class of $D$ contained in $I$; the $D\_i$ are unions of such classes, and $D\_i=1$ for $i\ge\max\_{\sigma\ne1}i\_L(\sigma)$.

*Proof.* For $\sigma\notin I$ some residue moves, so $i\_L(\sigma)=0$; for $\sigma\in I$ apply Lemma 2.1 to $K=L^{\mathrm{ur}}$, $x=\varpi\_L$. Conjugation invariance: $i\_L(\tau\sigma\tau^{-1})=\min\_zv\_L(\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}z-z)=\min\_{z'}v\_L(\tau(\sigma z'-z'))=i\_L(\sigma)$ with $z'=\tau^{-1}z$. $\square$

**Lemma 2.3 (precision).** Let $\delta(L)=v\_L(\mathfrak{D}*{L/\mathbb{Q}*\ell})=\sum\_{\sigma\ne1}i\_L(\sigma)=\sum\_{i\ge0}(|D\_i|-1)$. Then $\delta(L)\le e,\nu$, hence every finite value $i\_L(\sigma)$ and the largest lower break $b\_{\max}=\max{i\:D\_i\ne1}$ satisfy $i\_L(\sigma)\le b\_{\max}+1\le\delta(L)\le e\nu$. Consequently $\varpi\_L$ and $\sigma\varpi\_L$ at $\ell$-adic precision $\nu+1$ (i.e. modulo $\varpi\_L^{e(\nu+1)}$) determine every $i\_L(\sigma)$ exactly, in agreement with [local Galois groups](local-galois-groups.md) Proposition 1.1.

*Proof.* The two expressions for $\delta(L)$ are Hilbert's formula (Serre IV §1 Prop. 4). Write $L=\mathbb{Q}*\ell(\beta*{i\_1},\dots,\beta\_{i\_k})$ with distinct roots adjoined one at a time, $K\_s=K\_{s-1}(\beta\_{i\_s})$, and let $h\_s$ be the minimal polynomial of $\beta\_{i\_s}$ over $K\_{s-1}$, a divisor of $f$ in $K\_{s-1}[x]$, $f=h\_sq\_s$. Then $\mathfrak{D}*{K\_s/K*{s-1}}\mid h\_s'(\beta\_{i\_s})$ and $f'(\beta\_{i\_s})=h\_s'(\beta\_{i\_s})q\_s(\beta\_{i\_s})$ with $q\_s(\beta\_{i\_s})$ integral, so $v\_L(\mathfrak{D}*{K\_s/K*{s-1}})\le v\_L(f'(\beta\_{i\_s}))=e,v(f'(\beta\_{i\_s}))$ (valuation normalized by $v(\ell)=1$). Transitivity of the different gives $\delta(L)=\sum\_sv\_L(\mathfrak{D}*{K\_s/K*{s-1}})\le e\sum\_sv(f'(\beta\_{i\_s}))\le e\sum\_{i=1}^nv(f'(\beta\_i))=e,v(\mathrm{disc}f)=e\nu$, since $\mathrm{disc}f=\pm\prod\_if'(\beta\_i)$ and each factor has $v\ge0$. For $\sigma\ne1$ in $I$, $\sigma\in D\_{i\_L(\sigma)-1}$, so $i\_L(\sigma)-1\le b\_{\max}$, and every $D\_i$ with $i\le b\_{\max}$ contributes at least $1$ to $\sum(|D\_i|-1)$. $\square$

## 3. Ramification polygons of factors and composita

**Lemma 3.1 (coset sums).** Let $H\le D$, $K'=L^H$, $\mathcal{O}*{K'}=\mathbb{Z}*\ell[y]$. For every $\sigma\in D$,
$$v\_L(\sigma y-y)=\sum\_{\tau\in\sigma H}i\_L(\tau)\qquad(\text{with }v\_L(0)=\infty\text{ when }\sigma\in H).$$

*Proof.* Choose $x$ with $\mathcal{O}*L=\mathbb{Z}*\ell[x]$, so $i\_L(\tau)=v\_L(\tau x-x)$ for all $\tau$ (Lemma 2.1). Let $F\in\mathcal{O}*{K'}[X]$ be the minimal polynomial of $x$ over $K'$; its roots are $hx$, $h\in H$. For $\sigma\in D$ let $F^\sigma$ be $F$ with $\sigma$ applied to its coefficients; its roots are $\sigma(hx)=\tau x$, $\tau\in\sigma H$. Then $F^\sigma(x)=\prod*{\tau\in\sigma H}(x-\tau x)$ has valuation $\sum\_{\tau\in\sigma H}i\_L(\tau)$. On the other hand $F^\sigma(x)=F^\sigma(x)-F(x)$, and each coefficient $a$ of $F$ lies in $\mathbb{Z}*\ell[y]$, so $\sigma a-a$ is divisible by $\sigma y-y$; hence $v\_L(F^\sigma(x))\ge v\_L(\sigma y-y)$. This proves $v\_L(\sigma y-y)\le\sum*{\tau\in\sigma H}i\_L(\tau)$ for every $\sigma$. Now sum over a set $R$ of representatives of the cosets $\sigma H\ne H$: the minimal polynomial $m$ of $y$ over $\mathbb{Q}*\ell$ has roots $\sigma y$, $\sigma\in R\cup{1}$, so $v\_L(\mathfrak{D}*{K'/\mathbb{Q}*\ell})=v\_L(m'(y))=\sum*{\sigma\in R}v\_L(y-\sigma y)$; and by transitivity of the different and Hilbert's formula for $L/\mathbb{Q}*\ell$ and $L/K'$, $v\_L(\mathfrak{D}*{K'/\mathbb{Q}*\ell})=\sum*{\tau\ne1}i\_L(\tau)-\sum\_{\tau\in H\setminus1}i\_L(\tau)=\sum\_{\sigma\in R}\sum\_{\tau\in\sigma H}i\_L(\tau)$. The termwise inequalities sum to an equality, so each is an equality. $\square$

Normality of $H$ is not used; for normal $H$ the formula is Serre's $e',i\_{D/H}(\bar\sigma)=\sum\_{\tau\mapsto\bar\sigma}i\_L(\tau)$.

**Definition (ramification polygon).** For $K'=L^H$ with $\mathcal{O}*{K'}=\mathbb{Z}*\ell[y]$ and minimal polynomial $m$ of $y$ of degree $[D\:H]$, the *ramification polygon* $\mathcal{P}*{K'}$ is the Newton polygon, with respect to $v\_L$, of $m(Y+y)/Y=\prod*{\sigma H\ne H}\big(Y-(\sigma y-y)\big)$. By Lemma 3.1 its slopes are the negatives of the numbers $J\_{K'}(\sigma):=\sum\_{\tau\in\sigma H}i\_L(\tau)$, $\sigma H\ne H$, each with multiplicity equal to the number of cosets attaining it; equivalently $\mathcal{P}*{K'}$ is the lower convex hull of the points $\big(k,\sum*{m>k}J\_{(m)}\big)$, $0\le k<[D\:H]$, where $J\_{(1)}\ge J\_{(2)}\ge\cdots$ are the values $J\_{K'}(\sigma)$ in decreasing order. For a *factor* $g\_j$ with root $\beta$, $K'=\mathbb{Q}*\ell(\beta)$, $H=H*\beta=\mathrm{Stab}*D(\beta)$, and $y$ is a generator of the $\ell$-maximal order of $\mathbb{Z}*\ell[\beta]$ ([the ramified primes](ramified-primes.md) §2; e.g. $y=\varpi\_{K'}+\omega\_{K'}$). When $K'/\mathbb{Q}*\ell$ is totally ramified and $y=\beta$ is a uniformizer this is the polygon of Greve to Pauli, whose ramification polynomial is $g\_j(\beta Y+\beta)/(\beta^{d\_j}Y)$, differing from ours by the change of variable $Y\mapsto\beta Y$ and the scaling $v\_L=e(L/K')v*{K'}$. The *residual polynomial* of a segment of slope $-J$ is $\prod\_{\sigma:,J\_{K'}(\sigma)=J}\big(Z-\overline{(\sigma y-y)/\varpi\_L^{,J}}\big)\in k\_L[Z]$; with $L$ explicit its roots are computed directly, and for $H=1$, $J=i$ they are the images $\theta\_{i-1}(\sigma)$ of Proposition 1.2 (for $i\ge2$) or $\theta\_0(\sigma)-1$ (for $i=1$).

**Proposition 3.2 (what a factor polygon carries).** $\mathcal{P}*{\mathbb{Q}*\ell(\beta)}$ is the pushforward of $i\_L$ to $D/H\_\beta$: it determines and is determined by the function $\sigma H\_\beta\mapsto J\_\beta(\sigma)=\sum\_{\tau\in\sigma H\_\beta}i\_L(\tau)$. It determines $v\_L(\mathfrak{D}*{\mathbb{Q}*\ell(\beta)/\mathbb{Q}*\ell})=\sum*{\sigma H\_\beta\ne H\_\beta}J\_\beta(\sigma)$, which must agree with the discriminant exponent of [the ramified primes](ramified-primes.md) through $v\_\ell(\mathrm{disc},\mathcal{O}*{\mathbb{Q}*\ell(\beta)})=f(\mathbb{Q}*\ell(\beta))\cdot v\_L(\mathfrak{D})/e(L/\mathbb{Q}*\ell(\beta))$. It determines the filtration $(D\_i)$ exactly when $H\_\beta=1$, i.e. when $\beta$ generates $L$; when $H\_\beta\ne1$ the coset sums do not determine the individual values $i\_L(\tau)$ for $\tau\in\sigma H\_\beta$, and two filtrations with the same pushforward are not distinguished.

*Proof.* Lemma 3.1 and the definition; the different of $\mathbb{Q}*\ell(\beta)/\mathbb{Q}*\ell$ is generated by $m'(y)$, $m$ the minimal polynomial of $y$, whose roots are the $\sigma y$ over the cosets $\sigma H\_\beta$; if $H\_\beta=1$ the cosets are the elements. $\square$

The last remark is why the factor polygons are not, by themselves, the filtration, and why the filtration is computed from $L$ (Proposition 2.2) with the polygons as certificates.

**Proposition 3.3 (compositum polygon).** For roots $\beta,\beta'$ let $K''=\mathbb{Q}*\ell(\beta,\beta')$, $H''=H*\beta\cap H\_{\beta'}$, and $y''$ a generator of $\mathcal{O}\_{K''}$. Then

$$J\_{K''}(\sigma)=\sum\_{\tau\in\sigma(H\_\beta\cap H\_{\beta'})}i\_L(\tau)\ \le\ \min\big(J\_{\mathbb{Q}*\ell(\beta)}(\sigma),,J*{\mathbb{Q}\_\ell(\beta')}(\sigma)\big),$$

so the compositum polygon is derived from the factor data together with the group $D$ and its filtration: its slopes are the sums of $i\_L$ over the cosets of the intersection of the two stabilizers, which [local Galois groups](local-galois-groups.md) provides as subgroups of $D$. The pairwise polygon equals the polygon of $L$ (i.e. gives $i\_L$ itself) iff $H\_\beta\cap H\_{\beta'}=1$, i.e. iff $\beta,\beta'$ generate $L$ over $\mathbb{Q}*\ell$; in general a set of roots $\beta*{i\_1},\dots,\beta\_{i\_k}$ with $\bigcap\_sH\_{\beta\_{i\_s}}=1$ exists with $k\le\log\_2|D|$ (choose each next root outside the fixed field of the current intersection), and the polygon of their compositum is the polygon of $L$, with slopes $-i\_L(\sigma)$, $\sigma\ne1$.

The compositum polygon is *not* a function of the two factor polygons alone: the pushforwards of $i\_L$ to $D/H\_\beta$ and $D/H\_{\beta'}$ do not determine the pushforward to $D/(H\_\beta\cap H\_{\beta'})$. This is visible already for $D$ abelian, $|D|=\ell^2$, $H\_\beta,H\_{\beta'}$ two distinct subgroups of order $\ell$ and $H''=1$: the two coset sums are the sums of $i\_L$ over the two families of parallel lines in $\mathbb{F}*\ell^2$, and a class function on $\ell^2$ points is not determined by its $2\ell$ line sums; such filtrations occur (over a ramified base field, a $C*\ell\times C\_\ell$-extension whose upper filtration passes through a third line, so that the compositum has an upper break shared by neither subfield).

*Proof.* Lemma 3.1 with $H=H''$; the inequality because $\sigma H''\subseteq\sigma H\_\beta$ and all terms are $\ge0$. $H''=1$ iff no nontrivial element fixes both roots. The generating-set bound is the length of a strictly decreasing chain of subgroups. $\square$

So, in this pipeline, the polygons serve three purposes: (i) the factor polygons certify the local invariants $(e\_j,f\_j)$ and the per-factor filtrations $D\_iH\_\beta$ against [the ramified primes](ramified-primes.md); (ii) the polygon of a generating compositum is an independent computation of $i\_L$ (from the minimal polynomial of $y''$ rather than from $\varpi\_L$), and its agreement with Proposition 2.2 is a certificate; (iii) the residual polynomials certify the maps $\theta\_i$, hence the structure of the graded pieces $D\_i/D\_{i+1}$.

## 4. Upper numbering

**Definition (Herbrand function).** $\varphi\_L(u)=\int\_0^u\frac{dt}{[D\_0\:D\_t]}$ for $u\ge-1$, with $D\_t=D\_{\lceil t\rceil}$ for real $t$ and $\varphi\_L(u)=u$ on $[-1,0]$. Explicitly, for $m\le u\le m+1$, $m\ge0$ an integer,

$$\varphi\_L(u)=\frac{1}{|D\_0|}\Big(|D\_1|+|D\_2|+\dots+|D\_m|+(u-m),|D\_{m+1}|\Big).$$

$\varphi\_L$ is continuous, piecewise linear, increasing, concave, with $\varphi\_L(0)=0$; its inverse is $\psi\_L$. The upper filtration is $D^v:=D\_{\psi\_L(v)}$.

**Proposition 4.1.** (a) The lower breaks are the integers $b$ with $D\_b\ne D\_{b+1}$; the upper breaks are $\varphi\_L(b)=\frac{1}{|D\_0|}\sum\_{k=1}^{b}|D\_k|$, rational numbers with denominator dividing $|D\_0|$, and $D^{v}=D\_b$ for $\varphi\_L(b-1)\<v\le\varphi\_L(b)$. (b) The largest upper break is $\varphi\_L(b\_{\max})$. The conductor exponent of a class function $\chi$ ([the character table](character-table.md) §5) equals $\sum\_{i\ge0}[D\_0\:D\_i]^{-1},\mathrm{codim},V^{D\_i}$ and can be written as $\int\_{-1}^{\infty}\mathrm{codim},V^{D^v},dv$; both expressions use only the orders $|D\_i|$ and the restrictions $\chi|*{D\_i}$. (c) Upper numbering is compatible with quotients: for $N\trianglelefteq D$, $(D/N)^v=D^vN/N$; in particular for each factor $g\_j$, $\mathrm{Gal}(L\_j/\mathbb{Q}*\ell)^v=D^vN\_j/N\_j$ with $N\_j=\mathrm{Gal}(L/L\_j)$, and for abelian subquotients the upper breaks are integers (Hasse to Arf).

*Proof.* (a) $\varphi\_L$ is linear on $[b,b+1]$ with slope $|D\_{b+1}|/|D\_0|$, and $D\_t$ is constant on $(b-1,b]$; $D^v=D\_{\psi(v)}$ with $\psi$ inverse. (b) [the character table](character-table.md) §5 and the change of variables $v=\varphi\_L(u)$, $dv=du/[D\_0\:D\_u]$. (c) Herbrand's theorem (Serre IV §3 Prop. 14), and Hasse to Arf (Serre V §7). $\square$

**Theorem 4.2 (the conversion is determined by the computed data).** The data computed in §1 to §2 are the function $i\_L\:D\to\mathbb{Z}\cup{\infty}$, equivalently the chain $D=D\_{-1}\supseteq D\_0\supseteq D\_1\supseteq\cdots$ with its orders, together with the Frobenius coset $\Phi$. From these alone:

1. $\varphi\_L$ and $\psi\_L$ are determined (they depend only on the orders $|D\_i|$); the upper filtration $D^v$, its breaks $\varphi\_L(b)$, and $\delta(L)$ are determined.
2. For every subgroup $N\trianglelefteq D$ the upper filtration of $D/N$ is determined, as $D^vN/N$; for every subgroup $H\le D$ the lower filtration of $\mathrm{Gal}(L/L^H)=H$ is determined, as $H\cap D\_i$ (lower numbering is compatible with subgroups, Serre IV §1 Prop. 2), and its Herbrand function $\varphi\_{L/L^H}$ hence its upper filtration too.
3. The conversion commutes with the transport to the global numbering: if $\tau$ is a matching ([well-definedness](well-definedness.md)), the lower and upper filtrations of $D\_\ell=\tau D\tau^{-1}$ are $\tau D\_i\tau^{-1}$ and $\tau D^v\tau^{-1}$ with the same $\varphi$, and the upper breaks, $\delta(L)$, and all conductor exponents are independent of the matching and of the prime above $\ell$.
4. The following identities are certificates of the computation, each checkable from the data: $\sum\_{\sigma\ne1}i\_L(\sigma)=v\_L(\mathfrak{D}*{L/\mathbb{Q}*\ell})$ with the right side computed independently as $v\_L(m'(\varpi\_L))$ for the minimal polynomial $m$ of $\varpi\_L$ over $L^{\mathrm{ur}}$; the polygon of a generating compositum has slopes $-i\_L(\sigma)$; for each root $\beta$, the factor polygon computed from the minimal polynomial of a generator of $\mathcal{O}*{\mathbb{Q}*\ell(\beta)}$ has slopes $-\sum\_{\tau\in\sigma H\_\beta}i\_L(\tau)$ (Lemma 3.1), and its different exponent matches [the ramified primes](ramified-primes.md); $D\_1$ is an $\ell$-group and $D\_0/D\_1$ cyclic of order prime to $\ell$ dividing $\ell^f-1$; and the upper breaks of every abelian subquotient $D\_a/D\_b$ with $D\_b\trianglelefteq D\_a$ are integers.

*Proof.* 1. The formula for $\varphi\_L$ involves only $|D\_i|$; $D^v=D\_{\psi\_L(v)}$ and $\delta(L)=\sum(|D\_i|-1)$. 2. Serre IV §1 Prop. 2 and §3 Prop. 14. 3. $i\_L$ is transported by conjugation ($i\_{\tau D\tau^{-1}}(\tau\sigma\tau^{-1})=i\_L(\sigma)$ under the identification of [matching](matching.md) Lemma 1.1, the valuation on $\iota(L)\subseteq\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\_\ell$ being the same), so the chain and its orders are transported, and $\varphi$ is unchanged; [well-definedness](well-definedness.md) Theorem 3.3 gives independence. 4. Hilbert's formula, the Definition of §3 with $H=1$ and Lemma 3.1, Proposition 4.1(c), Proposition 1.2, Hasse to Arf. $\square$

## 5. Cost

With $|D|=\Delta$, $r\_I$ the number of $D$-conjugacy classes inside $I$, and arithmetic in $L$ at $\ell$-adic precision $\nu+1$ costing $\mathrm{M}=\tilde O(\Delta,\nu\log\ell)$: Proposition 1.1 costs $\Delta$ evaluations $\sigma\omega$ (or $O(\log\Delta)$ on generators plus group closure), $\tilde O(\Delta,\mathrm{M})$; Proposition 2.2 costs $r\_I$ evaluations of $\sigma\varpi\_L$ and valuations, $\tilde O(r\_I,\mathrm{M})$, after which every $D\_i$ is a union of classes and the whole chain is read off in $O(r\_I\log r\_I)$; the Herbrand function and the upper filtration cost $O(b\_{\max})\le O(e\nu)$ arithmetic operations on rationals. The optional certificates of Theorem 4.2(4) cost one minimal-polynomial computation of degree $e$ over $L^{\mathrm{ur}}$ for the different, and one polygon (a product of $[D\:H'']-1$ linear factors and a convex hull) per compositum used. All of this is dominated by the alignment of [matching](matching.md), and the precision $\nu+1$ is the one already used to construct $L$ in [local Galois groups](local-galois-groups.md), so no re-lifting is needed.
