# Frobenius at unramified primes: what coset actions determine

**Setting.** $\ell\nmid\mathrm{disc}f$, so $\ell$ is unramified in $N$ ([the ramified primes](https://claude.ai/chat/ramified-primes.md), Cor. 1.5) and the decomposition group at any $\mathfrak{L}\mid\ell$ is cyclic, generated by a Frobenius element $\phi$; its conjugacy class $\mathrm{Frob}*\ell=\phi^G$ is well defined. For $H\le G$ let $\pi\_H=\mathrm{fix}*{G/H}$ be the permutation character of $G$ on the left cosets $G/H$: $\pi\_H(\sigma)=#{gH:\sigma gH=gH}$. Throughout, $e=\exp(G)$ and $o(\sigma)$ is the order of $\sigma$.

## 1. Factorization types are fixed-point counts

**Lemma 1.1 (fixed-point formula).** For $\sigma\in G$ and $H\le G$,
$$\pi\_H(\sigma)=\frac{|C\_G(\sigma)|\cdot|\sigma^G\cap H|}{|H|}=[G\:H],\frac{|\sigma^G\cap H|}{|\sigma^G|}.$$

*Proof.* $\sigma gH=gH$ iff $g^{-1}\sigma g\in H$. The map $G\to\sigma^G$, $g\mapsto g^{-1}\sigma g$, is surjective with fibres the cosets of $C\_G(\sigma)$, so $#{g\in G\:g^{-1}\sigma g\in H}=|C\_G(\sigma)|\cdot|\sigma^G\cap H|$; divide by $|H|$ to count cosets, and use $|C\_G(\sigma)|=|G|/|\sigma^G|$. $\square$

Hence $\pi\_H$ is computed from the class list of [the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md) by classifying the elements of $H$ ($|H|$ class identifications) and tabulating $|C\_k\cap H|$.

**Proposition 1.2 (resolvents at unramified $\ell$).** Let $F$ be an $H$-invariant with squarefree resolvent $\mathrm{Res}*{F,H}(x)=\prod*{\tau\in G/H}(x-F(\tau\alpha))\in\mathbb{Z}[x]$.

1. The number of roots of $\mathrm{Res}*{F,H}$ in $\mathbb{Q}*\ell$ is $\pi\_H(\mathrm{Frob}*\ell)$; more generally the multiset of degrees of the irreducible factors of $\mathrm{Res}*{F,H}$ over $\mathbb{Q}*\ell$ is the cycle type of $\mathrm{Frob}*\ell$ acting on $G/H$, so the number of factors of degree $j$ is $\frac1j\sum\_{d\mid j}\mu(j/d),\pi\_H(\phi^{d})$.
2. If moreover $\ell\nmid\mathrm{disc},\mathrm{Res}*{F,H}$, the same statements hold for the factorization of $\mathrm{Res}*{F,H}$ modulo $\ell$; in particular the number of degree-one factors modulo $\ell$ is $\pi\_H(\mathrm{Frob}\_\ell)$.
3. For $H=G\_1$ the point stabilizer of the degree-$n$ action and $\mathrm{Res}=f$, item 2 is the classical statement: the factorization type of $f$ modulo $\ell$ is the cycle type of $\mathrm{Frob}\_\ell$ in $S\_n$.

*Proof.* Fix $\iota\:N\to\bar{\mathbb{Q}}*\ell$ and $\phi\in G$ the Frobenius of the corresponding prime. The roots $\iota F(\tau\alpha)$ are distinct (squarefreeness) and lie in $\iota(N)\subseteq\mathbb{Q}*\ell^{\mathrm{ur}}$, and $\phi$ acts on them by $F(\tau\alpha)\mapsto F(\phi\tau\alpha)$, i.e. by left multiplication on $G/H$. Over $\mathbb{Q}*\ell$ the irreducible factors of a polynomial with roots in $\mathbb{Q}*\ell^{\mathrm{ur}}$ correspond to the orbits of the Frobenius generator of $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}*\ell^{\mathrm{ur}}/\mathbb{Q}*\ell)$ on the roots, with degree equal to orbit length. This gives 1, with the Möbius count of $j$-cycles from fixed points of powers. If $\ell\nmid\mathrm{disc},\mathrm{Res}$, reduction modulo $\ell$ is injective on the roots and $\mathbb{Z}*\ell^{\mathrm{ur}}$-factors reduce to $\mathbb{F}*\ell$-factors of the same degrees, giving 2. Item 3 is the case $\mathrm{Res}=f$, $\ell\nmid\mathrm{disc}f$. $\square$

When $\ell\mid\mathrm{disc},\mathrm{Res}*{F,H}$ but $\ell\nmid\mathrm{disc}f$, item 1 still holds and the count of $\mathbb{Q}*\ell$-roots is obtained at the precision of [the precision policy](https://claude.ai/chat/precision-policy.md) (test T5), not from the reduction modulo $\ell$.

So the data available from coset actions at $\ell$ are the values $\pi\_H(\phi^{d})$, $d\ge1$, for whichever $H$ one attaches resolvents to. The question is what these values determine about $\phi^G$.

## 2. Rational classes

**Definition.** $\sigma,\tau\in G$ are *rationally conjugate*, $\sigma\sim\_\mathbb{Q}\tau$, if $\tau$ is conjugate to $\sigma^k$ for some $k$ with $\gcd(k,o(\sigma))=1$. The equivalence classes are the *rational classes* of $G$.

**Lemma 2.1.** (a) $\sigma\sim\_\mathbb{Q}\tau$ iff $\langle\sigma\rangle$ and $\langle\tau\rangle$ are conjugate subgroups. (b) The rational class of $\sigma$ is a union of $\varphi(o(\sigma))/[N\_G(\langle\sigma\rangle)\:C\_G(\sigma)]$ conjugacy classes. (c) The rational classes are the orbits of $(\mathbb{Z}/e)^\times$ acting on the set of conjugacy classes by $k\cdot\sigma^G=(\sigma^k)^G$; this action is well defined.

*Proof.* (a) The generators of a cyclic group of order $o$ are exactly the powers $\sigma^k$ with $\gcd(k,o)=1$; so if $\langle\tau\rangle=g\langle\sigma\rangle g^{-1}$ then $\tau=g\sigma^kg^{-1}$ with $\gcd(k,o)=1$, and conversely. (b) Let $C=\langle\sigma\rangle$. The conjugacy classes inside the rational class of $\sigma$ are the classes of the generators of $C$, and two generators $\sigma^k,\sigma^{k'}$ are conjugate iff $\sigma^{k'}=g\sigma^kg^{-1}$ for some $g$, which forces $g\in N\_G(C)$. So the classes correspond to the orbits of $N\_G(C)$ on the $\varphi(o)$ generators, i.e. to the cosets of the image of $N\_G(C)\to\mathrm{Aut}(C)=(\mathbb{Z}/o)^\times$, whose kernel is $C\_G(\sigma)$. (c) $(g\sigma g^{-1})^k=g\sigma^kg^{-1}$, so the action is well defined, and $(\mathbb{Z}/e)^\times\to(\mathbb{Z}/o(\sigma))^\times$ is surjective, so the orbit of $\sigma^G$ is ${(\sigma^k)^G:\gcd(k,o(\sigma))=1}$. $\square$

**Computing the partition.** From the power maps of [the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md) §2.2: the rational class of $C\_k$ is ${C\_{k^{(t)}}\:t\in(\mathbb{Z}/o\_k)^\times}$, so the partition is a union to find over the pairs $(k,k^{(t)})$, cost $\sum\_k\varphi(o\_k)$ table lookups. The number of rational classes equals the number of conjugacy classes of cyclic subgroups (Lemma 2.1(a)), and also the number of orbits of $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_e)/\mathbb{Q})$ on $\mathrm{Irr}(G)$ from [the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md) §2.3 (Brauer's permutation lemma: each $\sigma\_t$ acting on the rows and columns of the character table via $\chi^{\sigma\_t}(g)=\chi(g^t)$ fixes equally many rows as columns; Burnside's counting formula applied to the whole group $(\mathbb{Z}/e)^\times$, which need not be cyclic, then gives equally many orbits on each), which is a consistency check between the two computations.

**Rational class functions.** Let $\mathrm{Cl}*\mathbb{Q}(G)$ be the $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space of functions $G\to\mathbb{Q}$ constant on rational classes; $\dim\mathrm{Cl}*\mathbb{Q}(G)$ is the number of rational classes.

**Proposition 2.2.** For every $H\le G$, $\pi\_H\in\mathrm{Cl}*\mathbb{Q}(G)$: it takes values in $\mathbb{Z}$ and is constant on rational classes. Consequently, for $\gcd(k,o(\sigma))=1$, no collection of coset actions, and no collection of factorization types of resolvents at $\ell$, distinguishes $\mathrm{Frob}*\ell=\sigma^G$ from $\mathrm{Frob}\_\ell=(\sigma^k)^G$.

*Proof.* $\pi\_H(\sigma)$ is a count of fixed points, an integer. $\sigma$ and $\sigma^k$ generate the same cyclic group, hence have the same fixed points on every $G$-set; and $\pi\_H$ is a class function. For the consequence: by Proposition 1.2 the factorization type of $\mathrm{Res}\_{F,H}$ at $\ell$ is the function $d\mapsto\pi\_H(\phi^d)$; if $\phi'=\phi^k$ with $\gcd(k,o(\phi))=1$ then $\phi'^d$ and $\phi^d$ generate the same subgroup for every $d$ (since $\gcd(k,o(\phi^d))=1$), so all these functions agree. $\square$

The same conclusion holds for any rational-valued character $\chi$ (values $\chi(\sigma^k)=\sigma\_k(\chi(\sigma))=\chi(\sigma)$ for $\sigma\_k\in\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_e)/\mathbb{Q})$ fixing $\mathbb{Q}$), so in particular for every character of a permutation representation, of which $\pi\_H$ is the transitive case.

## 3. Artin induction: cyclic coset actions span the rational class functions

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a set of representatives of the conjugacy classes of cyclic subgroups of $G$, and for $C\in\mathcal{C}$ let $\theta\_C\in\mathrm{Cl}*\mathbb{Q}(G)$ be the indicator of the rational class of a generator of $C$ (the set of elements generating a conjugate of $C$). By Lemma 2.1(a) the $\theta\_C$, $C\in\mathcal{C}$, form a basis of $\mathrm{Cl}*\mathbb{Q}(G)$.

**Theorem 3.1 (Artin induction, permutation form).** The permutation characters $\pi\_C=\mathrm{Ind}*C^G1\_C$, $C\in\mathcal{C}$, form a basis of $\mathrm{Cl}*\mathbb{Q}(G)$. More precisely, ordering $\mathcal{C}$ by nondecreasing order $|C|$,

$$\pi\_C=\sum\_{C'\in\mathcal{C}}b\_{C,C'},\theta\_{C'}\quad\text{with}\quad b\_{C,C'}=0\text{ unless }C'\text{ is conjugate to a subgroup of }C,\qquad b\_{C,C}=[N\_G(C)\:C]\ge1,$$

so the matrix $(b\_{C,C'})$ is triangular with nonzero diagonal, and its inverse expresses each $\theta\_{C'}$ as a $\mathbb{Q}$-combination of the $\pi\_C$.

*Proof.* $\pi\_C\in\mathrm{Cl}*\mathbb{Q}(G)$ by Proposition 2.2, so $\pi\_C=\sum*{C'}b\_{C,C'}\theta\_{C'}$ with $b\_{C,C'}=\pi\_C(\tau)$ for any generator $\tau$ of $C'$. By Lemma 1.1, $\pi\_C(\tau)\ne0$ requires $\tau^G\cap C\ne\emptyset$, i.e. some conjugate of $\tau$ lies in $C$, i.e. $C'=\langle\tau\rangle$ is conjugate to a subgroup of $C$; if in addition $|C'|=|C|$ then $C'$ is conjugate to $C$, so among representatives of equal order only $C'=C$ contributes, which is the triangularity. For the diagonal, let $\sigma$ generate $C$. The conjugates of $\sigma$ lying in $C$ have order $|C|$, hence generate $C$, hence are of the form $g\sigma g^{-1}$ with $g\in N\_G(C)$; the number of them is the size of the $N\_G(C)$-orbit of $\sigma$ under conjugation, $|N\_G(C)|/|C\_G(\sigma)|$, because $C\_G(\sigma)\subseteq N\_G(C)$. Lemma 1.1 gives

$$\pi\_C(\sigma)=\frac{|C\_G(\sigma)|}{|C|}\cdot\frac{|N\_G(C)|}{|C\_G(\sigma)|}=[N\_G(C)\:C].$$

A triangular matrix with nonzero diagonal is invertible over $\mathbb{Q}$; $|\mathcal{C}|$ independent vectors in a space of dimension $|\mathcal{C}|$ form a basis. $\square$

The classical Artin induction theorem, $|G|\cdot\chi\in\sum\_C\mathbb{Z},\mathrm{Ind}\_C^G(\text{characters of }C)$ for every character $\chi$, is the integral refinement; only the $\mathbb{Q}$-span statement above is needed here, and its proof is the triangularity.

## 4. Coset actions determine the rational class of Frobenius exactly, and nothing finer

**Theorem 4.1.** Let $\phi\in G$ be a Frobenius element at $\ell\nmid\mathrm{disc}f$.

1. The vector $(\pi\_C(\phi))*{C\in\mathcal{C}}$ determines the rational class of $\phi$; so does $(\pi\_H(\phi))*{H\in\mathcal{H}}$ for any family $\mathcal{H}$ of subgroups whose permutation characters span $\mathrm{Cl}\_\mathbb{Q}(G)$, or more generally separate rational classes.
2. Conversely, the totality of factorization types of all resolvents $\mathrm{Res}\_{F,H}$, $H\le G$, at $\ell$ determines exactly the rational class of $\phi$: two Frobenius candidates in the same rational class produce identical factorization types for every $H$.

*Proof.* 1. By Theorem 3.1, $\theta\_{C'}=\sum\_Cc\_{C',C}\pi\_C$ with $c\_{C',C}\in\mathbb{Q}$, so $\theta\_{C'}(\phi)=\sum\_Cc\_{C',C}\pi\_C(\phi)$ is computable from the vector, and exactly one $\theta\_{C'}(\phi)$ equals $1$. For a family $\mathcal{H}$: if the $\pi\_H$ span $\mathrm{Cl}*\mathbb{Q}(G)$ the same argument applies; separation of rational classes is the weaker sufficient condition that the map (rational class) $\mapsto(\pi\_H(\cdot))*{H\in\mathcal{H}}$ be injective, which is decidable from the class table by Lemma 1.1. 2. Proposition 1.2 reduces factorization types to the values $\pi\_H(\phi^d)$, and Proposition 2.2 shows these depend only on the rational class of $\phi$. $\square$

**Corollary 4.2.** Coset actions at $\ell$ suffice to determine $\mathrm{Frob}\_\ell$ exactly iff every rational class of $G$ is a single conjugacy class, i.e. iff every character of $G$ is rational-valued (equivalently, by Lemma 2.1(b), $N\_G(\langle\sigma\rangle)\to\mathrm{Aut}(\langle\sigma\rangle)$ is surjective for every $\sigma$; e.g. $G=S\_n$, Weyl groups). Otherwise the finer determination requires identifying *which* root of a resolvent is $F(\alpha)$, not how many roots are rational: that is the alignment of [the precision policy](https://claude.ai/chat/precision-policy.md) §4, which is exact because it uses the certificate values $c\_i$ and the separation of resolvent roots at $\ell$ rather than counts.

**Corollary 4.3 (the degree-$n$ action alone).** The cycle type of $f$ modulo $\ell$ is the function $d\mapsto\pi\_{G\_1}(\phi^d)$. It determines the rational class iff the cyclic subgroups of $G$ that are not conjugate have distinct cycle types in the degree-$n$ action; whether this holds is checked from the table. When it fails, the resolvents for a separating family $\mathcal{H}$ from Theorem 4.1(1) (Lemma 1.1 gives the values, and a family is chosen greedily until the vectors are distinct over rational classes) supply the missing distinctions; the minimal such family is what the pipeline attaches to $G$ before the unramified-prime loop, and its invariants enter $\mathcal{F}$ in [the precision policy](https://claude.ai/chat/precision-policy.md) §5.

**Summary of the unramified-prime loop.** For each $\ell\le X$, $\ell\nmid\mathrm{disc}f$: factor $f$ modulo $\ell$ (cycle type); if the cycle type already determines the rational class and that rational class is a single conjugacy class, $\mathrm{Frob}*\ell$ is found; otherwise use the resolvents of the separating family to reach the rational class (Theorem 4.1), and, when the rational class contains several conjugacy classes, the alignment test of* [*the precision policy*](https://claude.ai/chat/precision-policy.md) *§4 to reach the conjugacy class. The first two stages need only factorizations modulo $\ell$ (or, when $\ell$ divides a resolvent discriminant, root counts at* [*the precision policy*](https://claude.ai/chat/precision-policy.md) *precision); only the last stage needs the $\ell$-adic roots at precision $k*\ell$.
