# $\mathrm{Gal}(f/\mathbb{Q}\_\ell)$ for reducible $f$

**Setting.** Over $\mathbb{Z}*\ell$, $f=\prod*{j=1}^sg\_j$ with $g\_j$ monic irreducible of degree $d\_j$, local invariants $(e\_j,f\_j)$ with $e\_jf\_j=d\_j$ ([the ramified primes](ramified-primes.md)), $\nu\_j=v\_\ell(\mathrm{disc},g\_j)$, $\nu=v\_\ell(\mathrm{disc}f)\ge\sum\_j\nu\_j$. Let $L\_j$ be the splitting field of $g\_j$ over $\mathbb{Q}*\ell$, $D\_j=\mathrm{Gal}(L\_j/\mathbb{Q}*\ell)$ acting on the $d\_j$ roots of $g\_j$, $L=L\_1\cdots L\_s$ the splitting field of $f$, and $D=\mathrm{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}*\ell)$ acting on all $n$ roots in their $\ell$-adic numbering. $D$ is the decomposition group $D*\ell\le G$ once the $\ell$-adic numbering is aligned with the $p$-adic one ([the precision policy](precision-policy.md) §4). Valuations are normalized by $v(\ell)=1$; for a finite extension $K/\mathbb{Q}\_\ell$, $e(K),f(K)$ are its ramification index and residue degree, and "precision $\kappa$ in $K$" means modulo $\varpi\_K^{\kappa}$.

## 1. What determines $D$

**Proposition 1.1 (Krasner determinacy).** The permutation group $D\le S\_n$, in the $\ell$-adic numbering, is determined by $f\bmod\ell^{\nu+1}$: if $\tilde f\equiv f\pmod{\ell^{\nu+1}}$ is monic, then $\tilde f$ has the same factorization type, the root bijection of [the precision policy](precision-policy.md) Lemma 3.3 identifies the roots of $\tilde f$ with those of $f$ Galois-equivariantly, and under it $\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde f/\mathbb{Q}\_\ell)=D$ as permutation groups.

*Proof.* [the precision policy](precision-policy.md) Proposition 3.4: the factorization of $\tilde f$ lifts factor by factor to $\tilde g\_j\equiv g\_j\pmod{\ell^{\nu+1}}$ with $\nu+1>\nu\_j$, and [the precision policy](precision-policy.md) Lemma 3.3 gives, for each $j$, a $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}*\ell/\mathbb{Q}*\ell)$-equivariant bijection between the roots of $\tilde g\_j$ and of $g\_j$ with $\mathbb{Q}*\ell(\tilde\alpha)=\mathbb{Q}*\ell(\alpha)$. The splitting field of $\tilde g\_j$ is generated by its roots, each generating the same field as its partner, so $\tilde L\_j=L\_j$ and $\tilde L=L$; the equivariant bijection transports the action of $\mathrm{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}\_\ell)$ on one root set to the other. $\square$

So every computation below may be done with $f$ replaced by any convenient lift, and no precision beyond what Proposition 1.1 and the internal tower precisions of §2 to §4 demand is ever needed to *define* the answer; [the precision policy](precision-policy.md)'s $k\_\ell$ is needed only to *align* it with the $p$-adic numbering.

## 2. Per-factor groups

**Construction 2.1 (splitting field as a tower).** $K^{(0)}=\mathbb{Q}*\ell$, $h^{(0)}=g\_j$. While $h^{(i)}$ has an irreducible factor $q\_i$ of degree $>1$ over $K^{(i)}$: $K^{(i+1)}=K^{(i)}[y]/(q\_i)$, and $h^{(i+1)}$ = the product of the nonlinear factors of $h^{(i)}$ over $K^{(i+1)}$. The tower stops at $K^{(r\_j)}=L\_j$ after $r\_j\le d\_j-1$ steps, $[L\_j:\mathbb{Q}*\ell]=|D\_j|=\prod\_i\deg q\_i$. Each factorization is a Montes/Round 4 factorization over the current field, correct at precision $e(K^{(i)}),\nu\_j+1$ in $K^{(i)}$ ([the ramified primes](ramified-primes.md) §2 and [the precision policy](precision-policy.md) Prop. 3.4 over the base $K^{(i)}$: the discriminant of the polynomial being factored divides $\mathrm{disc},g\_j$, whose $K^{(i)}$-valuation is $e(K^{(i)})\nu\_j$).

Elements of $K^{(i)}$ are represented recursively in the tower (or, after computing a uniformizer and an integral basis, as vectors over $\mathbb{Z}*\ell$ of length $[K^{(i)}:\mathbb{Q}*\ell]$); multiplication costs $\mathrm{M}(K^{(i)})=\tilde O([K^{(i)}:\mathbb{Q}\_\ell]\cdot\kappa\log\ell)$ bit operations at precision $\kappa$ in $\ell$-adic digits.

**Proposition 2.2 (the group).** Let $\beta\_1,\dots,\beta\_{d\_j}\in L\_j$ be the roots of $g\_j$ (each a tower element; the ones adjoined are generators, the others obtained by Hensel lifting of residue roots or as linear factors found along the way), numbered. For each tuple of choices $(\gamma\_0,\dots,\gamma\_{r\_j-1})$ with $\gamma\_i$ a root in $L\_j$ of $q\_i$ with coefficients transported by the partial map already defined, there is a unique automorphism of $L\_j$ sending the $i$-th tower generator to $\gamma\_i$, and all $|D\_j|$ automorphisms arise once. Each automorphism's action on the $\beta$'s is read off by evaluating the tower expressions of the $\beta$'s and identifying the results with roots, which is exact at precision $>\nu\_j/2$ ([the precision policy](precision-policy.md) Lemma 3.1(c)). The result is $D\_j\le S\_{d\_j}$, transitive.

*Proof.* $L\_j/\mathbb{Q}\_\ell$ is Galois, so an embedding of $K^{(i+1)}=K^{(i)}(\gamma)$ extending a given automorphism $\tau$ of $K^{(i)}$ exists for each root of $q\_i^{\tau}$ in $L\_j$ and these exhaust the extensions; induct up the tower. Root identification is by [the precision policy](precision-policy.md) Lemma 3.1(c): distinct roots of $g\_j$ differ by valuation $\le\nu\_j/2$. Transitivity is irreducibility. $\square$

Only generators of $D\_j$ are needed downstream. A Stauduhar-type descent inside $S\_{d\_j}$ (resolvents over $\mathbb{Q}*\ell$ with roots in $L\_j$; the integrality test is "$F(\tau\beta)\in\mathbb{Q}*\ell$", decided as the number of $\mathbb{Q}*\ell$-roots of the resolvent at precision $>v*\ell(\mathrm{disc},R)$, which is computed rather than bounded a priori) is an alternative to enumerating all automorphisms when $|D\_j|$ is large; its output is the same group and its correctness is [the precision policy](precision-policy.md) Proposition 3.4(4) applied to $R$.

**Proposition 2.3 (structure and certificate).** Let $I\_j\le D\_j$ be the automorphisms acting trivially on the residue field of $L\_j$ (computable: trivial modulo $\varpi\_{L\_j}$ on the tower generators) and $P\_j$ its $\ell$-Sylow subgroup. Then $I\_j\trianglelefteq D\_j$, $P\_j\trianglelefteq D\_j$, $D\_j/I\_j$ is cyclic of order $f(L\_j)$, $I\_j/P\_j$ is cyclic of order prime to $\ell$, and $D\_j$ is solvable; $I\_j$ has $f\_j$ orbits of size $e\_j$ on the roots of $g\_j$, and the stabilizer of a root has index $d\_j$. These are checked on the computed group and certify it against the invariants of [the ramified primes](ramified-primes.md).

*Proof.* Standard local theory for the Galois extension $L\_j/\mathbb{Q}*\ell$; the orbit statement is $g\_j$ factoring over $\mathbb{Q}*\ell^{\mathrm{ur}}$ into $f\_j$ irreducible factors of degree $e\_j$ because $\mathbb{Q}*\ell[x]/(g\_j)\otimes\mathbb{Q}*\ell^{\mathrm{ur}}$ is a product of $f\_j$ totally ramified extensions of degree $e\_j$. $\square$

**Cost 2.4.** Let $\delta\_j=[L\_j:\mathbb{Q}*\ell]=|D\_j|$. The tower requires at most $d\_j-1$ factorizations of polynomials of degree $\le d\_j$ over fields of degree $\le\delta\_j$ at $\ell$-adic precision $O(\nu\_j)$: $\tilde O\big(d\_j^{3},\nu\_j,\delta\_j\log\ell\big)$ bit operations, plus $O(\delta\_j)$ automorphism extensions each costing a root-finding of a polynomial of degree $\le d\_j$ over $L\_j$, $\tilde O(d\_j,\nu\_j,\delta\_j\log\ell)$ each, and $d\_j$ root identifications per automorphism. Total $\tilde O\big((d\_j^{3}+d\_j\delta\_j),\nu\_j,\delta\_j\log\ell\big)$; with only $O(\log\delta\_j)$ generators computed, the second term drops to $\tilde O(d\_j\nu\_j\delta\_j\log\ell\log\delta\_j)$. Here $\delta\_j\le\mathfrak{s}(d\_j)$, the largest order of a transitive solvable subgroup of $S*{d\_j}$ ($\mathfrak{s}(d)\le24^{(d-1)/3}$), and also $\delta\_j\le|G|$.

## 3. Two factors

Let $E=L\_1\cap L\_2$, $M\_1=\mathrm{Gal}(L\_1/E)\trianglelefteq D\_1$, $M\_2=\mathrm{Gal}(L\_2/E)\trianglelefteq D\_2$, and $D\_{12}=\mathrm{Gal}(L\_1L\_2/\mathbb{Q}\_\ell)$ acting on the $d\_1+d\_2$ roots.

**Lemma 3.1 (Goursat).** Restriction embeds $D\_{12}\hookrightarrow D\_1\times D\_2$ as a subdirect product, and

$$D\_{12}={(\sigma\_1,\sigma\_2)\in D\_1\times D\_2:\ \sigma\_1|\_E=\sigma\_2|\_E}={(\sigma\_1,\sigma\_2):\ \psi(\sigma\_1M\_1)=\sigma\_2M\_2},$$

where $\psi\:D\_1/M\_1\to D\_2/M\_2$ is the isomorphism through $\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q}*\ell)$. Conversely every subdirect product of $D\_1\times D\_2$ is of this form for a unique triple $(M\_1,M\_2,\psi)$: $M\_1\times1=D*{12}\cap(D\_1\times1)$, $1\times M\_2=D\_{12}\cap(1\times D\_2)$, and $D\_{12}/(M\_1\times M\_2)$ is the graph of $\psi$. In particular $|D\_{12}|=|D\_1|,|M\_2|=|D\_1||D\_2|/[E:\mathbb{Q}\_\ell]$.

*Proof.* An automorphism of $L\_1L\_2$ is determined by its restrictions, which agree on $E$; conversely a pair agreeing on $E$ defines an automorphism of $L\_1\otimes\_EL\_2\cong L\_1L\_2$ (the tensor product over $E$ is a field because $L\_1\cap L\_2=E$ and $L\_1/E$ is Galois). The abstract statement is Goursat's lemma: the projections of $D\_{12}\cap(D\_1\times1)$ and $D\_{12}\cap(1\times D\_2)$ are normal in the respective factors and the quotient is the graph of an isomorphism. $\square$

**Proposition 3.2 (the factorization data).** (a) The irreducible factors of $g\_2$ over $L\_1$ have coefficients in $E$ and are exactly the irreducible factors of $g\_2$ over $E$; their root sets are the orbits of $M\_2$ on the roots of $g\_2$. Symmetrically for $g\_1$ over $L\_2$ and $M\_1$. (b) $M\_2=\mathrm{Gal}(g\_2/L\_1)$, the Galois group of $g\_2$ over $L\_1$, and $L\_1L\_2$ is the splitting field of $g\_2$ over $L\_1$. (c) The orbit partitions alone do not determine $M\_1,M\_2,\psi$ in general (a normal subgroup is not determined by its orbits, and $\psi$ is not determined by the induced permutation of factors, e.g. when $g\_2$ stays irreducible over $E$); the splitting field of $g\_2$ over $L\_1$ does.

*Proof.* (a) $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\_\ell/L\_1)$ acts on the roots of $g\_2$ through $\mathrm{Gal}(L\_1L\_2/L\_1)\cong\mathrm{Gal}(L\_2/E)=M\_2$ (Lemma 3.1), so the factors over $L\_1$ are the $M\_2$-orbit polynomials, whose coefficients are $M\_2$-invariant elements of $L\_2$, i.e. lie in $E$; a factor over $E$ is a fortiori a factor over $L\_1$, and the orbit description shows they coincide. (b) is the same isomorphism. (c) Take $D\_2=S\_4$ (a $2$-adic quartic with group $S\_4$ exists) and $E$ either the $S\_3$-subfield $L\_2^{V\_4}$ or the quadratic subfield $L\_2^{A\_4}$: in both cases $g\_2$ is irreducible over $E$ and its single factor is $g\_2$ itself, carrying no information. $\square$

**Algorithm 3.3 (compositum from the two factors).**

1. Construct $L\_1$ and generators ${s\_1,\dots,s\_a}$ of $D\_1$ (§2).
2. Construction 2.1 with base field $L\_1$ and polynomial $g\_2$: a tower $L\_1=K^{(0)}\subset\dots\subset K^{(r)}=L\_1L\_2$, the roots of $g\_2$ in $L\_1L\_2$, and (Proposition 2.2 over the base $L\_1$) the group $\mathrm{Gal}(L\_1L\_2/L\_1)$ as a permutation group on the roots of $g\_2$. This is $M\_2$, given directly as a subgroup of the symmetric group on the $\ell$-adically numbered roots of $g\_2$; the abstract $D\_2$ of §2 is matched to this numbering by identifying the roots of $g\_2$ in $L\_2$ with those in $L\_1L\_2$ through one embedding $L\_2\hookrightarrow L\_1L\_2$ (a root of the defining polynomial of the $L\_2$-tower found in $L\_1L\_2$), and changing the embedding conjugates $M\_2$ inside $D\_2$.
3. For each generator $s\_i$ of $D\_1$, extend it to an automorphism $\tilde s\_i$ of $L\_1L\_2$: at each level of the tower of step 2, map the generator $\gamma$ with minimal polynomial $q$ over the field below to any root of $q^{\tilde s\_i}$ (coefficients transported by the extension already built) in $L\_1L\_2$, found by Hensel lifting from a residue root. Record the permutation $\sigma\_2(s\_i)$ of $\tilde s\_i$ on the roots of $g\_2$; then $\psi(s\_iM\_1)=\sigma\_2(s\_i)M\_2$.
4. $D\_{12}=\big\langle(s\_i,\sigma\_2(s\_i))\ (i\le a),\ (1,m)\ (m\in\text{generators of }M\_2)\big\rangle\le S\_{d\_1}\times S\_{d\_2}\le S\_{d\_1+d\_2}$.
5. Certificates: $|D\_{12}|=|D\_1||M\_2|$; the projection of $D\_{12}$ to the roots of $g\_2$ equals $D\_2$ (after the identification of step 2); $M\_1={\sigma\_1\in D\_1:\sigma\_2\in M\_2\text{ for the extension}}$ has $[D\_1\:M\_1]=[D\_2\:M\_2]=[E:\mathbb{Q}\_\ell]$; and the factorization degrees of $g\_1$ over $L\_2$ (Proposition 3.2(a) with the roles exchanged, computed as the $M\_1$-orbits) agree with those obtained by factoring $g\_1$ over $L\_2$ directly if that factorization is also computed.

**Theorem 3.4.** Algorithm 3.3 returns $D\_{12}=\mathrm{Gal}(L\_1L\_2/\mathbb{Q}\_\ell)$ acting on the $\ell$-adic numbering of the roots of $g\_1g\_2$.

*Proof.* Step 2 gives $M\_2$ by Proposition 3.2(b) and Proposition 2.2 applied over $L\_1$. In step 3 an extension of $s\_i$ to $L\_1L\_2$ exists and every choice of roots yields one (as in Proposition 2.2); its restriction to the roots of $g\_2$ is a coset representative of $\psi(s\_iM\_1)$ by Lemma 3.1. The group generated in step 4 is contained in $D\_{12}$ (each generator is an automorphism of $L\_1L\_2$), maps onto $D\_1$ (the $s\_i$ generate it) with kernel containing $1\times M\_2$, hence has order $\ge|D\_1||M\_2|=|D\_{12}|$; so it equals $D\_{12}$. $\square$

**Shortcuts.** If $\gcd(|D\_1|,|D\_2|)=1$ then $E=\mathbb{Q}*\ell$, $M\_2=D\_2$, $D*{12}=D\_1\times D\_2$, and steps 2 to 3 are unnecessary; steps 2 to 3 are then replaced by the cheaper direct product, but the roots of $g\_2$ must still be placed in a common field with those of $g\_1$ for the numbering, which is done in $L\_1L\_2\cong L\_1\otimes L\_2$. More generally $[E:\mathbb{Q}*\ell]$ divides $\gcd(|D\_1|,|D\_2|)$, $f(E)$ divides $\gcd(f(L\_1),f(L\_2))$, and if $L\_1$ and $L\_2$ have coprime ramification indices and $\gcd(f(L\_1),f(L\_2))=1$ then $E=\mathbb{Q}*\ell$.

## 4. All factors

Iterate: $L^{(1)}=L\_1$, $D^{(1)}=D\_1$; for $j=1,\dots,s-1$ apply Algorithm 3.3 with $(L^{(j)},D^{(j)})$ in place of $(L\_1,D\_1)$ and $g\_{j+1}$ in place of $g\_2$, obtaining $M\_{j+1}=\mathrm{Gal}(g\_{j+1}/L^{(j)})$, the extensions of the generators of $D^{(j)}$, and $D^{(j+1)}\le S\_{d\_1+\dots+d\_{j+1}}$. The order of the factors is free; taking them in decreasing $|D\_j|$ keeps the towers shallow.

**Theorem 4.1.** $D=D^{(s)}=\mathrm{Gal}(f/\mathbb{Q}*\ell)$ acting on all $n$ roots in the $\ell$-adic numbering, with $|D|=\prod*{j=1}^s|M\_j|$ ($M\_1=D\_1$), $|M\_{j+1}|=[L^{(j+1)}\:L^{(j)}]$, and $E\_{j+1}=L^{(j)}\cap L\_{j+1}$ of degree $|D\_{j+1}|/|M\_{j+1}|$. Each step is certified as in Algorithm 3.3(5), and the whole is certified by: $D$ is solvable; its orbits on the roots are the root sets of the $g\_j$ with the projections equal to the $D\_j$; the subgroup $I={\sigma\in D:\sigma\equiv\mathrm{id}\bmod\varpi\_L}$ is normal with $D/I$ cyclic of order $f(L)=\mathrm{lcm}\_jf(L\_j)$ (the maximal unramified subextension of a compositum is the compositum of the maximal unramified subextensions); and $I$ has, on the roots of $g\_j$, $f\_j$ orbits of size $e\_j$.

*Proof.* Induction on $j$ with Theorem 3.4; the certificates are Proposition 2.3 for $L/\mathbb{Q}\_\ell$. $\square$

**Alignment into $G$.** Lift the $n$ roots in $L$ to $\ell$-adic precision $k\_\ell$ ([the precision policy](precision-policy.md) §5) by Hensel lifting in the tower, run the alignment of [the precision policy](precision-policy.md) §4 (or [the direct route](direct-route.md) §3 with the tower field in place of $\mathbb{F}*{\ell^r}$, since $\ell$ is now ramified and residue arithmetic does not suffice), obtaining an admissible $\pi$ with $\pi^{-1}D\pi\le G$; this is $D*\ell$ up to conjugacy in $G$, and $\pi^{-1}I\pi=I\_\ell$. That $\pi^{-1}D\pi\subseteq G$ is a certificate of both computations. An independent certificate of the conjugacy class of $D\_\ell$ in $G$: the number of $\mathbb{Q}*\ell$-roots of each auxiliary resolvent $R*{G,H,F}$ of [separating subgroups](separating-subgroups.md) equals the mark $\mathrm{fix}*{G/H}(D*\ell)$, and the vector of marks over the subgroup classes $H$ of $G$ determines the class of $D\_\ell$ by the invertibility of the table of marks; this costs one $\ell$-adic factorization per resolvent and is optional.

## 5. Cost

Let $\Delta^{(j)}=[L^{(j)}:\mathbb{Q}\_\ell]=|D^{(j)}|$, $\Delta=|D|\le\min\big(|G|,\prod\_j\mathfrak{s}(d\_j)\big)$, and let arithmetic in $L^{(j)}$ at $\ell$-adic precision $O(\nu)$ cost $\mathrm{M}\_j=\tilde O(\Delta^{(j)}\nu\log\ell)$.

**Proposition 5.1.** The computation of $D$ costs

$$\tilde O\Big(\sum\_{j=1}^{s}\big(d\_j^{3}+\log\Delta^{(j-1)}\cdot|M\_j|\big),\nu,\Delta^{(j)}\log\ell\Big)\ \le\ \tilde O\big((n^{3}+\Delta\log\Delta),\nu,\Delta\log\ell\big)$$

bit operations, where the first term in the sum is the factorizations building $L^{(j)}$ over $L^{(j-1)}$ (at most $d\_j-1$ factorizations of polynomials of degree $\le d\_j$ over fields of degree $\le\Delta^{(j)}$ at precision $e(L^{(j-1)})\nu\_j+1$ in $L^{(j-1)}$, i.e. $O(\nu\_j)$ $\ell$-adic digits) and the second the extension of the $O(\log\Delta^{(j-1)})$ generators of $D^{(j-1)}$, each a root-finding of a polynomial of degree $\le|M\_j|$ over $L^{(j)}$. Root identification and the group operations on permutations of $n$ points are dominated. Alignment adds $\tilde O(k\_\ell,\Delta,\sum\_im\_ic\_i\log\ell)$ ([the direct route](direct-route.md) §5 with $\mathbb{F}*{\ell^r}$ replaced by $\mathcal{O}L/\ell^{k\ell}$ and $k*\ell$ in place of the single digit).

*Proof.* Per step, Cost 2.4 over the base $L^{(j-1)}$ with $\delta$ replaced by $\Delta^{(j)}$; the precision claim is [the ramified primes](ramified-primes.md) §2/[the precision policy](precision-policy.md) Prop. 3.4 over the base, using $v\_{L^{(j-1)}}(\mathrm{disc},g\_j)=e(L^{(j-1)})\nu\_j$; the number of generators of a solvable group of order $\Delta$ acting on $\le n$ points is $O(\log\Delta)$. $\square$

**Dependence on the data.** The bound is polynomial in $n$, $\nu=v\_\ell(\mathrm{disc}f)$, $\log\ell$ and $\Delta=|D\_\ell|$; $\Delta$ is bounded by a function of the factor degrees alone ($\prod\_j\mathfrak{s}(d\_j)$) and by $|G|$. The dependence on $\nu$ is linear because every precision in the towers is $O(\nu\_j)$ $\ell$-adic digits by Proposition 1.1 and its relative version, independent of the depth of the towers; the ramification of the towers enters only through the number of uniformizer digits per $\ell$-adic digit, which is absorbed in $\mathrm{M}\_j$. The dependence on the degrees is the $d\_j^{3}$ of the factorizations and, through $\Delta$, the orders of the local groups. For the ramified primes of a degree-$12$ polynomial, $\Delta$ is at most a few thousand and the whole computation is dominated by the alignment, not by the compositum.
