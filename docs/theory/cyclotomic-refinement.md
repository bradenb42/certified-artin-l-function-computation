# The cyclotomic refinement of $\mathrm{Frob}\_\ell$

**Summary of what is and is not true.** Let $G'=\mathrm{Gal}(N(\zeta\_M)/\mathbb{Q})$. The rational class of $\mathrm{Frob}*\ell$ in $G'$ together with its cyclotomic coordinate $\ell\bmod M$ does* ***not*** *determine the class of $\mathrm{Frob}*\ell$ in $G$ in general (Example 3.3: a cyclic cubic field with $\ell\equiv1\bmod 3$ when $M=\exp G=3$; $C\_7\rtimes C\_3$ with $\ell\equiv1\bmod M$ for any $M$). What the cyclotomic coordinate does is constrain the exponent $k$ in $\sigma\mapsto\sigma^k$ to $k\equiv1\pmod{\gcd(o(\sigma),o(\ell\bmod M))}$ (Theorem 3.1). A genuinely finer tool exists at the same cost: *twisted* resolvents with coefficients in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_o]$, reduced at a prime $\lambda\mid\ell$ of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_o]$; they compute the values of the induced characters $\mathrm{Ind}*C^G\chi$ at $\sigma^{f}$, $f=o(\ell\bmod o)$, and by Artin induction determine the class of $\sigma^{f}$ exactly (Theorem 4.3). Combining both, the class of $\mathrm{Frob}*\ell$ is exact for all $\ell$ in an explicitly computable union of residue classes modulo $M$, including every $\ell\equiv1\pmod M$ and every $\ell$ with $o(\sigma)\mid o(\ell\bmod M)$; the residual ambiguity is characterized exactly (Theorem 5.1) and is nonempty for some groups (Example 5.2), where the alignment of [the precision policy](precision-policy.md) §4 remains the only exact method. Two corrections to the setup are also needed: the auxiliary modulus must include the conductor of $N^{ab}$, not just $\exp G$, and the gluing map of the fiber product must be pinned by a $p$-adic computation, since abstract group data determine it only up to power automorphisms.

Throughout, $o(x)$ is the order of $x$; $\pi\:G\to G^{ab}$; a prime $\ell$ satisfies $\ell\nmid M\cdot\mathrm{disc}f$.

## 1. $N^{ab}$ inside a cyclotomic field, and the group $G'$

**Cyclic subfields and conductors.** For $\psi\in\widehat{G^{ab}}$ of order $o\_\psi$, $N\_\psi=N^{\ker(\psi\circ\pi)}$ is cyclic of degree $o\_\psi$. By Kronecker to Weber there is a Dirichlet character $\chi\_\psi$ of some conductor $c\_\psi$ with $\chi\_\psi(\ell)=\psi(\pi(\mathrm{Frob}*\ell))$ for all $\ell\nmid c*\psi$, and $N\_\psi=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_{c\_\psi})^{\ker\chi\_\psi}$. The conductors $c\_\psi$ are input (from the discriminants of the $N\_\psi$ by the conductor to discriminant formula, or from [the character table](character-table.md) §5 once $D\_\ell,I\_\ell$ are known). Put $c=\mathrm{lcm}*\psi c*\psi$, so $N^{ab}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_c)$, and
$$M=\mathrm{lcm}(\exp G,\ c).$$

**Lemma 1.1.** $N\cap\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_M)=N^{ab}$.

*Proof.* $N\cap\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_M)$ is abelian over $\mathbb{Q}$, hence contained in $N^{ab}$; and $N^{ab}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_c)\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_M)$. $\square$

With $M=\exp G$ alone the intersection can be a proper subfield of $N^{ab}$ (Example 3.3), which is why $c$ is included.

**Proposition 1.2 (kernels from rational data).** $U\_\psi:=\ker\chi\_\psi\le(\mathbb{Z}/c\_\psi)^\times$ is determined by the rational classes of Frobenius at finitely many primes: for $\ell\nmid c$, $\ell\bmod c\_\psi\in U\_\psi$ iff $\mathrm{Frob}*\ell\in\ker(\psi\circ\pi)$, a condition on the rational class of $\mathrm{Frob}*\ell$ (a subgroup is closed under powers). Collect primes until the subgroup generated by the residues found in $U\_\psi$ has index $o\_\psi$ in $(\mathbb{Z}/c\_\psi)^\times$; the generated subgroup is then $U\_\psi$. This terminates by Chebotarev. Then $U=\bigcap\_\psi\big(U\_\psi\text{ pulled back to }(\mathbb{Z}/c)^\times\big)$, $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_c)^U=N^{ab}$, and $(\mathbb{Z}/c)^\times/U\cong G^{ab}$ abstractly.

The isomorphism is the problem. Any two isomorphisms $(\mathbb{Z}/c)^\times/U\to G^{ab}$ compatible with all rational-class data differ by an automorphism of $G^{ab}$ sending every element to a power of itself, and such automorphisms are invisible to every test of [rational classes](rational-classes.md) to [separating subgroups](separating-subgroups.md). The gluing map is pinned instead by the $p$-adic numbering:

**Proposition 1.3 (pinning $\iota$).** For each $\psi$ let $\beta\_\psi=F\_\psi(\alpha)\in N\_\psi$ be a primitive element obtained from an invariant of $\ker(\psi\circ\pi)$ ([separating subgroups](separating-subgroups.md) §3, degree $o\_\psi$ resolvent). Choose any primitive $c\_\psi$-th root of unity $\omega\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}*p$ (in $\mathbb{Z}*{q'}$ with $c\_\psi\mid q'-1$) and form the Gauss periods $\eta\_u=\sum\_{v\in uU\_\psi}\omega^v$, $u\in(\mathbb{Z}/c\_\psi)^\times/U\_\psi$, as $p$-adic numbers, together with a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis $e\_1,\dots,e\_{o\_\psi}$ of $N\_\psi$ made of $\mathbb{Z}$-combinations of the $\eta\_u$ (the periods themselves when they are independent). Solve $\iota\_p(\beta\_\psi)=\sum\_ib\_ie\_i$ for $b\_i\in\mathbb{Q}$ by $p$-adic linear algebra and rational reconstruction. Then for $x\in(\mathbb{Z}/c\_\psi)^\times$,

$$\iota\_\psi(x):=\text{the }g\in G^{ab}/\ker\psi\text{ with }F\_\psi(g\alpha)=\sum\_ib\_ie\_i^{[x]}\ \text{ in }\bar{\mathbb{Q}}*p,\qquad e\_i^{[x]}:=e\_i\text{ with }\eta\_u\text{ replaced by }\eta*{xu},$$

is well defined, independent of the choice of $\omega$, and equals the restriction to $N\_\psi$ of the Artin map: $\iota\_\psi(\ell)=\pi(\mathrm{Frob}*\ell)\bmod\ker\psi$ for every $\ell\nmid c*\psi$. The $\iota\_\psi$ assemble to the isomorphism $\iota:(\mathbb{Z}/c)^\times/U\to G^{ab}$ with $\chi\_\psi=\psi\circ\iota$.

*Proof.* $\zeta\_{c\_\psi}\mapsto\zeta\_{c\_\psi}^x$ is the automorphism of $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_{c\_\psi})$ that is the Artin symbol of $x$; it acts on periods by $\eta\_u\mapsto\eta\_{xu}$ and preserves $N\_\psi$, so on $N\_\psi$ it is $\beta\mapsto\sum b\_ie\_i^{[x]}$ when $\beta=\sum b\_ie\_i$. Every embedding $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_{c\_\psi})\to\bar{\mathbb{Q}}*p$ restricts on $N*\psi$ to $\iota\_p\circ g$ for some $g\in G^{ab}$, and the images of $N\_\psi$ under all of them coincide (it is Galois); replacing $\omega$ by $\omega^y$ replaces $\eta\_u$ by $\eta\_{yu}$, hence $b$ by its translate, and the expression $\sum b\_ie\_i^{[x]}$ is unchanged. The element $g$ with $F\_\psi(g\alpha)$ equal to it exists and is unique because the $F\_\psi(g\alpha)$, $g\in G^{ab}/\ker\psi$, are the $o\_\psi$ distinct roots of the resolvent and the Artin symbol of $x$ permutes them. Taking $x=\ell$ gives Frobenius. $\square$

The comparison "$F\_\psi(g\alpha)=\sum b\_ie\_i^{[x]}$" is a test of type (T2)/(T3) of [the precision policy](precision-policy.md) on the resolvent of $F\_\psi$, decided at the [the precision policy](precision-policy.md) precision after adding $(F\_\psi,o\_\psi)$ to $\mathcal{F}$; the rational reconstruction of the $b\_i$ uses the bound $|b\_i|\le o\_\psi^{o\_\psi/2},B\_{F\_\psi}\cdot|\det(\mathrm{Tr}(e\_ie\_j))|^{1/2}$, since $b$ solves a linear system whose matrix has algebraic-integer entries of modulus $\le c\_\psi$ and determinant of absolute value $\ge1$ times the square root of the discriminant of the basis. A consistency check is free: $\iota(p\bmod c)$ must equal $\pi(\mathrm{Frob}\_p)$, and $\mathrm{Frob}\_p$ is exact from the numbering ($\alpha\_i\mapsto\alpha\_i^p$).

**Proposition 1.4 (presentation of $G'$).** With $\kappa:(\mathbb{Z}/M)^\times\to(\mathbb{Z}/c)^\times\to(\mathbb{Z}/c)^\times/U\xrightarrow{\iota}G^{ab}$,

$$G'=\mathrm{Gal}(N(\zeta\_M)/\mathbb{Q})={(g,x)\in G\times(\mathbb{Z}/M)^\times:\ \pi(g)=\kappa(x)},\qquad|G'|=|G|,\varphi(M)/|G^{ab}|,$$

with $\ker(G'\to(\mathbb{Z}/M)^\times)=[G,G]\times{1}$ and $\ker(G'\to G)={1}\times\ker\kappa$. Generators: $(g\_i,x\_i)$ for the generators $g\_i$ of $G$ with $x\_i$ any preimage of $\pi(g\_i)$ under $\kappa$, and $(1,y\_j)$ for generators $y\_j$ of $\ker\kappa$. $G'$ acts faithfully on the $n+\varphi(M)$ symbols ${\alpha\_i}\cup{\zeta\_M^x}$, by $G$ on the first and by multiplication on the exponents of the second; a base of $G'$ is a base of $G$ followed by the single symbol $\zeta\_M$.

*Proof.* Restriction gives $G'\hookrightarrow G\times(\mathbb{Z}/M)^\times$ with image the pairs agreeing on $N\cap\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_M)=N^{ab}$ (Lemma 1.1); agreement on $N^{ab}$ is $\pi(g)=\kappa(x)$ by Proposition 1.3, and both projections are onto. The kernels and the order follow. $\square$

## 2. Frobenius in $G'$

**Lemma 2.1.** For $\ell\nmid M\cdot\mathrm{disc}f$, the Frobenius class of $\ell$ in $G'$ is that of $(\sigma,t)$ with $t=\ell\bmod M$ and $\sigma$ a Frobenius element of $\ell$ in $G$; the pair $(\sigma^G,t)$ is well defined and $\pi(\sigma)=\kappa(t)$.

*Proof.* Frobenius restricts to Frobenius on subfields; on $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_M)$ it is $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^\ell$. $\square$

So the image of $\mathrm{Frob}\_\ell$ in $G^{ab}$ is known exactly and for free.

## 3. What the rational class in $G'$ adds

**Theorem 3.1.** Let $\phi=(\sigma,t)\in G'$ and $d=\gcd(o(\sigma),o(t))$. The set of $\sigma'\in G$ with $(\sigma',t)$ in the rational class of $\phi$ in $G'$ is

$${,g\sigma^kg^{-1}:\ g\in G,\ k\equiv1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ d),\ \gcd(k,o(\sigma))=1,}.$$

Hence the rational class of $\mathrm{Frob}\_\ell$ in $G'$ (obtained by [separating subgroups](separating-subgroups.md) applied to $G'$ in its permutation representation of Proposition 1.4) together with $t$ determines $\sigma^G$ exactly iff $\sigma^k\sim\_G\sigma$ for every $k\equiv1\pmod d$ prime to $o(\sigma)$; in particular whenever $o(\sigma)\mid o(t)$.

*Proof.* Elements of the rational class of $\phi$ are $(g,y)(\sigma,t)^k(g,y)^{-1}=(g\sigma^kg^{-1},t^k)$ with $\gcd(k,o(\phi))=1$, $o(\phi)=\mathrm{lcm}(o(\sigma),o(t))$. Second coordinate $t$ forces $t^{k-1}=1$, i.e. $k\equiv1\pmod{o(t)}$; then $\gcd(k,o(t))=1$ automatically, so the condition on $k$ is $k\equiv1\pmod{o(t)}$ and $\gcd(k,o(\sigma))=1$. Reducing modulo $o(\sigma)$: by the Chinese remainder theorem the residues $k\bmod o(\sigma)$ so obtained are exactly the units $\equiv1\pmod{\gcd(o(\sigma),o(t))}$, and $\sigma^k$ depends only on $k\bmod o(\sigma)$. Conversely every such $k$ lifts to an admissible exponent, and $(g,y)$ with the required first coordinate exists since $G'\to G$ is onto. $\square$

**Corollary 3.2.** The rational-class data on $G'$ never separate $\sigma$ from $\sigma^k$ when $k\equiv1\pmod d$; when $t=1$ (i.e. $\ell\equiv1\pmod M$) they give exactly the rational class in $G$ and nothing more.

**Example 3.3.** (a) $N$ cyclic cubic of conductor $7$, $G=C\_3=\langle\sigma\rangle$, $M=\exp G=3$: $N\cap\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_3)=\mathbb{Q}$, $G'=C\_6$, and for $\ell\equiv1\pmod3$, $(\sigma,1)$ and $(\sigma^2,1)$ are rationally conjugate in $C\_6$ with the same second coordinate, although $\sigma\ne\sigma^2$ in $G$ and both occur as $\mathrm{Frob}*\ell$. With $M=21$ (conductor included) the second coordinate is $\ell\bmod21$ and $\iota(\ell\bmod7)$ is the answer. (b) $G=C\_7\rtimes C\_3=\langle a\rangle\rtimes\langle b\rangle$, classes of order $7$: ${a,a^2,a^4}$ and ${a^3,a^5,a^6}$, both mapping to $1\in G^{ab}=C\_3$. For $\ell\equiv1\pmod M$, $t=1$, $d=1$, and $\mathrm{Frob}*\ell\in{a^G,(a^3)^G}$ is not decided by any coset action of $G'$ for any $M$; both classes occur with density $3/21$ each.

## 4. Twisted resolvents over $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_o]$

**Construction.** Let $C=\langle\sigma\rangle\le G$ be cyclic of order $o$, $\chi\:C\to\mu\_o$ a character (faithful or not), $t$ a base of $G$ with monomial $m\_t$ as in [separating subgroups](separating-subgroups.md) §3. The Lagrange invariant is
$$\Lambda\_{C,\chi}=\sum\_{i=0}^{o-1}\chi(\sigma)^{-i},\sigma^i!\cdot m\_t\ \in\ \mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_o][x\_1,\dots,x\_n],\qquad \sigma\cdot\Lambda\_{C,\chi}=\chi(\sigma),\Lambda\_{C,\chi}.$$

**Proposition 4.1.** (a) $\Lambda\_{C,\chi}\ne0$, and ${g\in G:\ g\cdot\Lambda\_{C,\chi}\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}^\times\Lambda\_{C,\chi}}=C$, with $g\Lambda=\chi(g)\Lambda$ for $g\in C$. (b) The polynomial

$$R\_{C,\chi}(x)=\prod\_{g\in G/C}\big(x-(g\Lambda\_{C,\chi})(\alpha)^{o}\big)$$

is well defined on cosets and lies in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_o][x]$; $\deg R\_{C,\chi}=[G\:C]$; and $R\_{C,\chi}(x^o)=\prod\_{g\in G}(x-(g\Lambda\_{C,\chi})(\alpha))$. (c) $|\tau((g\Lambda)(\alpha))|\le o,R\_0^{\binom{k+1}2}=\:B\_\Lambda$ for every complex embedding $\tau$, so the coefficients of $R\_{C,\chi}$ have all conjugates bounded by $(1+B\_\Lambda^{,o})^{[G\:C]}$.

*Proof.* (a) The monomials $\sigma^im\_t=m\_{\sigma^it}$ are distinct because $G\_t=1$, so $\Lambda\ne0$; $g\Lambda$ has support $gCt$ and is a multiple of $\Lambda$ only if $gCt=Ct$, i.e. $g\in C$ ([separating subgroups](separating-subgroups.md) Prop. 3.1); on $C$ the eigenvalue is $\chi$. (b) $(gc\Lambda)^o=(\chi(c)g\Lambda)^o=(g\Lambda)^o$; the product over $G/C$ is $G$-invariant, $G$ acting $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_o)$-linearly, so its coefficients lie in $N^G\cap\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_o]$-integral $=\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_o]$. The last identity is $x^o-\Lambda^o=\prod\_j(x-\zeta\_o^j\Lambda)=\prod\_{c\in C}(x-c\Lambda)$. (c) $\Lambda$ has $o$ monomials of degree $\binom{k+1}2$ with coefficients of modulus $1$. $\square$

Squarefreeness of $R\_{C,\chi}$ ($(g\Lambda)^o\ne(g'\Lambda)^o$ for $gC\ne g'C$) is arranged as in [separating subgroups](separating-subgroups.md) Lemma 3.2 by a Tschirnhaus transformation and certified by $\mathrm{disc}(R\_{C,\chi})\ne0$ in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_o]$.

**Theorem 4.2 (twisted fixed-point counts).** Let $\ell\nmid o\cdot\mathrm{disc}f\cdot N\_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_o)/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{disc}R\_{C,\chi})$, let $\lambda\mid\ell$ be a prime of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_o]$ with residue field $\mathbb{F}*\lambda$ of degree $f\_o=o(\ell\bmod o)$, and let $\sigma*\ell$ be a Frobenius element of $\ell$ in $G$. For $j\in\mathbb{Z}/o$ put

$$n\_j=#{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}*\lambda:\ R*{C,\chi}(\gamma)=0,\ \gamma^{(N\lambda-1)/o}=\zeta\_o^{,j}\bmod\lambda}=\deg\gcd\big(R\_{C,\chi},\ x^{(N\lambda-1)/o}-\zeta\_o^{,j}\big)\ \text{over }\mathbb{F}\_\lambda .$$

Then
$$n\_j=#{gC\in G/C:\ g^{-1}\sigma\_\ell^{,f\_o}g\in C,\ \chi(g^{-1}\sigma\_\ell^{,f\_o}g)=\zeta\_o^{,j}},\qquad \sum\_{j}\zeta\_o^{,j},n\_j=\mathrm{Ind}*C^G\chi,(\sigma*\ell^{,f\_o}).$$

*Proof.* Fix $\mathfrak{L}\mid\lambda$ in $N(\zeta\_o)$. Its Frobenius over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_o)$ is $\Phi=\phi^{f\_o}$ where $\phi=(\sigma\_\ell,\ell)\in\mathrm{Gal}(N(\zeta\_o)/\mathbb{Q})$ is the Frobenius of $\mathfrak{L}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ (as $\ell^{f\_o}\equiv1\bmod o$); $\Phi$ fixes $\zeta\_o$ and acts on the roots of $f$ as $\sigma\_\ell^{f\_o}$. Since $\Lambda$ has coefficients in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_o]$, $\Phi((g\Lambda)(\alpha))=(\Phi g\Phi^{-1}\cdot\Lambda)(\Phi\alpha)$, and on $N(\zeta\_o)$ conjugation by $\Phi$ and the action on roots both come from $\sigma\_\ell^{f\_o}\in G$: $\Phi((g\Lambda)(\alpha))=(\sigma\_\ell^{f\_o}g\Lambda)(\alpha)$. The roots of $R\_{C,\chi}$ modulo $\lambda$ are the distinct residues $\gamma\_g=(g\Lambda)(\alpha)^o\bmod\mathfrak{L}$ (distinct by the hypothesis on $\ell$), and $\gamma\_g\in\mathbb{F}*\lambda$ iff $\Phi\gamma\_g=\gamma\_g$ iff $(\sigma*\ell^{f\_o}g\Lambda)^o=(g\Lambda)^o$ iff $\sigma\_\ell^{f\_o}g\Lambda=\zeta\_o^{,j}g\Lambda$ for some $j$ iff $g^{-1}\sigma\_\ell^{f\_o}g\in C$ (Proposition 4.1(a)), with $\zeta\_o^{,j}=\chi(g^{-1}\sigma\_\ell^{f\_o}g)$. In that case $\gamma\_g^{(N\lambda-1)/o}\equiv(g\Lambda)^{N\lambda-1}=\Phi(g\Lambda)/(g\Lambda)=\zeta\_o^{,j}\bmod\mathfrak{L}$, and $\zeta\_o\bmod\lambda$ has exact order $o$ since $\ell\nmid o$; so $n\_j$ counts the stated cosets. The gcd formula holds because an $\mathbb{F}\_\lambda$-root $\gamma$ satisfies $\gamma^{N\lambda-1}=1$, so $\gamma^{(N\lambda-1)/o}\in\mu\_o$. Finally $\mathrm{Ind}*C^G\chi(x)=\frac1{|C|}\sum*{g\in G,\ g^{-1}xg\in C}\chi(g^{-1}xg)$, and the summand is constant on cosets $gC$. $\square$

Computationally: $x^{(N\lambda-1)/o}\bmod R\_{C,\chi}$ over $\mathbb{F}*\lambda$ by square-and-multiply, then one gcd per $j$ (or a single gcd of $R$ with $x^{N\lambda-1}-1$ followed by splitting by the values of $\gamma^{(N\lambda-1)/o}$); cost $\tilde O([G\:C]^2,f\_o\log\ell)$ operations in $\mathbb{F}*\lambda$. The excluded primes are those dividing $o\cdot\mathrm{disc}f\cdot N(\mathrm{disc}R\_{C,\chi})$; their number is at most $\log\_2$ of that integer, and $|N(\mathrm{disc}R\_{C,\chi})|\le(2B\_\Lambda^{,o})^{\varphi(o)G\:C}$ by [the precision policy](precision-policy.md) Lemma 1.2 applied in each embedding. At an excluded $\ell\nmid\mathrm{disc}f$ the same count is obtained $\lambda$-adically at [the precision policy](precision-policy.md) precision.

**Theorem 4.3 (Artin induction, character form).** The class functions $\mathrm{Ind}*C^G\chi$, $C$ cyclic, $\chi\in\widehat C$, span the space of all class functions of $G$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta*{\exp G})$. Consequently, for a fixed element $x\in G$, the values ${\mathrm{Ind}*C^G\chi(x)}*{C,\chi}$ determine the conjugacy class of $x$.

*Proof.* If a class function $\theta$ is orthogonal to every $\mathrm{Ind}\_C^G\chi$, Frobenius reciprocity gives $\langle\theta|\_C,\chi\rangle\_C=0$ for all $\chi\in\widehat C$, so $\theta|\_C=0$ for every cyclic $C$; taking $C=\langle g\rangle$ gives $\theta(g)=0$ for all $g$. So the $\mathrm{Ind}*C^G\chi$ span over $\mathbb{C}$; as their coordinates in the basis of class indicators lie in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta*{\exp G})$, they span over that field too (rank is field-independent). The indicator of the class of $x$ is a class function, hence a combination of the $\mathrm{Ind}\_C^G\chi$, hence computable from their values. $\square$

**Corollary 4.4.** Let $f\_M=o(\ell\bmod M)$ and work over $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_M]$ with a prime $\lambda\mid\ell$ (so every $\chi$ of every cyclic $C$ has values in $\mathbb{F}*\lambda$ and $f\_o$ is replaced by $f\_M$ uniformly). The counts $n\_j(C,\chi)$ over all cyclic $C$ and all $\chi\in\widehat C$ determine the conjugacy class of $\sigma*\ell^{,f\_M}$ in $G$ exactly. Using $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_{o(\chi)}]$ for each $\chi$ separately gives in addition $\mathrm{Ind}*C^G\chi(\sigma*\ell^{f\_{o(\chi)}})$ with the smaller exponent $f\_{o(\chi)}\mid f\_M$.

Only a spanning subfamily of $(C,\chi)$ is needed; as in [separating subgroups](separating-subgroups.md) §2 it is chosen by a weighted cover over the classes to be separated, with weight $[G\:C]$ and the twisted signatures in place of the permutation signatures.

## 5. What is determined, exactly

**Theorem 5.1.** Let $\ell\nmid M\cdot\mathrm{disc}f$, $t=\ell\bmod M$, $f=o(t)$, $\sigma=\sigma\_\ell$, $d=\gcd(o(\sigma),f)$. The data obtainable from coset actions and twisted resolvents (over $\mathbb{Q}$, over $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_o]$ for $o\mid M$, at any primes above $\ell$) are:

1. $t$, hence $\pi(\sigma)=\kappa(t)\in G^{ab}$;
2. the rational class of $(\sigma,t)$ in $G'$, i.e. $\sigma^G$ up to $\sigma\mapsto\sigma^k$ with $k\equiv1\pmod d$;
3. the class $(\sigma^{f})^G$ exactly, and more generally $\mathrm{Ind}*C^G\chi(\sigma^{f*{o(\chi)}})$ for all $(C,\chi)$.

Two candidates $\sigma,\sigma'$ are indistinguishable by all of them iff $\sigma'\sim\_G\sigma^k$ for some $k$ with $\gcd(k,o(\sigma))=1$, $k\equiv1\pmod d$, and $\mathrm{Ind}*C^G\chi(\sigma^{kf*{o(\chi)}})=\mathrm{Ind}*C^G\chi(\sigma^{f*{o(\chi)}})$ for all $(C,\chi)$; the last condition implies $\sigma^{kf}\sim\sigma^f$ and is implied by $\sigma^{kf\_{o}}\sim\sigma^{f\_o}$ for every $o\mid M$. In particular $\sigma^G$ is determined exactly when any of the following holds:

- (a) $o(\sigma)\mid f$ (Theorem 3.1);
- (b) $\gcd(f,o(\sigma))=1$, e.g. $\ell\equiv1\pmod M$: then $\sigma=(\sigma^f)^{f^{-1}\bmod o(\sigma)}$ and the class of $\sigma^f$ determines that of $\sigma$;
- (c) every $k\equiv1\pmod d$ prime to $o(\sigma)$ with $\sigma^{kf}\sim\sigma^{f}$ satisfies $\sigma^k\sim\sigma$.

The set of residues $t\in(\mathbb{Z}/M)^\times$ for which the class is exact for every $\sigma$ compatible with $t$ is computable from the table of $G$ before any prime is examined; the primes in the complementary residue classes are handled by the alignment of [the precision policy](precision-policy.md) §4.

*Proof.* Items 1 to 3 are Lemma 2.1, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 4.4. If $\sigma'$ satisfies the stated conditions, every datum agrees: 1 because $\pi(\sigma^k)=\pi(\sigma)^k=\pi(\sigma)$ as $o(\pi(\sigma))\mid d$; 2 by Theorem 3.1; 3 by hypothesis. Conversely, agreement of 2 forces $\sigma'\sim\sigma^k$ with $k\equiv1\pmod d$ (Theorem 3.1) and agreement of 3 is the displayed condition. (a),(b),(c) are the cases where the indistinguishability set reduces to $\sigma^G$. The residue set is computable because the conditions involve only $t$ through $f$ and the class structure of $G$ (power maps of [the character table](character-table.md)). $\square$

**Example 5.2 (residual ambiguity is real).** $G=Q\_{16}=\langle a,b\mid a^8=1,\ b^2=a^4,\ bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle$. The classes of $a$ and $a^3$ are ${a^{\pm1}}$ and ${a^{\pm3}}$, rationally conjugate, with the same image in $G^{ab}=C\_2\times C\_2$. Take $\ell$ with $\ell\equiv5\pmod8$ and $t=\ell\bmod M$ of order $f=2$ (such classes exist modulo $M$ since $5$ has order $2$ modulo $8$ and $\ell\equiv1\bmod c$ is compatible). Then $d=\gcd(8,2)=2$, $k=3\equiv1\pmod2$, and $\sigma^{kf}=a^6\sim a^2=\sigma^f$ (the order-$4$ elements $a^{\pm2}$ are conjugate). So neither the $G'$-rational class nor any twisted count separates $\mathrm{Frob}*\ell=a^{\pm1}$ from $a^{\pm3}$; both occur by Chebotarev, and the distinction is exactly the sign of $\mathrm{tr}\rho(\mathrm{Frob}*\ell)=\pm\sqrt2$ for a faithful two-dimensional $\rho$ with $\mathbb{Q}(\chi)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2)$. For $\ell\equiv1\pmod8$, case (b) with $o=8$ applies and the class is exact from the single twisted resolvent $R\_{\langle a\rangle,\chi}$, $\chi$ faithful.

**Remark 5.3 (why nothing finer exists).** Every quantity computed at a prime above $\ell$ from residues is a function of the Frobenius of that prime over the field of coefficients: over $\mathbb{Q}$ it is $\phi$ acting semilinearly on $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_o)$-points, over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_o)$ it is $\phi^{f\_o}$ acting linearly. Fixed-point and eigen-line counts of $\phi$ are unchanged under $\phi\mapsto\phi^k$ with $t^k=t$ and $\langle\phi^k\rangle=\langle\phi\rangle$ (they depend on the cyclic group generated and on $t$); counts for $\phi^{f\_o}$ are class functions of $\sigma^{f\_o}$. Theorem 5.1 is the closure of these two kinds of data.

## 6. Realization and precision

**Computing $R\_{C,\chi}$ from the $p$-adic roots.** Choose the numbering prime with $p\nmid M\cdot\mathrm{disc}f$ and an element $\omega\in\mathbb{Z}*{q'}$ of exact order $o$ ($q'$ a power of $p$ with $o\mid q'-1$; the roots of $f$ lie in $\mathbb{Z}q$, and one works in $\mathbb{Z}{\mathrm{lcm}(q,q')}$). Evaluate $\Lambda$ with $\zeta\_o\mapsto\omega$ at the numbered roots, form $R*{C,\chi}$ as a product over $G/C$ (product tree), and recover each coefficient $c=\sum\_{j<\varphi(o)}c\_j\zeta\_o^{,j}\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_o]$ from its image $\sum c\_j\omega^j$ modulo $p^{k\_p}$.

**Lemma 6.1 (coordinate bound).** If $c\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_o]$ has $|\tau(c)|\le B$ for every complex embedding $\tau$, then its coordinates in the power basis satisfy $|c\_j|\le\varphi(o)^{\varphi(o)/2}B$.

*Proof.* Cramer's rule on the system $\tau\_i(c)=\sum\_jc\_j\tau\_i(\zeta\_o)^j$: the Vandermonde matrix $V=(\tau\_i(\zeta\_o)^j)$ has $|\det V|=|\mathrm{disc},\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_o]|^{1/2}\ge1$, and Hadamard's bound for the determinant with column $j$ replaced by $(\tau\_i(c))\_i$ gives $\varphi(o)^{(\varphi(o)-1)/2}\cdot\varphi(o)^{1/2}B$. $\square$

So the coefficients are recovered exactly once $p^{k\_p}>2\varphi(o)^{\varphi(o)/2}(1+B\_\Lambda^{,o})^{[G\:C]}$; since $\omega$ is a $p$-adic unit and $1,\omega,\dots,\omega^{\varphi(o)-1}$ are linearly independent modulo $p$ (reduction of the cyclotomic polynomial is separable), the coordinates are determined by the residue. Enter $M\_{C,\chi}:=\big(2\varphi(o)^{\varphi(o)/2}(1+B\_\Lambda^{,o})^{[G\:C]}\big)^{\mu([G\:C])}$ into the list $\mathcal{F}$ of [the precision policy](precision-policy.md) §5; then $k\_\ell$ from [the precision policy](precision-policy.md) also covers the $\lambda$-adic root counts at excluded primes and the separation of the roots of $R\_{C,\chi}$ at every prime ([the precision policy](precision-policy.md) Lemma 2.1 applied to $\gamma\_g-\gamma\_{g'}$, whose conjugates are bounded by $2B\_\Lambda^{,o}$ and whose degree is at most $\varphi(o)G\:C$).

**Choosing $\lambda$.** Any prime $\lambda\mid\ell$ of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_o]$ may be used: $\lambda$ is the kernel of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_o]\to\mathbb{F}*{\ell^{f\_o}}$, $\zeta\_o\mapsto$ any element of exact order $o$; replacing $\lambda$ by its Galois conjugate $\lambda^y$ conjugates $\Phi$ by a lift of $y$ in $\mathrm{Gal}(N(\zeta\_o)/\mathbb{Q})$, whose first coordinate is an element of $G$, so the class of $\sigma*\ell^{f\_o}$ and the counts $n\_j$ (with $\zeta\_o^{,j}$ read in the new residue map) are unchanged. In practice: factor the $o$-th cyclotomic polynomial over $\mathbb{F}*\ell$, take one irreducible factor of degree $f\_o$ as the defining polynomial of $\mathbb{F}*\lambda$, and reduce $R\_{C,\chi}$ coefficientwise.

**Independence from the choice of Frobenius element.** $\sigma\_\ell$ is defined up to $G$-conjugacy; the data of Theorem 5.1 are conjugation invariant, and the pinned map $\iota$ (Proposition 1.3) is what makes the second coordinate $t$ comparable with the first coordinate $\sigma$ in the same numbering.

**Placement in the pipeline.** Before the loop over $\ell\le X$: compute $c$, $U$, $\iota$ (Propositions 1.2 to 1.3), $G'$ (Proposition 1.4), the residue classes modulo $M$ where Theorem 5.1 gives exactness, the twisted resolvents for a spanning family of $(C,\chi)$ with their discriminants and excluded primes, and the augmented $\mathcal{F}$. At each $\ell$: the block and rational class from [rational classes](rational-classes.md) to [separating subgroups](separating-subgroups.md); $t=\ell\bmod M$; if $\ell$ lies in an exact residue class, the twisted counts (Theorem 4.2) and the $G'$-tests, then the class by Theorem 5.1; otherwise the alignment of [the precision policy](precision-policy.md) §4. The alignment is thus needed only on the explicitly known set of residue classes modulo $M$ where Theorem 5.1(c) fails for some candidate $\sigma$, which is empty for many groups (all $G$ with rational character table, all $G$ in which every element is conjugate to all its powers $\sigma^k$ with $k\equiv1$ modulo $\gcd(o(\sigma),f)$ for the $f$ that occur) and is a proper subset for the rest.
