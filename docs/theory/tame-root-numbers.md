# The local root number at a tamely ramified prime

**Setting.** $\ell$ ramified with $(D\_\ell)*1=1$ (tame): $D=D*\ell$, $I=I\_\ell=(D\_\ell)*0$ cyclic of order $e$ prime to $\ell$, $f=[D\:I]$, $D/I=\langle\phi I\rangle$ with $\phi$ a Frobenius lift (*[*Euler factors*](euler-factors.md) *§1). $L=N*\mathfrak{L}$, $k\_L=\mathbb{F}*{q\_L}$, $q\_L=\ell^f$, $L^{\mathrm{ur}}=\mathbb{Q}*{\ell^f}$. $\theta\_0\:I\to\mu\_e\subseteq k\_L^\times$, $\sigma\mapsto\sigma\varpi\_L/\varpi\_L\bmod\mathfrak{m}*L$, is an isomorphism (*[*the filtration*](ramification-filtration.md) *Prop. 1.2). $\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)$, $V=V*\chi$, $\rho=\rho\_\chi|*D$. The quantity to compute is the local $\varepsilon$-factor $\varepsilon*\ell(\chi):=\varepsilon(\rho,\psi\_\ell,dx\_\ell)$ at $s=\tfrac12$ (a number of modulus one for unitary $\rho$), whose product over all places is the global root number $W(\chi)$ of [the character table](character-table.md) Lemma 5.3.

**Conventions (fixed once, consistently with** [**the archimedean place**](archimedean.md)**).** $\psi\_\ell(x)=e^{2\pi i{x}*\ell}$ on $\mathbb{Q}*\ell$ (${x}*\ell\in\mathbb{Z}[1/\ell]/\mathbb{Z}$ the fractional part), $\psi*\infty(x)=e^{-2\pi ix}$, so that $\prod\_v\psi\_v$ is trivial on $\mathbb{Q}$; for a finite extension $F/\mathbb{Q}*\ell$, $\psi\_F=\psi*\ell\circ\mathrm{Tr}*{F/\mathbb{Q}*\ell}$; $dx$ self-dual. With these, $W(\chi)=\prod\_v\varepsilon\_v(\chi)$, $\varepsilon\_\infty(\chi)=i^{-b\_\chi}$ ([the archimedean place](archimedean.md)), and for a primitive Dirichlet character $\psi$ of conductor $q$ the finite root number is $\tau(\psi)/\sqrt q$ ([the archimedean place](archimedean.md) Prop. 4.1). The local reciprocity map $\mathrm{rec}\_F\:F^\times\to\mathrm{Gal}(F^{ab}/F)$ is normalized so that uniformizers map to arithmetic Frobenius elements ($x\mapsto x^{q\_F}$ on residues).

## 1. Structure of the tame decomposition group and of $\mathrm{Res}\_D\chi$

**Lemma 1.1.** $\phi\sigma\phi^{-1}=\sigma^{\ell}$ for $\sigma\in I$; hence $D$ is metacyclic, generated by $I$ and $\phi$, and the Frobenius action on the characters $\theta$ of $I$ is $\theta\mapsto\theta^{\phi}:=\theta\circ\mathrm{Ad}*{\phi^{-1}}=\theta^{\ell^{-1}}$. For $\theta\in\widehat I$ let $f*\theta$ be the length of its orbit ($=$ the order of $\ell$ modulo $\mathrm{ord},\theta$), $D\_\theta=I\langle\phi^{f\_\theta}\rangle$ its stabilizer, $F\_\theta=L^{D\_\theta}=\mathbb{Q}*{\ell^{f*\theta}}$ (unramified, $q\_{F\_\theta}=\ell^{f\_\theta}$).

*Proof.* $\theta\_0(\phi\sigma\phi^{-1})=\phi(\theta\_0(\sigma))=\theta\_0(\sigma)^{\ell}$ since $\phi$ acts on $k\_L$ as $x\mapsto x^\ell$ and $\theta\_0$ is $D$-equivariant for the conjugation action ($\phi\varpi\_L$ is again a uniformizer and $\theta\_0$ does not depend on the uniformizer, [the filtration](ramification-filtration.md) Prop. 1.2). $\square$

**Lemma 1.2.** Every $\theta\in\widehat I$ extends to a character $\tilde\theta$ of $D\_\theta$ ($D\_\theta/I$ is cyclic), the extensions are $\tilde\theta\mu$ with $\mu\in\widehat{D\_\theta/I}$, and $\mathrm{Irr}(D)={\pi\_{\theta,\mu}=\mathrm{Ind}*{D*\theta}^D\tilde\theta\mu}$ with $\theta$ up to Frobenius orbit. For $\chi$: $\mathrm{Res}*D\chi=\sum*{O,\mu}n\_{O,\mu}\pi\_{\theta\_O,\mu}$, $n\_{O,\mu}=\langle\chi|*{D*\theta},\tilde\theta\mu\rangle\_{D\_\theta}$, and $\mathrm{Res}*I\chi=\sum*\theta n\_\theta,\theta$ with $n\_\theta=\langle\chi|*I,\theta\rangle=\sum*\mu n\_{O(\theta),\mu}$, constant on orbits; $V\_\theta$ denotes the $\theta$-isotypic subspace of $V$, of dimension $n\_\theta$, stable under $D\_\theta$, and as a $D\_\theta$-module $V\_\theta\cong\bigoplus\_\mu n\_{O,\mu},\tilde\theta\mu$.

*Proof.* Clifford theory for the normal abelian subgroup $I$ with cyclic quotient (Isaacs 6.11, 11.22): invariant characters extend over cyclic quotients, the inertia group is $D\_\theta$, and induction from $D\_\theta$ of the extensions gives all irreducibles; the $\theta$-isotypic part of $\pi\_{\theta,\mu}$ is the line $\tilde\theta\mu$. $\square$

All of this is read from the table and the class identification of the elements of $D$ ([Euler factors](euler-factors.md) §4), once the characters $\theta$ of $I$ are made concrete (§3).

## 2. Reduction to Gauss sums

**Proposition 2.1 (inductivity, unramified base).** For $\theta\ne1$ let $\xi\_{\theta,\mu}:=\tilde\theta\mu\circ\mathrm{rec}*{F*\theta}$, a tamely ramified character of $F\_\theta^\times$ of conductor exponent $1$. Then
$$\varepsilon\big(\pi\_{\theta,\mu},\psi\_\ell\big)=\varepsilon\big(\xi\_{\theta,\mu},\psi\_{F\_\theta}\big),\qquad\varepsilon\big(\pi\_{1,\mu},\psi\_\ell\big)=1 .$$

*Proof.* Local constants are inductive in degree $0$; for induction from $F=F\_\theta$ to $\mathbb{Q}*\ell$ the defect is the Langlands constant $\lambda(F/\mathbb{Q}*\ell,\psi\_\ell)=\varepsilon(\mathrm{Ind}*F^{\mathbb{Q}*\ell}\mathbf{1},\psi\_\ell)/\varepsilon(\mathbf{1},\psi\_F)$, and for $F/\mathbb{Q}*\ell$ unramified and $\psi*\ell$ of conductor $\mathbb{Z}*\ell$ this is $1$: $\mathrm{Ind},\mathbf{1}$ is a sum of unramified characters and $\varepsilon(\eta,\psi)=\eta(c)$ with $v(c)=a(\eta)+n(\psi)=0$, so every factor is $1$. Hence $\varepsilon(\mathrm{Ind}*{D\_\theta}^D\tilde\theta\mu,\psi\_\ell)=\varepsilon(\tilde\theta\mu,\psi\_F)$, and by local class field theory the right side is $\varepsilon(\xi\_{\theta,\mu},\psi\_F)$. For $\theta=1$, $\xi$ is unramified and the same evaluation gives $1$. $\square$

**Proposition 2.2 (Tate).** For a character $\xi$ of $F^\times$ with conductor exponent $a(\xi)=1$, $F/\mathbb{Q}*\ell$ unramified with residue field $k\_F$ of order $q$, and $\xi\_0:=\xi|*{\mathcal{O}\_F^\times}$ regarded as a character of $k\_F^\times$:

$$\varepsilon(\xi,\psi\_F)=\xi(\ell)\cdot\frac{g(\bar\xi\_0)}{\sqrt q},\qquad g(\bar\xi\_0):=\sum\_{u\in k\_F^\times}\bar\xi\_0(u),e^{2\pi i,\mathrm{Tr}*{k\_F/\mathbb{F}*\ell}(u)/\ell},$$

and $|g(\bar\xi\_0)|=\sqrt q$, so $|\varepsilon(\xi,\psi\_F)|=1$.

*Proof.* Tate's formula: $\varepsilon(\xi,\psi\_F)=\xi(c),q^{-a/2}\sum\_{u\in(\mathcal{O}*F/\mathfrak{p}^a)^\times}\xi^{-1}(u)\psi\_F(u/c)$ for any $c$ with $v\_F(c)=a(\xi)+n(\psi\_F)$; here $n(\psi\_F)=0$, $a=1$, $c=\ell$, and $\psi\_F(u/\ell)=e^{2\pi i{\mathrm{Tr}*{F/\mathbb{Q}*\ell}(u)/\ell}*\ell}=e^{2\pi i,\mathrm{Tr}*{k\_F/\mathbb{F}*\ell}(\bar u)/\ell}$. The modulus: $|g|^2=\sum\_{u,v}\bar\xi\_0(u)\xi\_0(v)e^{2\pi i\mathrm{Tr}(u-v)/\ell}=\sum\_{t\in k\_F^\times}\bar\xi\_0(t)\sum\_{v\in k\_F^\times}e^{2\pi i\mathrm{Tr}(v(t-1))/\ell}$ (substituting $u=tv$); the inner sum is $q-1$ for $t=1$ and $-1$ for $t\ne1$ (trace is surjective, additive characters of $k\_F$ sum to $0$), so $|g|^2=(q-1)+\sum\_{t\ne1}\bar\xi\_0(t)\cdot(-1)=(q-1)-(0-1)=q$ using $\sum\_{t}\bar\xi\_0(t)=0$ for $\xi\_0\ne1$. Finally $|\xi(\ell)|=1$ since $\xi$ has finite order. $\square$

**Theorem 2.3 (the monomial).** Let $\Phi\_\theta:=\mathrm{rec}*{F*\theta}(\ell)$, an element of $\mathrm{Gal}(L\cap F\_\theta^{ab}/F\_\theta)=D\_\theta^{ab}$ (§3 makes it explicit as a class $\Phi\_\theta\ker$ inside $D\_\theta$). Then

$$\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)=\prod\_{O\ni\theta\ne1}\ \det!\big(\rho\_\chi(\Phi\_\theta),\big|,V\_\theta\big)\cdot\Big(\frac{g(\bar\theta\_F)}{\sqrt{q\_{F\_\theta}}}\Big)^{n\_\theta},$$

the product over the Frobenius orbits $O$ of nontrivial characters of $I$ occurring in $\mathrm{Res}*I\chi$, one representative $\theta$ per orbit, where $\theta\_F:=\theta\circ\mathrm{rec}*{F\_\theta}|*{\mathcal{O}*{F\_\theta}^\times}$ as a character of $k\_{F\_\theta}^\times$. Each factor has modulus one, hence $|\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)|=1$. The first factor is the "unramified twist" and the second the Gauss-sum part.

*Proof.* Additivity of $\varepsilon$ in direct sums, Lemma 1.2 and Propositions 2.1 to 2.2 give $\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)=\prod\_{O,\mu}\big(\xi\_{\theta,\mu}(\ell),g(\bar\theta\_F)/\sqrt{q\_F}\big)^{n\_{O,\mu}}$, since $(\xi\_{\theta,\mu})*0=\theta\circ\mathrm{rec}F|{\mathcal{O}^\times}=\theta\_F$ does not depend on $\mu$. Grouping: $\sum*\mu n\_{O,\mu}=n\_\theta$, and $\prod\_\mu\big(\tilde\theta\mu(\Phi\_\theta)\big)^{n\_{O,\mu}}=\det(\rho\_\chi(\Phi\_\theta)|V\_\theta)$ by the $D\_\theta$-decomposition of $V\_\theta$ in Lemma 1.2. $\square$

The determinant is a character-theoretic quantity: $\det(\rho\_\chi(\Phi\_\theta)|V\_\theta)=\prod\_{\eta}\eta(\Phi\_\theta)^{\langle\chi|*{D*\theta},\eta\rangle}$ over the extensions $\eta$ of $\theta$ to $D\_\theta$, and it is well defined on the class of $\Phi\_\theta$ modulo $\bigcap\_\eta\ker\eta$, in particular modulo $\ker\theta\cap[D\_\theta,D\_\theta]$-type subgroups: replacing $\Phi\_\theta$ by $\Phi\_\theta\kappa$ with $\kappa$ acting trivially on $V\_\theta$ changes nothing. It can also be evaluated on the model of [the character table](character-table.md) by restricting $\rho(\Phi\_\theta)$ to the image of the projector $e\_\theta=|I|^{-1}\sum\_{\sigma\in I}\bar\theta(\sigma)\rho(\sigma)$ and taking the $m$-th root of the determinant when the model has trace $m\chi$ (unique among roots of unity of the right order only up to $\mu\_m$; the character formula is used, the model as a check on $|\cdot|$ and on the $m$-th power).

## 3. Making $\theta$, $\theta\_F$ and $\Phi\_\theta$ explicit

**Identification of the characters of $I$.** Choose a group isomorphism $\iota:\mu\_{q\_L-1}(k\_L)\to\mu\_{q\_L-1}(\mathbb{C})$ (equivalently a prime of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta\_{q\_L-1}]$ above $\ell$, or the Teichmüller map followed by an embedding). The characters of $I$ are $\theta\_j(\sigma)=\iota(\theta\_0(\sigma))^{,j}$, $j\in\mathbb{Z}/e$. The values $\theta\_0(\sigma)$ are the residual data of [the filtration](ramification-filtration.md) §3: for $H=1$ the residual polynomial of the segment $J=1$ of the ramification polygon of $L$ has roots $\theta\_0(\sigma)-1$, $\sigma\in I\setminus1$, and $\theta\_0(\sigma)$ is also directly $\sigma\varpi\_L/\varpi\_L$ reduced. Then $n\_{\theta\_j}=\langle\chi|*I,\theta\_j\rangle=\frac1e\sum*{\sigma\in I}\chi(\sigma)\iota(\theta\_0(\sigma))^{-j}$ from the table, and the orbit structure is $j\mapsto\ell^{-1}j$ (Lemma 1.1). The final $\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)$ does not depend on $\iota$: changing $\iota$ relabels the $\theta\_j$ by a Galois automorphism of the coefficients while the multiplicities $n\_\theta$, the Gauss sums and the determinants are all computed with the same $\iota$, and the representation $\rho\_\chi|\_D$ is intrinsic.

**A good uniformizer.** Since $L/L^{\mathrm{ur}}$ is tamely totally ramified of degree $e$ and $\mu\_e\subseteq L^{\mathrm{ur}}$, one may take $\varpi\_L$ with $\varpi\_L^{,e}=\omega\ell$ for a root of unity $\omega\in\mu\_{q\_L-1}(L^{\mathrm{ur}})$ (Kummer theory over $L^{\mathrm{ur}}$: every such extension is $L^{\mathrm{ur}}(\sqrt[e]{\pi})$ with $\pi$ a uniformizer of $L^{\mathrm{ur}}$, and $U^{(1)}(L^{\mathrm{ur}})$ is $e$-divisible). Concretely: from any uniformizer $\varpi$ of the tower ([local Galois groups](local-galois-groups.md)), $\varpi^e/\ell$ is a unit $u$ of $L$; the Teichmüller representative $\omega$ of $\bar u$ lies in $L^{\mathrm{ur}}$ and $u/\omega\in U^{(1)}(L)$ has an $e$-th root $w$ by Hensel; $\varpi\_L:=\varpi/w$. Then $\theta\_0(\sigma)=\sigma\varpi\_L/\varpi\_L$ exactly (not only modulo $\mathfrak{m}$), for every $\sigma\in I$.

**Proposition 3.1 (tame reciprocity for $F=F\_\theta$, via Lubin to Tate).** Let $F=F\_\theta$, $q=q\_F=\ell^{f\_\theta}$, $m'=\mathrm{ord},\theta$. Then $m'\mid q-1$: $\theta$ extends to $D\_\theta$, hence factors through $\mathrm{rec}\_F(\mathcal{O}*F^\times)$, which is tame, i.e. a quotient of $k\_F^\times$. Let $\lambda\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}*\ell$ satisfy $\lambda^{q-1}=-\ell$ (a level-one torsion point of the Lubin to Tate group of $F$ for the uniformizer $\ell$, with $\ell=\ell X+X^{q}$), so that $F^{\mathrm{ur}}(\lambda)$ is the maximal tamely ramified abelian extension of $F$. Then, on $F^{\mathrm{ur}}(\lambda)$:

$$\mathrm{rec}*F(\ell)\big|*{F^{\mathrm{ur}}}=\mathrm{Frob}\_F,\quad\mathrm{rec}\_F(\ell)(\lambda)=\lambda;\qquad\mathrm{rec}*F(u)\big|*{F^{\mathrm{ur}}}=\mathrm{id},\quad\mathrm{rec}\_F(u)(\lambda)=\omega(u)^{-1}\lambda\ \ (u\in\mathcal{O}\_F^\times),$$

$\omega(u)$ the Teichmüller lift of $\bar u$. Writing $\varpi\_L^{,e/m'}=\lambda^{(q-1)/m'}\cdot r$ with $r\in\mu(F^{\mathrm{ur}})$, $r^{m'}=-\omega$, the consequences inside $L$ are:

1. For $u\in\mathcal{O}\_F^\times$, $\mathrm{rec}*F(u)$ is the element $\sigma\_u\in I$ (unique modulo $\ker\theta=\theta\_0^{-1}(\mu*{e/m'})$) with

$$\theta\_0(\sigma\_u)^{,e/m'}=\bar u^{,-(q-1)/m'}\in\mu\_{m'}(k\_L),\qquad\text{hence}\qquad\theta\_F(\bar u)=\theta(\sigma\_u)=\iota\big(\bar u^{,-(q-1)/m'}\big)^{,j,m'/e}\ \text{ for }\theta=\theta\_j .$$

2. $\Phi\_\theta=\mathrm{rec}*F(\ell)$ is the element of the Frobenius coset $\phi^{f*\theta}I$ of $D\_\theta$ (unique modulo $\ker\theta$, which suffices for $\det(\rho|V\_\theta)$ after also fixing its action on $L^{\mathrm{ur}}$, i.e. modulo $\ker\theta\cap\ker(D\_\theta\to\mathrm{Gal}(L^{\mathrm{ur}}/F))=\ker\theta$) characterized by

$$\Phi\_\theta\big|\_{L^{\mathrm{ur}}}=\mathrm{Frob}*F\ (x\mapsto x^{q}\text{ on residues}),\qquad\Phi*\theta\big(\varpi\_L^{,e/m'}\big)=\varpi\_L^{,e/m'}\cdot r^{,q-1}.$$

Concretely: take any $\Phi\in\phi^{f\_\theta}I$ ([Euler factors](euler-factors.md) §1) and compute $\eta\_\Phi:=\Phi(\varpi\_L^{e/m'})/\varpi\_L^{e/m'}$, a root of unity in $L^{\mathrm{ur}}$ with $\eta\_\Phi^{m'}=\omega^{q-1}$. For $\sigma\in I$, $\Phi\sigma(\varpi\_L^{e/m'})=\Phi\big(\theta\_0(\sigma)^{e/m'}\varpi\_L^{e/m'}\big)=\theta\_0(\sigma)^{(e/m')q},\eta\_\Phi,\varpi\_L^{e/m'}=\theta\_0(\sigma)^{e/m'}\eta\_\Phi,\varpi\_L^{e/m'}$, because $\Phi$ raises roots of unity of $L^{\mathrm{ur}}$ to the $q$-th power and $\theta\_0(\sigma)^{e/m'}\in\mu\_{m'}$ with $m'\mid q-1$. So $\Phi\_\theta=\Phi\sigma$ for the $\sigma\in I$ (unique modulo $\ker\theta$) with $\theta\_0(\sigma)^{e/m'}=r^{,q-1}\eta\_\Phi^{-1}$.

*Proof.* The description of $\mathrm{rec}*F$ on $F^{\mathrm{ur}}F*\ell$ is the Lubin to Tate theorem (Milne, *CFT* I.3; Serre in Cassels to Fröhlich VI §3): $\mathrm{rec}*F(\ell)$ acts as Frobenius on $F^{\mathrm{ur}}$ and trivially on $F*\ell$, and $\mathrm{rec}*F(u)$ trivially on $F^{\mathrm{ur}}$ and by $[u^{-1}]$ on $F*\ell$; on level-one torsion $[u]$ acts by multiplication by $\omega(u)$ because $\zeta=\zeta X$ for roots of unity $\zeta$ and $[u\_1]$ acts trivially on level one for $u\_1\in U^{(1)}$. The tame part of $F\_\ell$ is $F(\lambda)$ with $\lambda^{q-1}=-\ell$ (roots of $\ell/X$). Item 1: $\mathrm{rec}*F(u)$ multiplies $\lambda^{(q-1)/m'}$ by $\omega(u)^{-(q-1)/m'}$ and fixes $r\in F^{\mathrm{ur}}$, so it multiplies $\varpi\_L^{e/m'}$ by $\omega(u)^{-(q-1)/m'}$; on the other hand $\sigma(\varpi\_L^{e/m'})/\varpi\_L^{e/m'}=\theta\_0(\sigma)^{e/m'}$ (Teichmüller lift), which identifies $\theta\_0(\sigma\_u)^{e/m'}$; since $\theta=\theta\_j$ is $\iota\circ\theta\_0^{,j}$ and $\theta\_0(\sigma\_u)^{e/m'}$ determines $\theta\_0(\sigma\_u)$ modulo $\mu*{e/m'}=\theta\_0(\ker\theta)$, the value $\theta(\sigma\_u)=\iota(\theta\_0(\sigma\_u))^j=\iota(\theta\_0(\sigma\_u)^{e/m'})^{jm'/e}$ is well defined ($jm'/e\in\mathbb{Z}$ because $m'=\mathrm{ord},\theta\_j=e/\gcd(j,e)$). Item 2: $\mathrm{rec}*F(\ell)$ fixes $\lambda$ and maps $r\mapsto r^{q}$ (Frobenius on $F^{\mathrm{ur}}$), so it maps $\varpi\_L^{e/m'}=\lambda^{(q-1)/m'}r$ to $\lambda^{(q-1)/m'}r^{q}=\varpi\_L^{e/m'}r^{q-1}$; the correction computation is as displayed. Uniqueness modulo $\ker\theta$: two elements of $D*\theta$ with the same action on $L^{\mathrm{ur}}$ and on $\varpi\_L^{e/m'}$ differ by an element of $I$ acting trivially on $\varpi\_L^{e/m'}$, i.e. of $\theta\_0^{-1}(\mu\_{e/m'})=\ker\theta$. $\square$

**Remark (what the Frobenius lift class supplies).** [Euler factors](euler-factors.md) supplies the coset $\phi^{f\_\theta}I$; Proposition 3.1(2) selects inside it the element $\Phi\_\theta$ modulo $\ker\theta$ through a single evaluation $\eta\_\Phi$ in the tower. The determinant $\det(\rho\_\chi(\Phi\_\theta)|V\_\theta)$ is then a product of values of characters of $D\_\theta$ at $\Phi\_\theta$, each a root of unity, obtained from the table by identifying the class of $\Phi\_\theta$ in $G$ and of its powers times elements of $I$ ([Euler factors](euler-factors.md) §4); the correction $\sigma$ is what makes it independent of the arbitrary lift.

## 4. Checks

1. $|\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)|=1$ (Theorem 2.3), and $\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_\ell,\zeta\_{e'})$ for $e'=\mathrm{lcm}$ of the orders of the $\theta$ and the $\eta$ involved, an algebraic number of modulus one that can be kept exact or evaluated to any precision.
2. $\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi),\varepsilon\_\ell(\bar\chi)=\det\rho\_\chi\big(\mathrm{rec}*{\mathbb{Q}*\ell}(-1)\big)=\lambda\_\chi(\sigma\_{-1})$ with $\sigma\_{-1}\in I$ given by Proposition 3.1(1) for $F=\mathbb{Q}*\ell$, $u=-1$; for real-valued $\chi$ this fixes $\varepsilon*\ell(\chi)^2$.
3. For $\chi$ one-dimensional, $\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)$ must equal the $\ell$-component root number of the Dirichlet character $\chi\_\lambda$ of [cyclotomic refinement](cyclotomic-refinement.md), $\tau(\chi\_{\lambda,\ell})/\sqrt\ell$ times the unramified factor $\chi\_{\lambda}'(\ell)^{-1}$ of the complementary component (the product formula for the Hecke character, as in [the archimedean place](archimedean.md) Prop. 4.1's proof); this tests the sign of the Lubin to Tate normalization and the choice of $\iota$ in the simplest case.
4. Galois equivariance: $\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi^\tau)=\tau'(\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi))$ for $\tau'$ extending $\tau$ to $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_\ell,\zeta\_{e'})$ and fixing $\zeta\_\ell$, the Gauss sums transform by $g(\bar\theta^\tau)$ and the determinants by $\tau$, while $\psi\_\ell$ is fixed.
5. The global functional equation with $W(\chi)=\varepsilon\_\infty(\chi)\prod\_\ell\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)$ ([the character table](character-table.md) Lemma 5.3): with all tame $\varepsilon\_\ell$ from this note and the unramified ones equal to $1$, a polynomial $f$ whose ramified primes are all tame yields $W(\chi)$ exactly, and the numerical verification of [the character table](character-table.md) then has no free parameter; a failure isolates a wrong sign in Proposition 3.1 (which would replace $g(\bar\theta\_F)$ by $g(\theta\_F)=\theta\_F(-1)\overline{g(\bar\theta\_F)}$) or a wrong $\Phi\_\theta$.

## 5. Scope and cost

Wildly ramified primes ($(D\_\ell)*1\ne1$) are outside this note: there Deligne's local constants require either the Brauer-induction descent to characters of subgroups of $D*\ell$ with Tate's formula at higher conductor exponents (Gauss sums over $(\mathcal{O}*F/\mathfrak{p}^a)^\times$, $a\ge2$, with the additive character evaluated through a Lubin to Tate-corrected uniformizer) or the numerical determination of* [*the character table*](character-table.md) *Lemma 5.3 from the candidates $w\_0\mu\_m$. Per tame prime and character: the Gauss sums cost $O(q*{F\_\theta})$ operations in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_\ell,\zeta\_{m'})$ per orbit, at most $e$ orbits; the reciprocity corrections cost one evaluation of $\Phi(\varpi\_L^{e/m'})$ in the tower per orbit; the determinants cost class identifications of the elements $\Phi\_\theta\sigma$, $\sigma\in I$, already available from [Euler factors](euler-factors.md).
