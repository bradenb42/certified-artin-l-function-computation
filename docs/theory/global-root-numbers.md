# The global root number and its independent evaluations for self-dual characters

*One of the derivations behind* [*artin*](https://claude.ai/README.md)*; the* [*index*](https://claude.ai/chat/README.md) *lists them all and names the code that implements each.*

**Setting.** $\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)$ with representation $\rho=\rho\_\chi$; $\varepsilon\_\infty(\chi)=i^{-b\_\chi}$ ([the archimedean place](https://claude.ai/chat/archimedean.md)), $\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)=1$ at unramified $\ell$, $\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)$ from [tame root numbers](https://claude.ai/chat/tame-root-numbers.md) (tame) or [wild root numbers](https://claude.ai/chat/wild-root-numbers.md) (wild), all with the additive characters $\psi\_\ell(x)=e^{2\pi i{x}*\ell}$, $\psi*\infty(x)=e^{-2\pi ix}$ and self-dual measures. $\lambda\_\chi=\det\rho$ ([the global conductor](https://claude.ai/chat/global-conductor.md)). $\nu\_2(\chi)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum\_{g\in G}\chi(g^2)$ is the Frobenius to Schur indicator, computed from the table and the power maps of [the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md); $\chi$ is *self-dual* iff $\chi=\bar\chi$ iff $\nu\_2(\chi)\ne0$, *orthogonal* if $\nu\_2=+1$, *symplectic* if $\nu\_2=-1$.

## 1. Definition, normalization, modulus

**Definition.** $W(\chi):=\varepsilon\_\infty(\chi)\prod\_{\ell}\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)$, a finite product over the ramified primes and $\infty$.

**Proposition 1.1.** (a) $|W(\chi)|=1$. (b) With $\Lambda(s,\chi)=\mathfrak{f}(\chi)^{s/2}\gamma\_\chi(s)L(s,\chi)$ ([the global conductor](https://claude.ai/chat/global-conductor.md), [the archimedean place](https://claude.ai/chat/archimedean.md)), the functional equation reads $\Lambda(s,\chi)=W(\chi),\Lambda(1-s,\bar\chi)$: $W(\chi)$ is the constant of the equation verified in [the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md). (c) $W(\bar\chi)=\overline{W(\chi)}$ and $W(\chi)W(\bar\chi)=1$. (d) $W(\chi)$ is independent of the choices of [local Galois groups](https://claude.ai/chat/local-galois-groups.md) to [well-definedness](https://claude.ai/chat/well-definedness.md) and of the Brauer decompositions of [wild root numbers](https://claude.ai/chat/wild-root-numbers.md).

*Proof.* (a) Each factor has modulus one ([the archimedean place](https://claude.ai/chat/archimedean.md) §4, [tame root numbers](https://claude.ai/chat/tame-root-numbers.md) Thm. 2.3, [wild root numbers](https://claude.ai/chat/wild-root-numbers.md) Thm. 3.1). (b) Artin to Tate: with $\prod\_v\psi\_v$ trivial on $\mathbb{Q}$ and Tamagawa measure, the global constant is $\varepsilon(s,\rho)=\prod\_v\varepsilon(\rho\_v,\psi\_v,dx\_v)$ and $\Lambda\_0(s,\chi):=\gamma\_\chi(s)L(s,\chi)$ satisfies $\Lambda\_0(s,\chi)=\varepsilon(s,\rho)\Lambda\_0(1-s,\bar\chi)$; the $s$-dependence of the local constants at finite $v$ is $\varepsilon(\rho\_v,\psi\_v),\ell^{f\_\ell(\chi)(1/2-s)}$ and at $\infty$ none, so $\varepsilon(s,\rho)=\mathfrak{f}(\chi)^{1/2-s}\prod\_v\varepsilon\_v(\chi)$; multiplying $\Lambda\_0$ by $\mathfrak{f}(\chi)^{s/2}$ turns $\mathfrak{f}^{1/2-s}\mathfrak{f}^{s/2}$ into $\mathfrak{f}^{(1-s)/2}$ and leaves $W(\chi)=\prod\_v\varepsilon\_v(\chi)$. (c) Locally $\varepsilon(\rho\_v,\psi\_v)\varepsilon(\rho\_v^\vee,\psi\_v)=\det\rho\_v(-1)$ (Deligne 5.4/5.5; at $\infty$: $i^{-b}\cdot i^{-b}=(-1)^b=\det\rho(c)$), and $\rho^\vee$ has character $\bar\chi$; taking the product over $v$, $\prod\_v\det\rho(\mathrm{rec}\_v(-1))=\det\rho(\mathrm{rec}(-1))=1$ by global reciprocity ($-1\in\mathbb{Q}^\times$ is in the kernel of the global Artin map). Together with $|W|=1$ this gives $W(\bar\chi)=W(\chi)^{-1}=\overline{W(\chi)}$. (d) [well-definedness](https://claude.ai/chat/well-definedness.md) Theorem 3.3 and Deligne's uniqueness ([wild root numbers](https://claude.ai/chat/wild-root-numbers.md) Thm. 3.1). $\square$

**Corollary 1.2.** If $\chi$ is self-dual then $W(\chi)\in{+1,-1}$; if $\chi$ is not self-dual, $W(\chi)$ is a unit complex number in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_{4\cdot\exp G\cdot\prod\_{\ell\text{ ram}}\ell})$ with $W(\bar\chi)=\overline{W(\chi)}$, and no sign statement is available.

*Proof.* $\chi=\bar\chi$ in (c). $\square$

The three shortcuts below give a second, independent value of $W(\chi)$ for self-dual $\chi$; each has a hypothesis, stated with it, and the table in §4 says which characters are compared against which.

## 2. Degree one, real-valued

**Proposition 2.1.** If $\chi$ is a linear character with $\chi=\bar\chi$, i.e. $\chi^2=1$ ($\chi$ trivial or quadratic), then $W(\chi)=1$.

*Proof.* $\chi=\mathbf{1}$: all local factors are $1$. $\chi$ quadratic, corresponding to the primitive quadratic Dirichlet character $\psi$ of conductor $q=\mathfrak{f}(\chi)$ with $\psi(-1)=(-1)^\kappa$: $W(\chi)=\tau(\psi)/(i^\kappa\sqrt q)$ ([the archimedean place](https://claude.ai/chat/archimedean.md) Prop. 4.1) and Gauss's evaluation $\tau(\psi)=i^{\kappa}\sqrt q$ for quadratic $\psi$ gives $1$. $\square$

This is the degree-one case of the orthogonal theorem of §3 (and of Fröhlich to Queyrut, which concerns quadratic characters of number fields trivial on the base ideles). Its local content: $\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)$ from [tame root numbers](https://claude.ai/chat/tame-root-numbers.md)/[wild root numbers](https://claude.ai/chat/wild-root-numbers.md) restricted to the one-dimensional case must multiply with $i^{-\kappa}$ to $1$; a stronger test is local, $\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)=\tau(\psi\_\ell)/\sqrt{\ell^{a}}$ componentwise ([tame root numbers](https://claude.ai/chat/tame-root-numbers.md) §4 check 3).

## 3. Orthogonal characters: Deligne's theorem

Let $\chi$ be orthogonal ($\nu\_2=+1$), so $\rho$ is realizable on a real vector space with a $G$-invariant symmetric form; $w\_2(\rho)\in H^2(G,\mathbb{Z}/2)$ is the second Stiefel to Whitney class (the obstruction to lifting $\rho\:G\to O(V)$ to $\mathrm{Pin}(V)$), and for a place $v$ let $w\_2(\rho\_v)\in H^2(\mathbb{Q}\_v,\mathbb{Z}/2)=\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{Q}*v)[2]\cong{0,\tfrac12}$ be its inflation through $D\_v$; write $\zeta*{\rho,v}:=\exp(2\pi i,\mathrm{inv}\_v(w\_2(\rho\_v)))\in{\pm1}$.

**Theorem 3.1 (Deligne).** For every place $v$, $\varepsilon\_v(\rho-\dim\rho\cdot\mathbf{1},\psi\_v)=\varepsilon\_v(\det\rho,\psi\_v)\cdot\zeta\_{\rho,v}$; with $\varepsilon\_v(\mathbf{1},\psi\_v)=1$ this is
$$\varepsilon\_v(\chi)=\varepsilon\_v(\lambda\_\chi)\cdot\zeta\_{\rho,v}.$$

**Corollary 3.2 (Fröhlich to Queyrut, Deligne).** $\prod\_v\zeta\_{\rho,v}=1$, hence $W(\chi)=W(\lambda\_\chi)=1$ for every orthogonal $\chi$.

*Proof.* $w\_2(\rho)$ inflated to $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ is a global class in $\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{Q})[2]$, whose local invariants sum to $0$ (Albert to Brauer to Hasse to Noether); $\lambda\_\chi$ is real-valued of degree one, so Proposition 2.1 applies. $\square$

**Computing $\zeta\_{\rho,v}$.** The reduction to involutions rests on two facts: restriction to a Sylow $2$-subgroup is injective on $H^2(\cdot,\mathbb{Z}/2)$ (corestriction$\circ$restriction is multiplication by an odd index), and for a cyclic $2$-group $S$, $H^2(S,\mathbb{Z}/2)=\mathbb{Z}/2$ is detected by restriction to the involution $g\in S$ (the nonsplit extension $\mathbb{Z}/2\to\mathbb{Z}/2|S|\to S$ pulls back to the nonsplit extension of $\langle g\rangle$). For an involution $g$ with $b(g)=\frac{\chi(1)-\chi(g)}{2}$ eigenvalues $-1$, $\rho|*{\langle g\rangle}=a,\mathbf{1}+b(g),\mathrm{sgn}$ and the total Stiefel to Whitney class is $(1+x)^{b(g)}$, so $w\_2(\rho|*{\langle g\rangle})=\binom{b(g)}{2}x^2$: the class is determined by the value $\chi(g)$.

**Proposition 3.3.**

1. $v=\infty$: $\zeta\_{\rho,\infty}=(-1)^{\binom{b\_\chi}{2}}$, and indeed $\varepsilon\_\infty(\chi)/\varepsilon\_\infty(\lambda\_\chi)=i^{-b\_\chi+(b\_\chi\bmod2)}=(-1)^{\binom{b\_\chi}{2}}$, a consistency check on [the archimedean place](https://claude.ai/chat/archimedean.md).
2. $v=\ell$ unramified: $\zeta\_{\rho,\ell}=1$ (the inflation factors through $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}*\ell^{\mathrm{ur}}/\mathbb{Q}*\ell)\cong\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, of cohomological dimension $1$), consistent with $\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)=\varepsilon\_\ell(\lambda\_\chi)=1$.
3. $v=\ell$ ramified with $|D\_\ell|$ odd: $\zeta\_{\rho,\ell}=1$ ($H^2(D\_\ell,\mathbb{Z}/2)=0$).
4. $v=\ell$ ramified with cyclic Sylow $2$-subgroup $S\le D\_\ell$ of order $2^k\ge2$, involution $g\in S$, $K\_S=L^S$ (of odd degree over $\mathbb{Q}\_\ell$):

$$\zeta\_{\rho,\ell}=(-1)^{\binom{b(g)}{2}\cdot\epsilon\_S},\qquad\epsilon\_S=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\mathrm{rec}\_{K\_S}(-1)\text{ acts trivially on }L,\1&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}$$

i.e. $\epsilon\_S=0$ iff $-1\in N\_{L/K\_S}(L^\times)$ iff the cyclic extension $L/K\_S$ embeds in a cyclic extension of degree $2^{k+1}$; for $k=1$, $L=K\_S(\sqrt a)$ and $\epsilon\_S=0$ iff the Hilbert symbol $(a,-1)*{K\_S}=1$. Here $\mathrm{rec}*{K\_S}(-1)$ is computed by the reciprocity machinery of [tame root numbers](https://claude.ai/chat/tame-root-numbers.md) §3 / [wild root numbers](https://claude.ai/chat/wild-root-numbers.md) Prop. 2.3 on the abelian extension $L/K\_S$.
5\. In general ($S$ non-cyclic): $\zeta\_{\rho,\ell}$ is the invariant of the inflation of $w\_2(\rho|*S)\in H^2(S,\mathbb{Z}/2)$ to $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}*\ell/K\_S)$; it is not determined by the values of $\chi$ on involutions alone, and the pipeline uses only Corollary 3.2 (the product over all places is $1$, hence $\zeta\_{\rho,\ell}$ for one such prime is determined by all the others) and the sign constraint $\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)/\varepsilon\_\ell(\lambda\_\chi)\in{\pm1}$.

*Proof.* 1: $D\_\infty=\langle c\rangle$, $b(c)=b\_\chi$; the inflation of the generator $x^2$ of $H^2(\langle c\rangle,\mathbb{Z}/2)$ to $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R})$ is the nontrivial class (the quaternion algebra $(-1,-1)*\mathbb{R}$); the second equality is a direct computation of $i^{-b}/i^{-(b\bmod2)}$. 2, 3: as stated. 4: with $\mathrm{res}^{D*\ell}*S$ injective and $\mathrm{inv}*{K\_S}(\mathrm{res}^{\mathbb{Q}*\ell}*{K\_S}\beta)=[K\_S:\mathbb{Q}*\ell],\mathrm{inv}*\ell(\beta)=\mathrm{inv}*\ell(\beta)$ in $\tfrac12\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}$ (odd degree), $\mathrm{inv}*\ell(w\_2(\rho\_\ell))=\mathrm{inv}\_{K\_S}(\mathrm{infl},w\_2(\rho|*S))$, and $w\_2(\rho|S)=\binom{b(g)}{2}y\_S$ with $y\_S$ the class of $\mathbb{Z}/2\to\mathbb{Z}/2^{k+1}\to\mathbb{Z}/2^k$. The inflation of $y\_S$ along $\psi:\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\ell/K\_S)\to S$ is the connecting class $\delta(\psi)$, the obstruction to lifting $\psi$ to a character of order $2^{k+1}$; by local class field theory $\psi=\psi\_0\circ\mathrm{rec}*{K\_S}$ for a character $\psi\_0$ of $K\_S^\times$ of order $2^k$, and $\psi\_0$ is a square of a character iff it is trivial on the kernel ${\pm1}$ of $x\mapsto x^2$ on $K\_S^\times$, i.e. iff $\psi\_0(-1)=1$. For $k=1$, $\delta(\psi)=\psi\cup\psi=(a)\cup(-1)$. $\square$

**The orthogonal check, as used.** For orthogonal $\chi$: (i) $W(\chi)$ computed from [the archimedean place](https://claude.ai/chat/archimedean.md) to [wild root numbers](https://claude.ai/chat/wild-root-numbers.md) must equal $1$; (ii) at $\infty$ and at every ramified $\ell$ with odd $|D\_\ell|$ or cyclic Sylow $2$-subgroup, the local ratio $\varepsilon\_\ell(\chi)/\varepsilon\_\ell(\lambda\_\chi)$ must equal the $\zeta\_{\rho,\ell}$ of Proposition 3.3; (iii) at the remaining ramified primes the ratio must be $\pm1$ and the product of all ratios must be $1$. Item (ii) tests the wild computation of [wild root numbers](https://claude.ai/chat/wild-root-numbers.md) prime by prime, which (i) alone cannot.

## 4. Symplectic characters

**Proposition 4.1.** If $\chi$ is symplectic ($\nu\_2=-1$), then $\det\rho=\mathbf{1}$, $\chi(1)$ is even, $\varepsilon\_v(\chi)\in{\pm1}$ for every $v$, and $W(\chi)=\prod\_v\varepsilon\_v(\chi)\in{\pm1}$; no general closed formula gives the sign, which can be $-1$ (quaternionic extensions), and the shortcut consists of the reality and sign constraints only.

*Proof.* A symplectic form is alternating and nondegenerate, so $\dim V$ is even and $\det\rho=1$. Locally $\varepsilon\_v(\rho)\varepsilon\_v(\rho^\vee)=\det\rho\_v(-1)=1$ and $\rho\cong\rho^\vee$, so $\varepsilon\_v(\rho)^2=1$. $\square$

**The symplectic check, as used.** Each local $\varepsilon\_v(\chi)$ from [the archimedean place](https://claude.ai/chat/archimedean.md) to [wild root numbers](https://claude.ai/chat/wild-root-numbers.md) must be real, hence $\pm1$: at $\infty$, $\det\rho(c)=(-1)^{b\_\chi}=1$ forces $b\_\chi$ even and $\varepsilon\_\infty(\chi)=i^{-b\_\chi}=(-1)^{b\_\chi/2}$; at finite $v$ Proposition 4.1. The product $W(\chi)$ must be $\pm1$, and its sign is a genuine output, confirmed by the functional equation of [the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) against the two candidates $\pm1$ ([the character table](https://claude.ai/chat/character-table.md) Lemma 5.3), the one case in which the numerical verification carries information not already fixed by a theorem.

## 5. Which shortcut applies to which $\chi$

| class of $\chi$ test from the table shortcut what is compared  |                     |                                                                   |                                                                                                                           |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| $\chi(1)=1$, $\chi^2=1$                                        | linear, real-valued | $W=1$ (Gauss)                                                     | global product, and each $\varepsilon\_\ell$ against $\tau(\psi\_\ell)/\sqrt{\ell^{a}}$                                   |
| $\nu\_2(\chi)=+1$                                              | orthogonal          | $W=1$ (Deligne); local ratios $=\zeta\_{\rho,v}$ where computable | global product; local ratios at $\infty$, unramified, odd $D\_\ell$, cyclic $2$-Sylow; $\pm1$ and product $1$ elsewhere   |
| $\nu\_2(\chi)=-1$                                              | symplectic          | $W=\pm1$, $\varepsilon\_v=\pm1$                                   | reality of every local factor; sign left to [the functional-equation test](https://claude.ai/chat/functional-equation.md) |
| $\nu\_2(\chi)=0$                                               | not self-dual       | none                                                              | $\lvert W\rvert=1$, $W(\bar\chi)=\overline{W(\chi)}$, Galois equivariance                                                 |

A real-valued $\chi$ of degree $\ge2$ is never compared with Proposition 2.1; an orthogonal $\chi$ is never compared with the symplectic sign statement, and conversely; a non-real $\chi$ is compared with nothing beyond Proposition 1.1. The indicator $\nu\_2(\chi)$, computed once from the power maps, routes every character to exactly one row. For the odd two-dimensional characters of family 3, $\chi(c)=0$ ([the archimedean place](https://claude.ai/chat/archimedean.md)) gives $b\_\chi=1$ and $\varepsilon\_\infty=-i$; such $\chi$ is orthogonal iff real-valued with $\nu\_2=+1$ (dihedral with real coefficient field, e.g. $S\_3$-type), in which case $W(\chi)=1$ is the prediction; it is never symplectic (symplectic forces $\det\rho=1$, but $\det\rho(c)=-1$ for odd $\chi$); and when its coefficient field is not real, only Proposition 1.1 applies.
