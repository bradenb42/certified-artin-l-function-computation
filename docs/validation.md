# Cross-checks against independent computations

Each of these compares the output with a quantity computed by a different
route, in code that shares nothing with the stage it tests. All are runnable
from `examples/`.

## Abelian fields against class field theory

`artin/check_abelian.py`. For every odd squarefree conductor c <= 500 and every
subgroup H of (Z/c)^* whose fixed field has conductor exactly c, the field is
defined by the minimal polynomial of a Gauss period, computed exactly in
Z[zeta_c], and run through the pipeline. The comparison is against Dirichlet
characters: the matching of table rows to characters is derived from the
Frobenius classes at every unramified prime up to 120 (its existence and
bijectivity are part of the check), then the conductor, the Euler
factors at ramified primes, the parity and the root number
tau(psi)/(i^kappa sqrt f) are compared.

| degree | fields | agreeing |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 | 181 | 181 |
| 3 | 55 | 55 |
| 4 | 268 | 268 |
| 5 | 26 | 26 |

The family is the odd squarefree conductors: an even conductor needs 4 | c and
is wildly ramified at 2.

## Explicit Galois closures

`artin/check_splitting_field.py`. For a fixed sample of degree at most 5 with |G| <= 120,
the Galois closure is constructed as the field generated by a primitive
element and the ramification index, residue degree, number of primes and
discriminant valuation at each ramified prime are read off it, then compared
with the computed decomposition and inertia groups and the conductor
exponents. All twenty-one comparisons that could be made agree, including
three Galois closures of degree 120. Where no primitive element gives a
maximal order at a prime within the budget and the discriminant valuation is
at most 2, the comparison uses the local structure the factor data already
force, and the record says so; where the valuation is larger the reference is
marked unavailable.

## Weight-one newforms

`artin/check_newforms.py`. For odd two-dimensional characters induced from an
imaginary quadratic field, the associated modular form is the theta series of
a class group character. That series is computed from binary quadratic forms
with Gauss composition, with no input from the pipeline, and compared: every
coefficient up to 300, the level, the nebentypus and the root number.
Seventeen characters with conductor up to 1823 and class numbers up to 45;
5100 coefficients; no mismatch. As an anchor, discriminant -23 reproduces
eta(z) eta(23z).

## The subfield zeta anchor

`artin/falsifier.py`. The residue of the Dedekind zeta function of the field
generated by a root is computed from f alone (residue degrees by factoring f
modulo each prime, the discriminant by constructing maximal orders, the
signature by Sturm sequences) and compared with the product of L-values
obtained from the characters. Agreement to twelve or more digits on every
example; the class number formula is recovered for Q(zeta_5) and Q(sqrt 13).

## Measuring the functional-equation test

The same decision rule is applied to perturbed data: a wrong root number, a conductor off by one prime factor, an Euler factor replaced at a ramified prime, the identity class substituted at a small unramified prime. Each verdict is recorded as rejected or unresolved at the truncation point used. On the examples with small conductor every perturbation is rejected; where the altered prime lies past the truncation point the verdict is recorded as unresolved.
